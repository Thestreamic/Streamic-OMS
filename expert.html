Neil Sadwelkar on AI and the Future of Digital Imaging

From negative cutting to AI-assisted colour grading — a candid conversation with one of India's foremost DI pioneers on what the technology revolution really means for broadcast and cinema post-production.

By The Streamic Editorial Team April 2, 2026 9 min read
Neil B. Sadwelkar
Digital Imaging Technician & Post-Production Pioneer
Founder, Digital DADA · Mumbai, India

Welcome to the latest edition of The Streamic Chronicles. I am thrilled to share this deep-dive Q&A with a true pioneer of the DI workflow, Mr. Neil B. Sadwelkar. From the manual drudgery of negative cutting to the high-speed world of AI-assisted color grading, Neil's career is a roadmap of our industry's evolution. I invited him to share his expert insights on the 'Data Tsunami' facing modern productions and his advice for veterans navigating the rapid integration of AI. It is a privilege to host such a wealth of knowledge here on my platform.

01. Craft vs. Automation

Having seen the transition from physical film to DI, which parts of the process are "pure human craft" and which are "manual drudgery" you're happy to automate?

The process of editing, which is the selection, and sequencing of shots, is the human effort part. In film, this was very mechanical, and one had to physically cut and splice the film. That manual drudgery was eliminated when we moved over to editing on NLEs. NLE editing began in the nineties in India. But we continued shooting on film and cutting negative for the next decade. DI began in about 2003 or so after which we stopped cutting negative (very manual process) and began selectively scanning negative. By 2010 we began shooting digital and film shooting was discontinued by 2014 or so.

02. The "Story" Grade

AI can now "match" a grade in seconds. Does this devalue the Colorist, or does it free them to focus more on the emotional narrative?

Grade match by AI is getting better all the time and for prosumer uses like wedding/event videography, documentaries, Vlogs, reels, shorts, AI based colour grading has superseded manual grading in that area almost completely. But for cinema and OTT release work we still do manual grading. But I'm not sure if and when AI will completely replace colourists. Maybe not very soon.

03. The Data Tsunami

With AI now able to auto-tag and organize footage, how do you see the role of the Digital Imaging Technician (DIT) evolving?

Yes, this is an area where AI will replace a large amount of manual work. Just last month, Netflix acquired Ben Affleck's company Interpositive, which has a product that does something like this.

04. Workforce Bottlenecks

Where is the biggest bottleneck in a high-end Indian film workflow today? Can AI solve it?

For professional cinema release and OTT release productions, the manual viewing, tagging, syncing and assembly of clips — like over 100 hours of shot material for a 150 min film — is something that takes months. If this was parsed and curated by AI, then the editor can begin work quicker. The editor can interact with an AI bot which can answer questions on the material available.

05. Generative Post

What are your thoughts on "Vubbing" (AI lip-syncing) and its impact on the original performance of an actor?

This is in its infancy and the current state of the process is not very good looking. But if it improves and preserves the mannerisms of the actor, then it will be a winner.

06. Advice for Veterans

What is your advice to "old school" professionals who feel threatened by the speed of AI integration?

Keep calm and watch what's going on. Try to find a niche within which you can fit. A rock or branch you can hold on to, to avoid being swept by the river. Otherwise, take up gardening, or cooking.
