Are Broadcasters About to be Handed a Much-Needed Investment to Upgrade Their Distribution Infrastructure?

Are Broadcasters About to be Handed a Much-Needed Investment to Upgrade Their Distribution Infrastructure?

The Streamic Editorial Team March 23, 2026 546 words · 3 min read

Technical Analysis: BBC R&D Announces Breakthrough in Low-Latency IP Transmission Using JPEG XS

The BBC's Research and Development department has announced a significant breakthrough in low-latency IP transmission using the JPEG XS compression standard. This development has the potential to revolutionize the way live signals are transported over IP networks, offering a more efficient and cost-effective alternative to traditional methods. The technology is based on the SMPTE ST 2110 standard for professional media over managed IP networks.

Operational ROI

The implementation of JPEG XS compression in live signal transport can significantly reduce transmission costs for broadcasters. By achieving latency as low as 1-2 ms, JPEG XS enables real-time transmission of high-quality video signals over IP networks, making it an attractive alternative to satellite or fiber-based transmission methods. This technology can simplify signal routing and reduce the hardware rack footprint required for signal processing, resulting in lower equipment and maintenance costs. Specifically, this affects the signal contribution and distribution workflow step, where broadcasters can now transport high-quality live signals over IP networks with minimal latency.

In comparison to existing approaches, the use of JPEG XS compression offers a more efficient and scalable solution for live signal transport. Traditional methods often rely on uncompressed video signals, which require significant bandwidth and processing power. The adoption of JPEG XS compression can reduce the bandwidth requirements for live signal transport, resulting in lower transmission costs and improved signal quality. Additionally, the use of JPEG XS compression can simplify the signal routing process, reducing the complexity and cost associated with signal distribution.

Security & Integration

The integration of JPEG XS compression into existing broadcast IT stacks requires careful consideration of security and compliance requirements. The SMPTE ST 2110 standard provides a framework for professional media over managed IP networks, ensuring that JPEG XS compression is implemented in a secure and compliant manner. The use of AES67 audio over IP and NMOS IS-04 device registration and connection management protocols can also ensure seamless integration with existing broadcast systems. Furthermore, the implementation of RIST (Reliable Internet Stream Transport) protocol can provide an additional layer of security and reliability for live signal transport over IP networks.

Engineering Takeaways

  • Deployment: The deployment of JPEG XS compression requires a minimum network bandwidth of 1 GbE and a compatible hardware platform, such as an FPGA or ASIC, to ensure low-latency processing and transmission.
  • Compatibility: The JPEG XS compression standard is compliant with SMPTE ST 2110-22, ensuring seamless integration with existing broadcast systems and devices that support the ST 2110 standard.
  • What to Watch: The upcoming update to the SMPTE ST 2110 standard, which is expected to include new features and enhancements for JPEG XS compression, is an important development that engineers should track, as it may impact future deployment and integration plans.

The adoption of JPEG XS compression for live signal transport has significant implications for the broadcast industry, enabling more efficient and cost-effective transmission of high-quality video signals over IP networks. As a result, engineering teams should begin evaluating and planning for the integration of JPEG XS compression into their existing broadcast systems and workflows to take advantage of its benefits.

Analysis: ST 2110 adoption may accelerate with potential infrastructure upgrades, enabling broadcasters to leverage IP-based workflows with reduced latency and increased bitrate efficiency.
