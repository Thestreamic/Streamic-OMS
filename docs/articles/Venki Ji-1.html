✦ Part 1: The Veteran’s Lens

Venkatakrishna A on the Philosophy of Invisible Restoration

Authenticity over perfection: Why the goal of film and video restoration should be preservation rather than modernization.

By The Streamic Editorial TeamMay 9, 20267 min read
Venkatakrishna A
Domain Expert & Senior Colorist
Real Image Media Solutions (Qube Cinema) · Chennai, India

I am honored to present this two-part Q&A with Mr. Venkatakrishna A (Venki). My own career path was shaped by a twenty-year journey with Qube Cinema Technologies (QCT) / Real Image, and it is a privilege to showcase the expertise of a professional who has been at the heart of India’s restoration evolution. In Part 1, we discuss the core philosophy of preservation and the essential tools that bridge the gap between legacy film and the digital era.

01. The Preservation Mindset

How would you describe your overall approach to film and video restoration?

The goal of film and video restoration should be preservation rather than modernization.
The objective is to recover what was originally available and intended for viewing, while respecting the culture of the era and the original presentation intent.
Always Prioritize:

  • Authenticity over perfection
  • Minimal intervention to ensure maximum impact
  • Consistency across the entire timeline
02. The Media Spectrum

What types of material do you most often work with, and what common issues do you encounter?

I typically work with:

  • Film scans (16mm, 35mm)
  • Archival broadcast content
  • Early digital formats preserved in Tapes

For film content, negative scans are best suited for restoration as they contain significantly higher detail, dynamic range, and colour information than theatrical prints.
Negatives offer superior resolution when scanned and allow for precise digital editing of exposure, rescuing details in both shadows and highlights.
For broadcast and early digital formats, it is best to acquire the uncompressed source content if available, or capture directly from the master tapes rather than working on mezzanine or compressed data formats.

Common issues include:

  • Dust, dirt, scratches, and chemical stains
  • Flicker and exposure instability
  • Grain misinterpretation as noise
  • Colour fading or shift
  • Warping, gate weave, and shrinkage
03. The Toolkit

How do you decide which tools or platforms to use for different restoration tasks?

Tool selection depends on the nature of the problem:

  • Filmworkz’s Phoenix for high end automated restoration + manual clean-up
  • Diamant for dust busting & Stain removal
  • Filmworkz’s Nucoda & DaVinci Resolve – colour restoration and finishing

Ensure the appropriate tools are used for each type of artefact, with a clear distinction between film-based artefacts and those originating from video or electronic sources.

04. The Need for Human Touch

In an era of AI driven clean-up tools, why is manual intervention still essential for professional grade restoration?

AI is excellent for speed and bulk clean-up, but it lacks:

  • Contextual understanding
  • Artistic judgment
  • Sensitivity to texture and motion

Manual intervention ensures:

  • No loss of detail or authenticity
  • Avoidance of artefacts or “plastic” look
  • Preservation of subtle nuances
05. Critical Judgments

What kinds of defects or problems still require frame by frame human judgment rather than automated processing?

  • Complex scratches overlapping moving subjects
  • Faces and skin textures affected by clean-up
  • Motion-related artefacts (ghosting, smearing)
  • Grain vs noise decisions based on scene-specific exposure inconsistencies
  • End of Frame or scene Splicing
06. The Balanced Workflow

Can you explain the balance between automated passes and manual corrections in a typical restoration workflow?

There is no rule of thumb for the balance between both types of passes.
We must ensure the authenticity of the source content during the automation process.
In essence: automation handles volume, while manual work ensures quality

A typical workflow:

  • Automated passes for bulk issues (dust, flicker, stabilization)
  • Evaluation pass to identify failures/artefacts
  • Manual clean-up for critical frames and sensitive areas
  • Final colour management and final QC for consistency
07. AI Realities

What are the biggest misconceptions people have about AI based restoration tools?

Common myths:

  • “AI can fully restore anything automatically”
  • “Cleaner always means better”
  • “Grain is just noise to remove”

In reality:

  • AI can introduce artefacts
  • Over-processing can destroy original detail

Restoration is still a creative and technical craft, where Ai based automation should be overseen by QC.
