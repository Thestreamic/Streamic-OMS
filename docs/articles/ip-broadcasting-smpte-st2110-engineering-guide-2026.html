IP-Based Broadcasting and SMPTE ST 2110: Complete Engineering Guide for 2026

SMPTE ST 2110 defines how uncompressed video, audio, and metadata travel over IP in broadcast facilities. A thorough engineering guide covering PTP timing, gateway selection, NMOS control layer, and the skills gap facing broadcast teams.

The Streamic Editorial Team March 25, 2026 1,059 words · 5 min read Analysis
Source Attribution This analysis is based on publicly available reporting from: thestreamic.in (March 2026).
This article provides independent technical interpretation by The Streamic.

Engineering Brief

  • ST 2110 defines uncompressed media transport over IP — video (ST 2110-20), audio (ST 2110-30), ancillary data (ST 2110-40)
  • PTP (IEEE 1588) timing is the non-negotiable foundation — PTP failure produces immediate loss of sync, not gradual degradation
  • NMOS IS-04 (discovery) and IS-05 (connection management) are the control plane that makes ST 2110 networks manageable at scale
  • JT-NM Tested programme is the practical benchmark for vendor interoperability — prioritise certified products

The transition from SDI to IP-based broadcast infrastructure is the largest architectural change the industry has undergone since the move from analogue to digital. Unlike previous technology transitions, the SDI-to-IP shift does not simply replace one signal format with another — it replaces a deterministic, point-to-point physical layer with a shared network infrastructure that requires broadcast engineers to understand and operate technologies that were previously the domain of IT.

Technical Deep Dive

SMPTE ST 2110 is a suite of standards, not a single document. The core components for broadcast facility design:

  • ST 2110-20: Uncompressed active video packetised over UDP/IP. A single 1080i 59.94 stream carries approximately 1.485 Gb/s. A production environment with 64 simultaneous streams requires over 95 Gb/s of aggregate switching capacity.
  • ST 2110-30: Linear PCM broadcast audio at sample rates up to 96kHz.
  • ST 2110-40: Ancillary data: closed captions, timecode, AFD.
  • ST 2110-21: Traffic shaping models — Wide, Narrow, Narrow Linear — that control packet spacing to prevent network congestion. Verify compatibility between your specific sender and receiver before deployment.

PTP (IEEE 1588) is the timing backbone. Every device — cameras, routers, production switchers, playout servers — must synchronise to a common reference clock at sub-microsecond accuracy. PTP failure is catastrophic: not subtle degradation, but immediate video frame tears across every device that loses PTP lock. The grandmaster clock (GPS-disciplined OCXO or Rubidium oscillator) must be redundant with tested automatic failover. Broadcast-configured switches from Arista 7050X Cisco Nexus Mellanox with IEEE 1588 boundary clock support are the standard specification.

NMOS IS-04 and IS-05 provide the control and discovery layer. Without NMOS, routing an ST 2110 signal between two devices requires manual multicast address configuration on both endpoints. With NMOS, a broadcast control system discovers all devices and manages routing programmatically — equivalent to a traditional SDI matrix but software-defined.

Why This Matters in 2026

Every major broadcast equipment vendor has made the commitment explicit: SDI-only product lines will not receive new feature development. Grass Valley AMPP, Nevion VideoIPath, Lawo .edge, and Ross Video Ultrix are all ST 2110-native platforms. EBU R 148 has been adopted as a design standard by major public service broadcasters across Europe. Any broadcast facility design project in 2026 that does not account for ST 2110 as the target architecture is planning for premature obsolescence.

Limitations & Engineering Risks

  • PTP grandmaster redundancy is systematically under-specified: Most deployment guides cover configuring a single PTP grandmaster. The failover timing — how long devices take to re-acquire PTP lock from the backup — determines how long a PTP failure event affects output. Test failover timing explicitly, with all production devices active, before going live.
  • Gateway device selection determines migration path: Full SDI rip-and-replace is rarely correct. Gateway devices — Matrox ConvertIP (up to 12G SDI), AJA IPT/IPR, Riedel MediorNet, Grass Valley Kula — allow phased migration. Selection must match your specific SDI format and PTP implementation compatibility with your chosen switches.
  • NMOS implementation quality varies: NMOS is a standard, but implementation quality across vendors varies significantly. Prioritise products with current JT-NM Tested certificates when specifying NMOS-dependent control systems.
  • Skills gap: Broadcast engineers who have worked with SDI need significant upskilling for IP infrastructure — multicast configuration, VLAN design, IGMP snooping, PTP troubleshooting. Budget for formal training. A misconfigured switch can bring down an entire ST 2110 facility.

Implementation Checklist

  1. Commission PTP infrastructure first. Use a PTP analyser (MEINBERG, Pendulum) to measure grandmaster accuracy and path delay variation across all network paths before connecting any video equipment.
  2. Configure multicast and IGMP snooping using the switch manufacturer's broadcast-specific configuration guide — not default enterprise configuration. Arista, Cisco, and Mellanox all publish broadcast-specific guides.
  3. Verify ST 2110-21 traffic shaping compatibility between your specific sender and receiver hardware before installation. Request test units and validate the combination before specifying.
  4. Deploy NMOS IS-04 and IS-05 from day one, even on small installations. Retrofitting NMOS into an existing ST 2110 installation is significantly harder than building it in from the start.
  5. Establish a network monitoring baseline using an ST 2110 analyser (Phabrix Qx, Leader LV5600) within the first week. Capture baseline multicast traffic levels, PTP metrics, and packet timing data.
