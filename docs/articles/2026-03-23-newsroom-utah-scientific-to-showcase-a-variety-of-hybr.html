Technical Analysis: Utah Scientific's Hybrid SDI/IP Solutions to Reduce Latency and Increase Signal Integrity

Utah Scientific has announced its plans to showcase a range of hybrid SDI/IP innovations at the 2026 NAB Show, focusing on real-time transport, latency, and signal integrity. The company's solutions will be based on the SMPTE ST 2110 standard, which defines the carriage of uncompressed video and audio over IP networks. By leveraging this standard, Utah Scientific aims to provide broadcast engineers with more efficient and reliable signal transport options.

Operational ROI

The introduction of Utah Scientific's hybrid SDI/IP solutions is expected to save broadcast CTOs significant amounts of money by reducing transmission costs and lowering latency compared to traditional satellite or fibre-based systems. For example, by using the ST 2110 standard, broadcasters can achieve latency as low as 1-2 milliseconds, which is significantly lower than the 100-200 milliseconds typically seen in satellite-based systems. This reduction in latency can be particularly beneficial in live production environments, where timely and accurate signal transport is critical. In terms of workflow, this technology affects the signal routing step, allowing for more streamlined and efficient signal distribution.

In comparison to existing approaches, Utah Scientific's hybrid SDI/IP solutions offer a more simplified signal routing process, reducing the need for complex and costly infrastructure. This is achieved through the use of IP-based networks, which can be more easily managed and scaled than traditional SDI-based systems. Additionally, the use of standards such as ST 2110 and NMOS (Networked Media Open Specification) enables greater interoperability between different devices and systems, further simplifying the signal routing process. By reducing the complexity and cost of signal routing, broadcasters can achieve significant operational savings and improve their overall return on investment.

Security & Integration

Utah Scientific's hybrid SDI/IP solutions are designed to integrate seamlessly into existing broadcast IT stacks, with a focus on security and compliance with relevant standards. The company's use of the ST 2110 standard, for example, ensures that its solutions are compatible with other ST 2110-compliant devices and systems, providing a high level of interoperability and security. Additionally, Utah Scientific's solutions are designed to be compliant with the NMOS IS-04 standard, which defines the protocol for device discovery and registration in IP-based media networks. This ensures that the company's solutions can be easily integrated into existing IP-based networks, while also providing a high level of security and reliability. In terms of cybersecurity, Utah Scientific's solutions are designed to meet the requirements of the AES67 standard, which defines the requirements for audio over IP networks.

This may involve the use of 12G-SDI or 25GbE networks, depending on the specific requirements of the application. Additionally, engineers will need to ensure that their systems are compatible with the ST 2110 and NMOS standards, which may require the use of specialized hardware or software.
  • Compatibility: Utah Scientific's hybrid SDI/IP solutions are designed to be compatible with the SMPTE ST 2110-20 standard, which defines the carriage of uncompressed video over IP networks. The company's solutions are also compatible with the NMOS IS-04 standard, which defines the protocol for device discovery and registration in IP-based media networks.
