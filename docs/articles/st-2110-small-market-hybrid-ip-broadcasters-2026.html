The ST 2110 Transition: Why Small-Market Broadcasters Are Choosing Hybrid-IP Over Full Migration

The Streamic Editorial Team March 19, 2026 541 words · 3 min read Analysis

What Was Announced

Full IP migration, the holy grail of broadcast infrastructure modernisation, is proving to be a costly and complex endeavour for small-market broadcasters. As the industry converges on SMPTE ST 2110 as the future of broadcast infrastructure, a hybrid gateway architecture has emerged as a practical alternative for organisations with limited budgets and ageing but functional SDI infrastructure.

The Engineering and Operational Significance

The technical case for full IP migration is compelling: uncompressed video over IP, flexible routing, software-defined production, and the ability to run broadcast workflows in the cloud. However, the economics tell a different story for small-market broadcasters, who must consider the cost of replacing or modifying a substantial portion of their signal chain, including cameras, routing switchers, production switchers, multiviewers, monitoring systems, and ingest and playback infrastructure. The hybrid gateway approach offers a more feasible solution, allowing broadcasters to introduce IP connectivity incrementally and selectively, starting with the interconnect between facilities and expanding their IP infrastructure as needed. This approach enables organisations to develop IP engineering competency, deliver measurable operational benefits, and transition to IP-based infrastructure at a pace and scale that their budgets can sustain. The hybrid gateway architecture utilises gateway devices that convert between SDI and IP signals, allowing legacy SDI equipment to participate in an IP workflow without being replaced. This enables broadcasters to introduce IP connectivity incrementally, starting with the interconnect between facilities, and expand their IP infrastructure selectively, replacing SDI equipment with IP-native alternatives as it reaches end of life. The gateway layer handles the conversion at each interface point, adding capability without requiring the existing gear to be replaced. This approach also allows broadcasters to develop IP engineering competency, deliver measurable operational benefits, and transition to IP-based infrastructure at a pace and scale that their budgets can sustain.

Industry Context

The industry is converging on SMPTE ST 2110 as the future of broadcast infrastructure, with major network affiliates, national broadcasters, and large production facilities already embarking on full IP migration programmes. However, for small-market broadcasters, the economics of full migration are challenging, and the complexity of the ST 2110 standard itself is a significant barrier. The hybrid gateway approach is not a new concept, but it has gained traction in recent years as a practical solution for organisations with limited budgets and ageing infrastructure. Comparable approaches, such as cloud-based production infrastructure, are also emerging as an alternative to both full IP migration and hybrid gateway architectures.

Implications for Broadcast and Media Teams

For broadcast and media teams, the hybrid gateway approach represents a pragmatic middle path that allows organisations to begin the transition to IP-based infrastructure, develop IP engineering competency, and deliver measurable operational benefits without betting the organisation on a high-risk, high-cost full migration programme. Engineers and operations managers should take note of the following: the hybrid gateway approach requires careful system design to manage added latency and complexity; the value of working with vendors and system integrators who have ST 2110 interoperability experience is high; and the risk of relying on vendor claims of compliance without independent verification is real. Moreover, the economics of cloud-based production infrastructure are still challenging, particularly for markets where bandwidth costs are significant, but it is worth evaluating seriously alongside hybrid-IP on-premises approaches.
