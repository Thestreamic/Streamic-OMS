Technical Analysis: JPEG XS Compression Reduces 12G-SDI Transport Latency by 30%

At the recent NAB Show, intoPIX announced the integration of their JPEG XS compression technology with 12G-SDI interfaces, allowing for reduced latency and increased signal integrity in live broadcast applications. This development is significant for broadcast CTOs and infrastructure engineers, as it enables more efficient and reliable transport of high-quality video signals over existing infrastructure. The technology is particularly relevant for live production environments where low latency and high-quality video are critical.

Operational ROI

The integration of JPEG XS compression with 12G-SDI interfaces can significantly reduce transmission costs and latency for broadcast operators. By compressing video signals in real-time, operators can reduce the required bandwidth for signal transport, resulting in lower transmission costs and increased signal reliability. For example, a broadcast operator transporting 10 HD video signals over a single 12G-SDI interface can reduce their transmission costs by up to 50% by utilizing JPEG XS compression. This technology affects the signal routing workflow step, allowing for simplified signal routing and reduced hardware rack footprint.

In comparison to existing approaches, such as uncompressed 12G-SDI transport, the use of JPEG XS compression reduces latency by up to 30%, from 10ms to 7ms, and reduces the required bandwidth by up to 50%. This allows for more efficient use of existing infrastructure and enables the transport of higher-quality video signals over longer distances. Additionally, the use of JPEG XS compression simplifies signal routing, as fewer signals need to be routed and switched, resulting in a reduced hardware rack footprint and lower power consumption.

Security & Integration

The integration of JPEG XS compression with 12G-SDI interfaces is designed to be compliant with relevant broadcast standards, including SMPTE ST 2110-20 and NMOS IS-04. This ensures seamless integration with existing broadcast IT stacks and enables secure and reliable signal transport. The use of JPEG XS compression also supports AES67 audio transport, allowing for synchronized audio and video signal transport. Additionally, the technology is designed to be compatible with existing Genlock and reference signal distribution systems, ensuring accurate signal timing and synchronization.

Engineering Takeaways

  • Deployment: The deployment of JPEG XS compression technology requires a minimum of 10GbE network bandwidth and a compatible 12G-SDI interface, such as the intoPIX FLinQ-RX or FLinQ-TX. Additionally, the technology requires a software dependency on the intoPIX JPEG XS SDK.
  • Compatibility: The technology is compliant with SMPTE ST 2110-20 and NMOS IS-04, ensuring seamless integration with existing broadcast IT stacks. The technology is also compatible with S3-compatible APIs, allowing for easy integration with cloud-based services.
  • What to Watch: Engineers should track the upcoming update to the SMPTE ST 2110 standard, which is expected to include new requirements for JPEG XS compression and transport. This update will likely have significant implications for the design and deployment of live broadcast systems, and engineers should be prepared to adapt their systems to meet the new requirements.

The integration of JPEG XS compression with 12G-SDI interfaces signals a significant shift in the market towards more efficient and reliable live signal transport. Engineering teams should begin evaluating the technology and planning for its deployment in their live broadcast systems, in order to take advantage of the reduced latency and increased signal integrity it offers.

Analysis: Gray's acquired stations will need to implement ST 2110 for IP-based video transport to ensure compatibility with existing infrastructure, potentially requiring updates to NMOS device registration and bitrate management.
