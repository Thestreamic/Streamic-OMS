Sinclair and ONE Media Technologies Outline NextGen TV, Tech Plans for NAB Show

Sinclair and ONE Media Technologies Outline NextGen TV, Tech Plans for NAB Show

The Streamic Editorial Team April 13, 2026 577 words · 3 min read Analysis
Source Attribution This analysis is based on publicly available reporting from: TV Technology (April 2026).
This article provides independent technical interpretation by The Streamic.

The Quick Hit

Sinclair and ONE Media Technologies are teasing new NextGen TV tech at NAB 2026 that could change how broadcast signals move behind the scenes—think faster IP routing, but engineers will want to test if it actually cuts costs or just adds complexity.

What Happened

  • Sinclair Broadcast Group and its subsidiary ONE Media Technologies have previewed plans for NextGen TV and related broadcast infrastructure upgrades ahead of the NAB Show 2026 [Source 1].
  • The focus includes signal routing and transport—specifically the ST 2110 (think of it like a high-speed digital highway for video, audio, and metadata, replacing older SDI cables) or SDI fabric connecting cameras, production switchers, graphics engines, and distribution paths [Source 1].
  • The announcement highlights practical engineering concerns like timing, redundancy, and IP routing behavior, but stops short of detailing specific products or measurable improvements [Source 1].

The Tech Spec

The infrastructure changes revolve around two core technologies:

  • ST 2110 (as mentioned earlier, a digital standard for moving uncompressed video, audio, and metadata over IP networks instead of traditional SDI cables), which the source positions as a potential replacement for legacy SDI setups [Source 1].
  • SDI fabric (think of it like a physical patch panel for video signals, but using coaxial cables and dedicated hardware), which remains an alternative for broadcasters not yet fully migrated to IP [Source 1].

The source emphasizes that these systems handle critical workflows—linking cameras, production switchers, graphics engines, and distribution paths—but doesn’t specify whether Sinclair or ONE Media will introduce new hardware, software, or integrations at NAB [Source 1]. Key engineering considerations like timing (synchronizing video and audio so they don’t drift apart), redundancy (backup paths to keep signals alive if one fails), and IP routing behavior are flagged as areas where real-world performance will matter [Source 1].

So What? (The Human Impact)

For broadcast engineers and operations teams, this announcement is a heads-up to evaluate whether Sinclair’s NextGen TV infrastructure plans align with their own IP migration roadmaps. If the company debuts ST 2110-compatible tools or workflows at NAB, it could simplify transitions from SDI to IP—assuming the solutions deliver on reliability and cost efficiency [Source 1]. The source explicitly warns against treating vendor claims as "production-ready" without testing against real workflows, a reminder that lab demos don’t always translate to seamless live operations [Source 1].

For CTOs, the bigger question is whether these upgrades measurably improve throughput, reduce latency, or cut costs—or if they’re incremental steps in a longer industry shift. The lack of specifics on pricing, compatibility, or performance benchmarks means teams will need to dig deeper at NAB before committing to changes [Source 1].

Blind Spots

  • The source doesn’t specify which specific products, software, or hardware Sinclair or ONE Media will showcase at NAB 2026 [Source 1].
  • The source doesn’t clarify whether the ST 2110 or SDI solutions are new developments from Sinclair/ONE Media or integrations of existing third-party tech [Source 1].
  • The source doesn’t provide measurable targets (e.g., latency reductions, cost savings, or throughput improvements) for the announced infrastructure changes [Source 1].
  • The source doesn’t state whether these upgrades are backward-compatible with existing Sinclair or industry-standard broadcast systems [Source 1].
  • The source doesn’t mention cloud integration—whether these NextGen TV plans include hybrid or full-cloud workflows, or remain on-premises [Source 1].
