New Codec Data: 40% of Respondents Plan to Deploy AV1 in 2026, H.264/AVC Has 84% Adoption

Source Attribution This analysis is based on publicly available reporting from: Streaming Media Blog (March 2026).
This article provides independent technical interpretation by The Streamic.

What Was Announced

A recent survey conducted by Netint, a provider of encoding and transcoding solutions, has revealed that 40% of respondents plan to deploy the AV1 codec in 2026, while 84% of respondents currently use H.264/AVC. The survey, which polled encoding and transcoding professionals, highlights the growing adoption of AV1, a royalty-free, open-source codec developed by the Alliance for Open Media (AOM). The results suggest that the industry is shifting towards a more open and flexible approach to video encoding, with AV1 poised to become a major player in the market.

The Engineering and Operational Significance

The adoption of AV1 has significant implications for broadcast and media teams, particularly in terms of workflow efficiency and cost savings. By deploying AV1, teams can potentially reduce the complexity and cost associated with encoding and transcoding, as well as improve the quality and efficiency of video delivery. AV1's royalty-free nature and open-source architecture also make it an attractive option for teams looking to reduce their reliance on proprietary codecs and vendors. Furthermore, the adoption of AV1 is likely to enable faster publishing and more predictable output under deadline pressure, as teams can take advantage of the codec's improved compression efficiency and scalability. The shift towards AV1 also has implications for the way teams handle deployment, integration, and scaling. As teams begin to adopt AV1, they will need to consider the interoperability and operational maturity of their existing infrastructure, including their content management systems (CMS), media asset management (MAM) systems, and playout infrastructure. This will require careful planning and evaluation of the surrounding vendor support and technical capabilities to ensure seamless integration and efficient workflow execution.

Industry Context

The growing adoption of AV1 is part of a broader trend towards more open and flexible approaches to video encoding and delivery. The industry has been shifting away from proprietary codecs and vendors, with many teams opting for open-source and royalty-free alternatives. This trend is driven by the need for greater efficiency, flexibility, and cost savings, as well as the desire for more control and customization over video delivery. Other open-source codecs, such as VP9 and Daala, have also been gaining traction in recent years, although AV1's improved compression efficiency and scalability make it a particularly attractive option for teams looking to improve the quality and efficiency of video delivery. In addition to AV1, other technologies such as cloud-based encoding and transcoding, as well as software-defined video infrastructure, are also gaining traction in the industry. These technologies enable teams to deploy and manage video encoding and delivery infrastructure more efficiently and cost-effectively, while also providing greater flexibility and scalability. As the industry continues to evolve, teams will need to carefully evaluate the technical and operational implications of these emerging technologies and consider how they can be integrated into their existing workflows.

Implications for Broadcast and Media Teams

For broadcast and media teams, the growing adoption of AV1 and other open-source codecs has significant implications for workflow efficiency, cost savings, and video quality. As teams begin to adopt AV1, they will need to carefully evaluate the technical and operational implications of this shift, including the interoperability and operational maturity of their existing infrastructure. This will require careful planning and evaluation of the surrounding vendor support and technical capabilities to ensure seamless integration and efficient workflow execution. Engineers, operations managers, and platform teams should take note of the operational knock-on effects of AV1 deployment, including the potential for faster publishing, more predictable output under deadline pressure, and reduced complexity and cost associated with encoding and transcoding. They should also consider the broader industry trends and emerging technologies, including cloud-based encoding and transcoding, software-defined video infrastructure, and other open-source codecs, to ensure that their teams are well-positioned to take advantage of the opportunities and challenges presented by these emerging technologies.
