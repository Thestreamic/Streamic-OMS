Why Real-Time Transport Protocol Choices Are Forcing Broadcasters to Rethink Signal Integrity

Live signal transport is under pressure to deliver frame-accurate, low-latency video across increasingly complex networks. As broadcasters navigate the transition from traditional SDI to IP-based infrastructures, the choice of real-time transport protocol has become a critical decision point.

What This Development Actually Means

The recent adoption of SRT (Secure Reliable Transport) by several major broadcast vendors marks a significant shift in the industry's approach to live signal transport. SRT, an open-source protocol, offers a compelling alternative to traditional protocols like ST 2110 and NDI, with its ability to provide low-latency, secure, and reliable transport of high-quality video over unpredictable networks. This development is particularly noteworthy given the growing demand for remote production and contribution workflows, where signal integrity and latency are paramount.

The industry's move towards SRT is, in part, a response to the need for more flexible and scalable signal transport solutions that can accommodate the increasing complexity of modern broadcast workflows. As broadcasters continue to adopt IP-based infrastructures, the need for protocols that can handle the unique challenges of IP transport, such as packet loss and jitter, has become increasingly important.

Why It Matters for Broadcast Operations

The choice of real-time transport protocol has significant implications for broadcast operations, particularly in terms of signal integrity, latency, and overall system reliability. For example, the use of SRT can enable broadcasters to reduce their reliance on expensive and complex SDI infrastructure, while also providing a more flexible and scalable solution for remote production and contribution workflows. Vendors like Matrox, with their ConvertIP gateway, and Riedel, with their MediorNet solution, are already incorporating SRT into their products, providing broadcasters with a range of options for implementing this protocol. Meanwhile, companies like Harmonic, with their VOS360 platform, are leveraging SRT to enable low-latency, cloud-based workflows for live video processing and delivery.

Impact by Stakeholder

  • Regional and national broadcasters: Will need to budget for the deployment of SRT-enabled devices and infrastructure, which can help reduce transmission costs and simplify signal routing. They can also eliminate the need for expensive SDI infrastructure, resulting in a lower hardware rack footprint.
