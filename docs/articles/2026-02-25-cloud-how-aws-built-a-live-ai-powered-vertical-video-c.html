How AWS Built a Live AI-Powered Vertical Video Capability for Fox Sports with AWS Elemental Inference

The Streamic Editorial Team February 25, 2026 557 words · 3 min read Analysis
Source Attribution This analysis is based on publicly available reporting from: AWS (February 2026).
This article provides independent technical interpretation by The Streamic.

What Was Announced

Amazon Web Services (AWS) has collaborated with Fox Sports to develop an AI-powered vertical video capability using AWS Elemental Inference. This new capability enables the real-time transformation of traditional 16:9 video into vertically-oriented content for mobile devices, leveraging the power of cloud-based AI processing. According to AWS, the solution was designed to simplify the process of creating vertical video content, reducing the complexity and manual effort required for content creators.

The Engineering and Operational Significance

The introduction of AI-powered vertical video processing represents a significant shift in the way broadcast and media teams manage content ingest, processing, and delivery. By leveraging AWS Elemental Inference, teams can automate the transformation of traditional video content into vertical formats, eliminating the need for manual intervention and reducing the risk of human error. This capability is particularly relevant in the context of mobile-first delivery, where vertically-oriented video content has become increasingly popular. The ability to process and deliver vertical video content in real-time will enable media teams to streamline their workflows, increase efficiency, and improve the overall viewing experience for their audiences. Furthermore, the use of cloud-based AI processing will provide greater scalability and flexibility, allowing teams to easily adapt to changing viewer habits and preferences. The significance of this announcement extends beyond the specific feature itself, as it reflects a broader trend towards software-defined workflows in the broadcast and media industry. By removing repetitive labor and automating complex tasks, software-defined workflows can help teams simplify operations, preserve resilience, and maintain editorial speed. In this context, the AI-powered vertical video capability represents a key enabler of these workflows, providing a critical building block for the development of more efficient and agile content delivery systems.

Industry Context

The development of AI-powered vertical video processing capabilities is part of a broader conversation around cloud production in the broadcast and media industry. As teams seek to increase flexibility and reduce operational drag, they are turning to cloud-based solutions that can simplify ingest, monitoring, distribution, and post-production throughput. This trend is driven by the need to support multi-platform delivery, including OTT and social media, where vertically-oriented video content has become increasingly popular. In this context, AWS Elemental Inference represents a key player in the cloud production ecosystem, providing a scalable and flexible platform for AI-powered video processing. Comparable approaches and competing technologies include the use of on-premises AI processing solutions, such as those offered by companies like IBM and Microsoft, as well as cloud-based video processing platforms from vendors like Google Cloud and Microsoft Azure.

Implications for Broadcast and Media Teams

The introduction of AI-powered vertical video processing capabilities has significant implications for broadcast and media teams, particularly in terms of workflow optimization and efficiency. As teams seek to simplify operations and preserve resilience, they will need to evaluate the impact of this new capability on their existing workflows and infrastructure. This will involve assessing the potential benefits of automation, scalability, and flexibility, as well as the potential risks and challenges associated with adopting new technologies. In particular, teams will need to consider the operational maturity of their existing systems, including their ability to handle increased complexity and fragility. By carefully evaluating the implications of this new capability, teams can ensure that they are well-positioned to take advantage of the opportunities it presents, while minimizing the risks and challenges associated with adoption.
