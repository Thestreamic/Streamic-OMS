OpenDrives Shows Off Sports Expertise in Sports Business Hub located in NAB Show’s West Hall

OpenDrives Shows Off Sports Expertise in Sports Business Hub located in NAB Show’s West Hall

The Streamic Editorial Team March 30, 2026

Why Live Signal Transport Just Got a Whole Lot More Complicated

Live broadcast operations are facing a perfect storm of increasing complexity, driven by the relentless pursuit of lower latency and higher quality. With the rise of remote production, cloud-connected workflows, and the proliferation of IP-based signal transport, engineers are struggling to keep pace with the demands of real-time signal transport. The stakes are high, with even a fraction of a second of latency difference between competing broadcasts making all the difference in the world.

What This Development Actually Means

OpenDrives has announced a major partnership with a leading sports media company to deploy its high-performance storage solution in a new Sports Business Hub located in the NAB Show's West Hall. This move is significant, as it highlights the growing need for robust, high-capacity storage solutions that can keep pace with the demands of live sports production.

This development is part of a broader trend towards the adoption of cloud-connected workflows in live production. As more and more broadcasters look to take advantage of the flexibility and scalability offered by cloud-based solutions, the need for high-performance storage that can integrate seamlessly with these workflows is becoming increasingly pressing.

Why It Matters for Broadcast Operations

The implications of this development are far-reaching, with significant implications for broadcast engineers and operations teams. With the rise of cloud-connected workflows, the need for high-performance storage solutions that can integrate seamlessly with these workflows is becoming increasingly pressing. This means that engineers will need to carefully evaluate the storage solutions they deploy, taking into account factors such as latency, throughput, and scalability. In particular, vendors like Harmonic, Telestream, and Avid will need to be evaluated for their ability to integrate with cloud-based workflows, as well as their support for emerging standards like ST 2110 and NMOS.

Regional and national broadcasters will need to budget for the increased complexity and cost of cloud-connected workflows, while OTT and streaming platforms will need to adapt their delivery strategies to accommodate the changing demands of live sports production. Post-production and MAM teams will also need to adapt their workflows to accommodate the increased volume and complexity of live sports content.
