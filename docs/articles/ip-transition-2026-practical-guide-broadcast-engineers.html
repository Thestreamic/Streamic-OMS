The IP Transition in 2026: A Practical Engineering Guide for Broadcasters

The Streamic Editorial Team March 22, 2026 909 words · 5 min read Analysis

Introduction to the IP Transition

The broadcast industry is undergoing a significant transformation as it migrates from traditional SDI (Serial Digital Interface) infrastructure to IP (Internet Protocol) infrastructure. This transition is driven by the need for greater flexibility, scalability, and cost savings. By 2030, IP infrastructure is expected to become the norm, with most broadcasters adopting SMPTE ST 2110 as the standard for professional media over managed IP networks. For small and mid-market broadcasters, this transition poses unique challenges, including limited resources, technical expertise, and budgets.

Understanding SMPTE ST 2110

SMPTE ST 2110 is a suite of standards that defines the carriage, synchronization, and description of professional media over managed IP networks. It provides a framework for transporting video, audio, and ancillary data over IP networks, enabling broadcasters to take advantage of the flexibility and scalability of IP infrastructure. SMPTE ST 2110 is composed of several parts, including SMPTE ST 2110-10 for video, SMPTE ST 2110-30 for audio, and SMPTE ST 2110-40 for ancillary data. Understanding these standards is crucial for broadcasters to ensure a successful IP transition.

Technical Challenges and Solutions

One of the primary technical challenges faced by small and mid-market broadcasters is the complexity of IP infrastructure. Unlike traditional SDI infrastructure, which is relatively straightforward to set up and manage, IP infrastructure requires a deep understanding of networking protocols, quality of service (QoS), and network management. To address these challenges, broadcasters can leverage NMOS (Networked Media Open Specifications), an open standard for device discovery and registration. NMOS provides a simple and efficient way to manage IP devices on a network, making it easier to set up and manage IP infrastructure.

Hybrid Approaches

For many small and mid-market broadcasters, a hybrid approach to the IP transition may be the most practical solution. This involves maintaining existing SDI infrastructure while gradually introducing IP components. AES67 is a key standard for audio over IP, providing a way to transport audio signals over IP networks. By using AES67 and other IP standards, broadcasters can create hybrid systems that integrate SDI and IP components, allowing for a gradual transition to IP infrastructure.

Industry Observations

According to

Jan Weigner, CTO at Qvest Media, "The IP transition is not just about replacing SDI with IP, it's about creating a more flexible and scalable infrastructure that can support new business models and services."
This observation highlights the importance of considering the broader implications of the IP transition, beyond just the technical aspects. By embracing IP infrastructure, broadcasters can create new revenue streams, improve operational efficiency, and enhance the overall viewer experience.

Streaming and Distribution

Another critical aspect of the IP transition is streaming and distribution. With the rise of OTT (Over-the-Top) services, broadcasters need to ensure that their content can be delivered efficiently and effectively to a wide range of devices. HLS (HTTP Live Streaming) and HEVC (High Efficiency Video Coding) are widely used for streaming and compression, respectively. However, new standards like AV1 are emerging, offering improved compression efficiency and lower latency. Broadcasters should consider these emerging standards when planning their streaming and distribution infrastructure.

Reliable Transport and Security

When it comes to transporting live video and audio over IP networks, reliability and security are paramount. SRT (Secure Reliable Transport) and RIST (Reliable Internet Stream Transport) are two protocols that provide reliable and secure transport of live video and audio over IP networks. These protocols are particularly important for live events, where packet loss and latency can have significant consequences. By using SRT or RIST, broadcasters can ensure that their live content is delivered reliably and securely, even over unpredictable networks.

NDI and IP Production

NDI (Network Device Interface) is a popular protocol for IP production, providing a simple and efficient way to transport video and audio over IP networks. NDI is widely used in live production environments, where it enables the transport of multiple video and audio streams over a single network. By leveraging NDI and other IP production protocols, broadcasters can create flexible and scalable production systems that can support a wide range of applications and services.

Practical Guidance for Broadcasters

For small and mid-market broadcasters, the IP transition can seem daunting. However, by taking a practical and incremental approach, broadcasters can ensure a successful transition to IP infrastructure. Here are some key steps to consider:

  • Assess existing infrastructure and identify areas for upgrade or replacement
  • Develop a clear roadmap for the IP transition, including timelines and budgets
  • Invest in staff training and education to ensure a smooth transition
  • Consider hybrid approaches to integrate SDI and IP components
  • Monitor industry developments and emerging standards, such as AV1 and SRT

By following these steps and staying informed about the latest technical developments, small and mid-market broadcasters can navigate the IP transition with confidence and create a robust, flexible, and scalable infrastructure that supports their business goals.

Future-Proofing Infrastructure

As the broadcast industry continues to evolve, it's essential for broadcasters to future-proof their infrastructure. This means investing in IP infrastructure that can support emerging standards and technologies, such as 5G and cloud-based production. By taking a forward-looking approach, broadcasters can ensure that their infrastructure remains relevant and effective, even as the industry continues to change. According to industry experts,

"The key to a successful IP transition is to focus on the long-term benefits, rather than just the short-term costs."
By prioritizing flexibility, scalability, and future-proofing, broadcasters can create an IP infrastructure that supports their business goals and drives growth and innovation.
