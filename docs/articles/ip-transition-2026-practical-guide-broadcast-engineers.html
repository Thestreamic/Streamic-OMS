The IP Transition in 2026: A Practical Engineering Guide for Broadcasters

The migration from traditional **SDI (Serial Digital Interface)** to **IP (Internet Protocol)**-based infrastructure is a significant undertaking for broadcasters, particularly for small and mid-market operations. As the industry continues to evolve, the adoption of **SMPTE ST 2110** and **NMOS (Networked Media Open Specifications)** has become increasingly important for facilitating a smooth transition. In this article, we will delve into the practical challenges and solutions for broadcasters navigating this complex process.

Understanding the Benefits of IP Infrastructure

IP-based infrastructure offers several advantages over traditional SDI, including greater flexibility, scalability, and cost-effectiveness. With IP, broadcasters can easily scale their infrastructure to meet growing demands, while also reducing the need for expensive hardware and cabling. Additionally, IP enables the use of **COTS (Commercial Off-The-Shelf)** hardware, which can significantly reduce costs and improve overall system reliability. As

the IP transition is not just about replacing SDI with IP, but about creating a more agile and flexible infrastructure that can adapt to changing broadcast requirements

Challenges in Implementing IP Infrastructure

Despite the benefits of IP infrastructure, small and mid-market broadcasters often face significant challenges when implementing **SMPTE ST 2110** and **NMOS**. One of the primary concerns is the lack of standardization, which can lead to interoperability issues between different vendors and devices. Furthermore, the complexity of IP networks can be overwhelming, particularly for broadcasters with limited IT expertise. To address these challenges, many broadcasters are adopting hybrid approaches that combine the benefits of IP with the familiarity of SDI.

Hybrid Approaches to IP Transition

Hybrid approaches to IP transition involve using a combination of IP and SDI infrastructure to facilitate a gradual migration. This can include using **SDI-to-IP gateways** to convert SDI signals to IP, or **IP-to-SDI gateways** to convert IP signals to SDI. By using hybrid approaches, broadcasters can minimize disruption to their existing operations while still benefiting from the advantages of IP infrastructure. For example, a small broadcaster might use a hybrid approach to transition their master control room to IP, while still maintaining SDI infrastructure for other parts of their operation.

Key Technologies for IP Transition

Several key technologies are essential for a successful IP transition, including **SMPTE ST 2110**, **NMOS**, and **AES67**. **SMPTE ST 2110** is a suite of standards that defines the carriage of video, audio, and metadata over IP networks. **NMOS** is a set of specifications that enable the discovery and registration of devices on an IP network. **AES67** is a standard for audio over IP, which provides a common protocol for audio transmission. Additionally, **HLS (HTTP Live Streaming)** and **HEVC (High Efficiency Video Coding)** are also important technologies for broadcasters, as they enable the efficient distribution of video content over IP networks.

Security Considerations for IP Infrastructure

As broadcasters transition to IP infrastructure, security becomes a major concern. IP networks are inherently more vulnerable to cyber threats than traditional SDI infrastructure, which can put sensitive broadcast content at risk. To mitigate these risks, broadcasters must implement robust security measures, including **firewalls**, **VPNs (Virtual Private Networks)**, and **encryption**. Furthermore, broadcasters should also consider using **SRT (Secure Reliable Transport)** or **RIST (Reliable Internet Stream Transport)**, which are protocols designed to provide secure and reliable transport of video content over IP networks.

Case Study: Implementing IP Infrastructure at a Small Broadcaster

A small broadcaster in the United States recently implemented an IP-based infrastructure using **SMPTE ST 2110** and **NMOS**. The broadcaster used a hybrid approach, combining IP and SDI infrastructure to facilitate a gradual migration. The implementation involved using **SDI-to-IP gateways** to convert SDI signals to IP, and **IP-to-SDI gateways** to convert IP signals to SDI. The broadcaster also implemented **AES67** for audio over IP, and **HLS** and **HEVC** for video distribution. To ensure security, the broadcaster implemented **firewalls** and **VPNs**, and used **SRT** for secure transport of video content.

Best Practices for IP Transition

To ensure a successful IP transition, broadcasters should follow several best practices, including:

  • Conducting a thorough assessment of their existing infrastructure and operations
  • Developing a clear migration plan and timeline
  • Providing training and support for staff
  • Implementing robust security measures
  • Monitoring and testing IP infrastructure regularly

By following these best practices, broadcasters can minimize disruption to their operations and ensure a smooth transition to IP infrastructure.

Future of IP Infrastructure in Broadcasting

As the broadcast industry continues to evolve, IP infrastructure is likely to play an increasingly important role. With the advent of new technologies such as **AV1 (AOMedia Video 1)** and **NDI (Network Device Interface)**, broadcasters will have even more options for distributing and processing video content. Additionally, the use of **COTS** hardware and **cloud-based services** will become more prevalent, enabling broadcasters to reduce costs and improve efficiency. As

Real-World Implementations of IP Infrastructure

Several broadcasters have already implemented IP infrastructure, with impressive results. For example, a mid-market broadcaster in Europe used **SMPTE ST 2110** and **NMOS** to build a fully IP-based master control room, which enabled them to reduce costs and improve efficiency. Another broadcaster in the United States used **AES67** and **HLS** to distribute live video content over IP networks, which enabled them to reach a wider audience and improve the quality of their streams.

Industry Outlook and Future Developments

The outlook for IP infrastructure in the broadcast industry is promising, with many broadcasters already investing in IP-based solutions. As the industry continues to evolve, we can expect to see new and innovative applications of IP infrastructure, such as **virtualized master control rooms** and **cloud-based playout systems**. Additionally, the use of **artificial intelligence** and **machine learning** will become more prevalent, enabling broadcasters to automate and optimize their operations. With the right technologies and strategies in place, broadcasters can ensure a successful IP transition and thrive in a rapidly changing media landscape.
