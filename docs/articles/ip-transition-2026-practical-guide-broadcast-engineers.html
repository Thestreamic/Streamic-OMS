The IP Transition in 2026: A Practical Engineering Guide for Broadcasters

The real-world challenges and solutions for broadcasters migrating from SDI to IP infrastructure using SMPTE ST 2110, NMOS, and hybrid approaches. Focus on small and mid-market broadcasters, not tier-

The Streamic Editorial Team March 22, 2026 Analysis

Introduction to the IP Transition

The broadcast industry is undergoing a significant transformation as it migrates from traditional **SDI (Serial Digital Interface)** to **IP (Internet Protocol)**-based infrastructure. This shift is driven by the need for greater flexibility, scalability, and cost-effectiveness in broadcast operations. By 2026, small and mid-market broadcasters will be at the forefront of this transition, embracing **SMPTE ST 2110** and other related standards to stay competitive. In this article, we will delve into the practical engineering aspects of the IP transition, exploring the challenges and solutions for broadcasters navigating this complex landscape.

Understanding SMPTE ST 2110

SMPTE ST 2110 is a suite of standards that defines the carriage of **video**, **audio**, and **ancillary data** over **IP networks**. It provides a framework for the transport of uncompressed video and audio signals, allowing for greater flexibility and scalability in broadcast operations. The standard is divided into several parts, each addressing a specific aspect of the IP transition, such as **video (ST 2110-20)**, **audio (ST 2110-30)**, and **ancillary data (ST 2110-40)**. By adopting **SMPTE ST 2110**, broadcasters can take advantage of the benefits of IP-based infrastructure, including reduced cabling costs, improved signal routing, and enhanced system flexibility.

Hybrid Approaches to the IP Transition

While **SMPTE ST 2110** provides a comprehensive framework for the IP transition, many broadcasters are adopting hybrid approaches that combine traditional **SDI** infrastructure with **IP**-based systems. This approach allows broadcasters to leverage their existing investments in **SDI** equipment while still benefiting from the flexibility and scalability of **IP**.

The key to a successful hybrid approach is to ensure that the **SDI** and **IP** systems can interoperate seamlessly, using standards like **NMOS (Networked Media Open Specification)** to enable discovery and registration of devices on the network.
By adopting a hybrid approach, broadcasters can minimize disruption to their operations while still achieving the benefits of the IP transition.

Implementing NMOS

NMOS is a critical component of the IP transition, providing a standardized framework for device discovery and registration on **IP networks**. By implementing **NMOS**, broadcasters can enable automatic discovery and configuration of devices on their network, simplifying the process of adding new equipment and reducing the risk of human error. NMOS also provides a standardized API for device control, allowing broadcasters to integrate devices from multiple vendors and create a more cohesive and efficient system. As NMOS continues to evolve, it is likely to play an increasingly important role in the IP transition, enabling broadcasters to build more flexible and scalable systems.

Audio Over IP

In addition to **video**, the IP transition also involves the transport of **audio** signals over **IP networks**. AES67 is a widely adopted standard for **audio over IP**, providing a framework for the transport of uncompressed audio signals over **IP networks**. By adopting AES67, broadcasters can take advantage of the benefits of **IP**-based audio transport, including reduced cabling costs and improved signal routing. AES67 is also compatible with **SMPTE ST 2110**, allowing broadcasters to integrate **audio** and **video** signals on the same **IP network**. This provides a more streamlined and efficient workflow, enabling broadcasters to focus on creating high-quality content rather than managing complex infrastructure.

Streaming and Playout

The IP transition also has significant implications for streaming and playout operations. By adopting **IP**-based infrastructure, broadcasters can take advantage of more efficient and scalable streaming and playout systems. HLS (HTTP Live Streaming) and **HSS (HTTP Stream Streaming)** are widely adopted standards for streaming, providing a framework for the delivery of live and on-demand content over **IP networks**. By using HLS and **HSS**, broadcasters can reach a wider audience and provide a more engaging viewing experience. In terms of playout, IP**-based systems can provide more flexibility and scalability, enabling broadcasters to create more complex and dynamic playlists.

Compression and Encoding

Compression and encoding are critical components of the IP transition, enabling broadcasters to reduce the bandwidth requirements of their **IP**-based systems. HEVC (High Efficiency Video Coding) and AV1 are widely adopted standards for video compression, providing a framework for the efficient encoding of **video** signals. By adopting HEVC and AV1, broadcasters can reduce the bandwidth requirements of their **IP**-based systems, enabling them to transport more signals over their networks. This provides a more efficient and cost-effective workflow, allowing broadcasters to focus on creating high-quality content rather than managing complex infrastructure.

Security and Reliability

Security and reliability are critical considerations in the IP transition, as broadcasters seek to ensure the integrity and availability of their **IP**-based systems. SRT (Secure Reliable Transport) and RIST (Reliable Internet Stream Transport) are widely adopted standards for secure and reliable transport of **video** and **audio** signals over **IP networks**. By adopting SRT and RIST, broadcasters can ensure the secure and reliable transport of their signals, reducing the risk of errors and interruptions. This provides a more robust and resilient workflow, enabling broadcasters to maintain the highest levels of quality and availability.

NDI and the IP Transition

NDI (Network Device Interface) is a widely adopted standard for **IP**-based live production, providing a framework for the transport of **video**, **audio**, and **metadata** over **IP networks**. By adopting NDI, broadcasters can take advantage of the benefits of **IP**-based live production, including reduced cabling costs and improved signal routing. NDI is also compatible with **SMPTE ST 2110**, allowing broadcasters to integrate **NDI** devices with their existing **IP**-based infrastructure. This provides a more streamlined and efficient workflow, enabling broadcasters to focus on creating high-quality content rather than managing complex infrastructure.

Best Practices for the IP Transition

As broadcasters navigate the IP transition, there are several best practices that can help ensure a successful migration. These include:

  • Developing a comprehensive migration plan, including timelines and budgets
  • Assessing existing infrastructure and identifying areas for upgrade or replacement
  • Adopting standardized protocols and interfaces, such as **SMPTE ST 2110** and **NMOS**
  • Providing training and support for staff, to ensure a smooth transition to **IP**-based systems
  • Monitoring and evaluating the performance of **IP**-based systems, to identify areas for improvement
By following these best practices, broadcasters can minimize disruption to their operations and ensure a successful transition to **IP**-based infrastructure.

The Future of the IP Transition

As the IP transition continues to evolve, it is likely that we will see significant advancements in areas such as **artificial intelligence**, **machine learning**, and **cloud-based services**. These technologies will enable broadcasters to create more efficient and scalable systems, with greater flexibility and autonomy.

The IP transition is not just about replacing traditional **SDI** infrastructure with **IP**-based systems - it's about creating a more agile and responsive broadcast ecosystem, capable of adapting to changing viewer habits and technological advancements.
As broadcasters navigate this complex landscape, they must remain focused on the core principles of flexibility, scalability, and cost-effectiveness, while also embracing the latest technologies and innovations.
