The IP Transition in 2026: A Practical Engineering Guide for Broadcasters

The real-world challenges and solutions for broadcasters migrating from SDI to IP infrastructure using SMPTE ST 2110, NMOS, and hybrid approaches. Focus on small and mid-market broadcasters, not tier-

The Streamic Editorial Team March 22, 2026 Analysis

Introduction to IP Transition

The broadcast industry is undergoing a significant transformation as it shifts from traditional SDI (Serial Digital Interface) to IP (Internet Protocol) infrastructure. By 2030, broadcasters are expected to have largely completed this transition, adopting SMPTE ST 2110 and other related standards. For small and mid-market broadcasters, this transition poses unique challenges, from infrastructure costs to staff training. However, with the right approach, these broadcasters can leverage IP to enhance their operations, improve flexibility, and reduce costs.

Understanding SMPTE ST 2110

SMPTE ST 2110 is a suite of standards that defines the carriage, synchronization, and description of separate video, audio, and ancillary data essences over IP networks. This standard is crucial for broadcasters as it enables them to transition their facilities to IP while maintaining the professional-grade quality and reliability they require. SMPTE ST 2110 supports uncompressed video, which is essential for live production, ensuring that broadcasters can maintain the high quality of their content.

Challenges in the IP Transition

For small and mid-market broadcasters, one of the primary challenges in transitioning to IP is the cost of new equipment and infrastructure. Replacing SDI routers, switches, and other hardware with IP-based solutions requires significant investment. Moreover, training staff to work with IP systems and troubleshooting IP networks can be time-consuming and costly. Despite these challenges, the long-term benefits of IP, including greater flexibility and scalability, make the transition worthwhile.

Hybrid Approaches to IP Transition

Given the challenges associated with a full IP transition, many broadcasters are adopting hybrid approaches. These approaches involve integrating IP systems with existing SDI infrastructure, allowing for a gradual transition. Hybrid systems can use SDI to IP gateways to convert SDI signals to IP and vice versa, enabling broadcasters to use their existing SDI equipment while starting to build out their IP capabilities.

Role of NMOS in IP Infrastructure

NMOS (Networked Media Open Specification) plays a critical role in IP infrastructure for broadcasters. NMOS provides a standard for the registration and discovery of IP stream sources and destinations on a network, making it easier for devices to automatically discover and connect with each other. This capability simplifies the setup and management of IP networks, especially in environments where devices from multiple manufacturers are used. By adopting NMOS, broadcasters can create more agile and responsive IP networks.

Audio Over IP with AES67

For audio transmission over IP, AES67 is becoming the standard of choice. AES67 provides a method for transporting audio over IP networks, ensuring that audio signals are transmitted reliably and with high quality. This standard is particularly important for live productions where audio quality is critical. By using AES67, broadcasters can ensure that their audio signals are handled professionally over IP networks, maintaining the quality expected by their audiences.

Video Encoding and Streaming

For video encoding and streaming, broadcasters are looking at various standards and technologies, including HLS (HTTP Live Streaming), HEVC (High Efficiency Video Coding), and AV1. HEVC and AV1 offer high-efficiency video coding, which can significantly reduce the bandwidth required for streaming high-quality video. Meanwhile, HLS is widely adopted for streaming because it allows for adaptive bitrate streaming, ensuring that viewers receive the best possible video quality based on their internet connection.

Reliable Transport with SRT and RIST

For reliable and low-latency transport of video over IP networks, technologies like SRT (Secure Reliable Transport) and RIST (Reliable Internet Stream Transport) are gaining traction. These protocols are designed to provide error-free and secure video transport over the internet, which is critical for live broadcasting. By using SRT or RIST, broadcasters can ensure that their streams are delivered reliably, even over unpredictable internet connections.

NDI for IP Video Production

NDI (Network Device Interface) is a technology that enables IP video production workflows. NDI allows devices on the same network to automatically discover and communicate with each other, making it easy to set up complex IP video production environments. This technology is particularly useful for live productions, where the ability to quickly and easily set up and manage IP streams is essential. By adopting NDI, broadcasters can simplify their IP video production workflows.

As the broadcast industry moves towards IP, standards like SMPTE ST 2110 and NMOS will be crucial. They provide the framework for a professional, reliable IP infrastructure that can support the demands of live production and broadcast. The key for broadcasters, especially those in the small and mid-market, will be to approach this transition in a way that is both pragmatic and visionary, leveraging hybrid solutions and new technologies to create agile, responsive IP networks.

Future of Broadcast Engineering

By 2030, the landscape of broadcast engineering will be significantly different, with IP infrastructure at its core. Broadcasters will need to be adept at managing IP networks, troubleshooting IP issues, and leveraging the flexibility and scalability that IP offers. The adoption of cloud services, artificial intelligence (AI), and machine learning (ML) will also play a significant role in shaping the future of broadcast engineering, enabling more automated and personalized content delivery. As the industry moves forward, the ability to adapt to new technologies and standards will be essential for broadcasters looking to stay competitive.

Practical Steps for Broadcasters

For broadcasters embarking on the IP transition, several practical steps can be taken. First, assessing the current infrastructure and identifying areas where IP can be integrated is crucial. This includes evaluating the need for SDI to IP gateways, NMOS for device discovery, and AES67 for audio over IP. Next, broadcasters should develop a phased transition plan, starting with areas of their operation where IP can offer the most immediate benefits. Training staff on IP technologies and troubleshooting is also essential. Finally, staying updated with the latest standards and technologies, such as HEVC, AV1, SRT, and RIST, will help broadcasters make informed decisions about their IP infrastructure.

Industry Collaboration and Standards

Industry collaboration and the development of open standards are critical for the successful transition to IP. Standards like SMPTE ST 2110 and NMOS are the result of collaborative efforts among broadcasters, manufacturers, and other industry stakeholders. These standards ensure interoperability among devices from different manufacturers, which is essential for creating flexible and scalable IP networks. As the industry continues to evolve, ongoing collaboration and the development of new standards will be necessary to address emerging challenges and opportunities.

  • Assess current infrastructure to identify areas for IP integration.
  • Develop a phased transition plan to gradually move to IP.
  • Train staff on IP technologies and troubleshooting.
  • Stay updated with the latest IP standards and technologies.
  • Participate in industry collaborations to contribute to the development of open standards.

The journey to an IP-based broadcast infrastructure is complex and requires careful planning, execution, and ongoing learning. However, with the right approach and a commitment to adopting and leveraging the latest standards and technologies, small and mid-market broadcasters can successfully navigate this transition and position themselves for success in a rapidly changing media landscape.
