The IP Transition in 2026: A Practical Engineering Guide for Broadcasters

The Streamic Editorial Team March 22, 2026 1,228 words · 6 min read Analysis

Introduction to the IP Transition

The broadcast industry is undergoing a significant transformation as it migrates from traditional SDI (Serial Digital Interface) infrastructure to IP (Internet Protocol) based systems. This transition is driven by the need for greater flexibility, scalability, and cost efficiency in broadcast operations. By 2030, it is expected that most broadcasters will have adopted IP infrastructure, with SMPTE ST 2110 emerging as the standard for professional media over IP networks. For small and mid-market broadcasters, this transition poses both challenges and opportunities, as they seek to balance the benefits of IP with the practical realities of their existing infrastructure and resources.

Understanding SMPTE ST 2110

SMPTE ST 2110 is a suite of standards that defines the carriage of professional media over IP networks. It provides a framework for the transport of video, audio, and metadata over IP, allowing for the creation of flexible and scalable broadcast systems. SMPTE ST 2110 is designed to work in conjunction with other standards, such as NMOS (Networked Media Open Specification), which provides a control and management layer for IP based systems. By adopting SMPTE ST 2110 and NMOS, broadcasters can create a robust and flexible IP infrastructure that meets their specific needs.

Challenges in the IP Transition

For small and mid-market broadcasters, the transition to IP infrastructure poses several challenges. One of the primary concerns is the need for new skills and training, as traditional SDI engineers may not have experience with IP networking and protocols. Additionally, the cost of new IP equipment and infrastructure can be prohibitive, especially for smaller broadcasters with limited budgets.

The IP transition is not just about replacing SDI with IP, it's about changing the way we think about broadcast infrastructure and operations. It requires a fundamental shift in our approach to engineering and workflow design.
Furthermore, the complexity of IP systems can make it difficult to ensure reliable and stable operation, which is critical for broadcast applications.

Hybrid Approaches to the IP Transition

Given the challenges of a full IP transition, many broadcasters are adopting hybrid approaches that combine traditional SDI infrastructure with IP based systems. This allows them to leverage the benefits of IP while still utilizing their existing SDI equipment and expertise. Hybrid systems often use SDI to IP gateways or converters to bridge the two technologies, enabling broadcasters to gradually transition to IP over time. This approach can also help to reduce the upfront costs and complexity associated with a full IP transition.

Key Technologies for the IP Transition

Several key technologies are playing a critical role in the IP transition, including AES67 for audio over IP, HLS (HTTP Live Streaming) and HEVC (High Efficiency Video Coding) for video compression and streaming, and AV1 for open-source video compression. Additionally, SRT (Secure Reliable Transport) and RIST (Reliable Internet Stream Transport) are being used for secure and reliable IP streaming, while NDI (Network Device Interface) is providing a flexible and scalable way to transport video and audio over IP networks. These technologies are enabling broadcasters to create flexible and efficient IP based systems that meet their specific needs and requirements.

Practical Considerations for the IP Transition

As broadcasters embark on the IP transition, there are several practical considerations that must be taken into account. These include the need for robust and reliable IP networking infrastructure, as well as the importance of careful planning and design to ensure that IP systems meet the specific needs of the broadcaster. Additionally, broadcasters must consider the impact of the IP transition on their existing workflows and operations, and develop strategies for managing and troubleshooting IP based systems. By taking a practical and incremental approach to the IP transition, broadcasters can minimize disruption and ensure a smooth transition to IP infrastructure.

Best Practices for the IP Transition

As the broadcast industry continues to evolve and adopt IP infrastructure, several best practices are emerging for the IP transition. These include the importance of careful planning and design, as well as the need for ongoing training and education to ensure that engineers and operators have the skills and knowledge needed to work with IP based systems. Additionally, broadcasters should prioritize the development of flexible and scalable IP infrastructure, using open standards and protocols wherever possible to ensure interoperability and compatibility with a wide range of equipment and systems. By following these best practices, broadcasters can ensure a successful and efficient IP transition that meets their specific needs and requirements.

The Future of the IP Transition

Looking ahead to 2030, it is clear that the IP transition will have a profound impact on the broadcast industry. As IP infrastructure becomes the norm, broadcasters will be able to create more flexible and efficient systems that are better suited to the demands of modern broadcasting. With the adoption of SMPTE ST 2110 and other open standards, broadcasters will be able to leverage the benefits of IP while ensuring interoperability and compatibility with a wide range of equipment and systems. As the industry continues to evolve, it is likely that we will see the development of new and innovative applications of IP technology in broadcasting, such as the use of IP for remote production and contribution. By embracing the IP transition and adopting a forward-looking approach to broadcast engineering, broadcasters can position themselves for success in a rapidly changing media landscape.

Industry Insights and Observations

According to industry experts, the IP transition is not just about technology, but also about changing the way broadcasters think about their operations and workflows.

The IP transition is an opportunity for broadcasters to rethink their entire workflow and create more efficient and flexible systems that are better suited to the demands of modern broadcasting.
As the industry continues to evolve, it is likely that we will see a greater emphasis on the use of IP for cloud-based services and remote production, as well as the development of new and innovative applications of IP technology in broadcasting. By staying at the forefront of these trends and developments, broadcasters can ensure that they remain competitive and relevant in a rapidly changing media landscape.

Key Takeaways for Broadcasters

For broadcasters embarking on the IP transition, there are several key takeaways to consider. These include the importance of careful planning and design, as well as the need for ongoing training and education to ensure that engineers and operators have the skills and knowledge needed to work with IP based systems. Additionally, broadcasters should prioritize the development of flexible and scalable IP infrastructure, using open standards and protocols wherever possible to ensure interoperability and compatibility with a wide range of equipment and systems. By following these best practices and staying informed about the latest trends and developments in IP technology, broadcasters can ensure a successful and efficient IP transition that meets their specific needs and requirements. The following are some key considerations for broadcasters:

  • Develop a clear understanding of the benefits and challenges of the IP transition
  • Create a detailed plan and timeline for the IP transition
  • Provide ongoing training and education for engineers and operators
  • Prioritize the development of flexible and scalable IP infrastructure
  • Use open standards and protocols wherever possible to ensure interoperability and compatibility

By following these guidelines and staying focused on the key considerations for the IP transition, broadcasters can ensure a successful and efficient migration to IP infrastructure that meets their specific needs and requirements.
