The IP Transition in 2026: A Practical Engineering Guide for Broadcasters

Introduction to the IP Transition

The broadcast industry is undergoing a significant transformation as it migrates from traditional SDI (Serial Digital Interface) to IP (Internet Protocol) infrastructure. This transition is driven by the need for greater flexibility, scalability, and cost-effectiveness in broadcast operations. By 2030, it is expected that most broadcasters will have completed their transition to IP, with SMPTE ST 2110 emerging as the dominant standard for professional media over IP networks. In this article, we will delve into the practical challenges and solutions faced by small and mid-market broadcasters as they navigate this transition.

Understanding the SMPTE ST 2110 Standard

SMPTE ST 2110 is a suite of standards that defines the carriage of professional media over IP networks. It provides a framework for the transport of video, audio, and metadata over IP networks, enabling broadcasters to build more flexible and scalable systems. The standard consists of several parts, including SMPTE ST 2110-10 for video, SMPTE ST 2110-30 for audio, and SMPTE ST 2110-40 for metadata. By adopting SMPTE ST 2110, broadcasters can take advantage of the benefits of IP technology, including greater flexibility, improved scalability, and reduced costs.

Challenges in the IP Transition

While the benefits of IP infrastructure are clear, the transition from SDI to IP is not without its challenges. One of the main hurdles faced by small and mid-market broadcasters is the need for significant investment in new equipment and training. IP systems require a different set of skills and knowledge than traditional SDI systems, and broadcasters must invest in training their staff to ensure a smooth transition. Additionally, the transition to IP requires a thorough understanding of network architecture and cybersecurity, which can be a challenge for broadcasters with limited IT expertise.

Hybrid Approaches to the IP Transition

For many small and mid-market broadcasters, a hybrid approach to the IP transition may be the most practical solution. This involves maintaining existing SDI infrastructure while gradually introducing IP components. By using SDI-to-IP gateways and IP-to-SDI converters, broadcasters can integrate IP equipment into their existing SDI workflows, allowing them to transition to IP at their own pace. This approach enables broadcasters to take advantage of the benefits of IP technology while minimizing disruption to their existing operations.

The key to a successful IP transition is to take a phased approach, focusing on one area of the broadcast chain at a time. This allows broadcasters to test and refine their IP systems before rolling them out more widely. - John Smith, Chief Engineer, ABC Broadcasting

The Role of NMOS in the IP Transition

NMOS (Networked Media Open Specifications) is a suite of APIs (Application Programming Interfaces) and data models that enables the discovery, registration, and control of IP devices on a network. NMOS provides a standardized way for IP devices to communicate with each other, allowing broadcasters to build more flexible and scalable systems. By using NMOS, broadcasters can simplify the process of device discovery and registration, reducing the complexity and cost of their IP systems.

Audio over IP using AES67

AES67 is a standard for audio over IP that provides a way for audio signals to be transported over IP networks. AES67 is widely used in the broadcast industry, and is often used in conjunction with SMPTE ST 2110 for video and metadata. By using AES67 for audio over IP, broadcasters can take advantage of the benefits of IP technology, including greater flexibility and improved scalability. AES67 is also compatible with other audio over IP protocols, such as Dante and Ravenna, allowing broadcasters to build systems that integrate equipment from multiple vendors.

Streaming and Distribution in the IP Era

The transition to IP infrastructure also has significant implications for streaming and distribution. HLS (HTTP Live Streaming) and DASH (Dynamic Adaptive Streaming over HTTP) are widely used for streaming, and provide a way for broadcasters to deliver high-quality video to a wide range of devices. By using HEVC (High Efficiency Video Coding) or AV1 for video compression, broadcasters can reduce the bandwidth required for streaming, making it possible to deliver high-quality video to a wider audience. Additionally, protocols such as SRT (Secure Reliable Transport) and RIST (Reliable Internet Stream Transport) provide a way for broadcasters to transport IP streams over the internet, allowing for more flexible and scalable distribution systems.

NDI and the Future of IP in Broadcast

NDI (Network Device Interface) is a protocol developed by NewTek that enables IP devices to communicate with each other and transport video, audio, and metadata over IP networks. NDI is widely used in the broadcast industry, and provides a way for broadcasters to build flexible and scalable systems. By using NDI, broadcasters can take advantage of the benefits of IP technology, including greater flexibility and improved scalability. As the broadcast industry continues to evolve, it is likely that NDI will play an increasingly important role in the development of IP systems.

As the broadcast industry continues to transition to IP infrastructure, it is clear that the benefits of IP technology will have a significant impact on the way broadcasters operate. By adopting standards such as SMPTE ST 2110 and NMOS, and using protocols such as AES67 and NDI, broadcasters can build more flexible and scalable systems, and take advantage of the benefits of IP technology. As we look to the future, it is likely that the broadcast industry will continue to evolve, with IP technology playing an increasingly important role in the development of new systems and workflows.

Real-World Implementations of IP Infrastructure

Several broadcasters have already begun to implement IP infrastructure in their operations. For example, ABC Broadcasting has built a new IP-based master control system using SMPTE ST 2110 and NMOS. This system provides a flexible and scalable platform for managing and distributing content, and has allowed ABC Broadcasting to reduce costs and improve efficiency. Similarly, XYZ Media has implemented an IP-based streaming system using HLS and HEVC, allowing them to deliver high-quality video to a wide range of devices.

Best Practices for IP Implementation

When implementing IP infrastructure, there are several best practices that broadcasters should follow. First, it is essential to have a clear understanding of the network architecture and cybersecurity requirements of the system. This includes ensuring that the IP network is properly configured and secured, and that all devices are properly registered and authenticated. Second, broadcasters should ensure that they have the necessary IT expertise and resources to support the IP system. This may involve hiring new staff or providing training to existing staff. Finally, broadcasters should ensure that they have a clear plan for transitioning to IP infrastructure, including a phased approach to implementation and a clear timeline for completion.

By following these best practices and adopting standards such as SMPTE ST 2110 and NMOS, broadcasters can ensure a successful transition to IP infrastructure and take advantage of the benefits of IP technology. As the broadcast industry continues to evolve, it is likely that IP technology will play an increasingly important role in the development of new systems and workflows, and broadcasters that are prepared to adapt to these changes will be well-positioned for success.
