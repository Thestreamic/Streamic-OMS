The IP Transition in 2026: A Practical Engineering Guide for Broadcasters

Introduction to IP Transition

The migration from traditional SDI (Serial Digital Interface) to IP (Internet Protocol) infrastructure is a significant undertaking for broadcasters, particularly for small and mid-market players. As the industry continues to evolve, the need for a more flexible, scalable, and cost-effective solution has become increasingly evident. The SMPTE ST 2110 standard has emerged as a key enabler of this transition, providing a common framework for the transport of video, audio, and metadata over IP networks. In this article, we will delve into the practical aspects of IP transition, exploring the challenges and solutions for broadcasters navigating this complex landscape.

Understanding SMPTE ST 2110

SMPTE ST 2110 is a suite of standards that defines the carriage of video, audio, and metadata over IP networks. It provides a framework for the separation of essence (video, audio, and metadata) from the transport layer, allowing for greater flexibility and scalability. The standard comprises several parts, including SMPTE ST 2110-10 for video, SMPTE ST 2110-30 for audio, and SMPTE ST 2110-40 for metadata. By adopting SMPTE ST 2110, broadcasters can take advantage of the many benefits of IP infrastructure, including reduced cabling, increased flexibility, and improved scalability.

Challenges in IP Transition

While the benefits of IP transition are clear, the process of migration poses several challenges for broadcasters. One of the primary concerns is the need for new skills and training, as IP infrastructure requires a different set of expertise than traditional SDI systems. Additionally, the selection of equipment and vendors can be overwhelming, with a multitude of options available in the market.

The biggest challenge we faced was not the technology itself, but rather the cultural shift within our organization. We had to retrain our staff and change our workflows to accommodate the new IP infrastructure.
Furthermore, the integration of IP systems with existing SDI infrastructure can be complex, requiring careful planning and execution to ensure a smooth transition.

Hybrid Approaches

For many broadcasters, a hybrid approach to IP transition may be the most practical solution. This involves deploying IP infrastructure in parallel with existing SDI systems, allowing for a gradual transition to the new technology. Hybrid approaches can help minimize disruption to existing workflows and provide a fallback option in case of any issues with the IP infrastructure. NMOS (Networked Media Open Specifications) is an important standard in this context, providing a framework for the discovery and registration of devices on IP networks. By using NMOS, broadcasters can simplify the process of integrating IP devices with their existing infrastructure.

Audio and Video Transport

The transport of audio and video over IP networks is a critical aspect of the transition process. AES67 is a widely adopted standard for audio over IP, providing a common framework for the transport of audio essence. For video, SMPTE ST 2110-10 defines the carriage of video over IP networks, while SMPTE ST 2110-20 provides a standard for the transport of metadata. In addition to these standards, HLS (HTTP Live Streaming) and HEVC (High Efficiency Video Coding) are important technologies for the delivery of video over IP networks. AV1 is another emerging standard for video compression, offering improved efficiency and performance compared to HEVC.

Reliable Internet Stream Transport

Reliable internet stream transport is essential for the delivery of high-quality video and audio over IP networks. SRT (Secure Reliable Transport) and RIST (Reliable Internet Stream Transport) are two important protocols in this context, providing a mechanism for the secure and reliable transport of video and audio over the internet. These protocols can help broadcasters ensure the quality and integrity of their streams, even in the presence of network congestion or packet loss. NDI (Network Device Interface) is another important technology for the transport of video and audio over IP networks, providing a high-performance and low-latency solution for live production and streaming applications.

Practical Considerations

When planning an IP transition, there are several practical considerations that broadcasters must take into account. One of the most important is the selection of equipment and vendors, as this can have a significant impact on the success of the transition. Broadcasters should look for vendors that have experience with SMPTE ST 2110 and NMOS, and that can provide a comprehensive solution for their IP infrastructure needs. Additionally, broadcasters must consider the need for new skills and training, as well as the potential impact on their existing workflows and business processes.

Equipment Selection

When selecting equipment for an IP transition, broadcasters should consider a range of factors, including compatibility with SMPTE ST 2110 and NMOS, as well as support for other relevant standards such as AES67 and HLS. Broadcasters should also consider the scalability and flexibility of the equipment, as well as its ability to integrate with existing SDI infrastructure.

We looked for equipment that was flexible and scalable, and that could integrate with our existing infrastructure. We also considered the cost and the support offered by the vendor.
By carefully evaluating these factors, broadcasters can ensure a successful IP transition and achieve the many benefits of IP infrastructure.

Future Outlook

As the broadcast industry continues to evolve, the importance of IP infrastructure will only continue to grow. By 2030, it is likely that most broadcasters will have completed their transition to IP, and will be leveraging the many benefits of this technology to improve their operations and deliver high-quality content to their audiences. The development of new standards and technologies, such as AV1 and RIST, will continue to play a critical role in this process, enabling broadcasters to take advantage of the latest advancements in IP infrastructure. As the industry looks to the future, it is clear that IP transition will be a key driver of innovation and growth, enabling broadcasters to meet the evolving needs of their audiences and stay competitive in a rapidly changing market.

Emerging Trends

Several emerging trends are likely to shape the future of IP transition in the broadcast industry. One of the most significant is the growing adoption of cloud-based services, which will enable broadcasters to take advantage of the scalability and flexibility of cloud infrastructure. Another important trend is the development of new IP protocols and standards, such as SMPTE ST 2110-22 for compressed video, which will provide broadcasters with even greater flexibility and efficiency in their IP infrastructure. By staying at the forefront of these trends, broadcasters can ensure that they are well-positioned to take advantage of the many benefits of IP infrastructure, and to deliver high-quality content to their audiences in the years to come.

Real-World Applications

IP transition is not just a theoretical concept, but a practical reality that is being implemented by broadcasters around the world. One example is the use of SMPTE ST 2110 for live production and streaming applications, which is enabling broadcasters to take advantage of the flexibility and scalability of IP infrastructure. Another example is the use of NDI for remote production and contribution applications, which is allowing broadcasters to reduce their costs and improve their efficiency. By examining these real-world applications, broadcasters can gain a better understanding of the practical benefits and challenges of IP transition, and can develop strategies for implementing this technology in their own operations.

Case Studies

Several case studies illustrate the successful implementation of IP transition in the broadcast industry. One example is a small-market broadcaster that used SMPTE ST 2110 to transition its master control and playout operations to IP infrastructure. Another example is a mid-market broadcaster that used NDI to implement a remote production workflow, reducing its costs and improving its efficiency. By examining these case studies, broadcasters can gain a better understanding of the practical challenges and benefits of IP transition, and can develop strategies for implementing this technology in their own operations.

We were able to reduce our costs and improve our efficiency by implementing an IP-based remote production workflow. The flexibility and scalability of IP infrastructure have been a game-changer for our organization.

Industry Collaboration

The success of IP transition in the broadcast industry will depend on the collaboration and cooperation of multiple stakeholders, including broadcasters, vendors, and industry organizations. One example of this collaboration is the development of the SMPTE ST 2110 standard, which was the result of a joint effort by multiple industry organizations and stakeholders. Another example is the NMOS initiative, which is a collaborative effort to develop a common framework for the discovery and registration of devices on IP networks. By working together, the broadcast industry can ensure a smooth and successful transition to IP infrastructure, and can take advantage of the many benefits that this technology has to offer.

Standards Development

The development of standards is a critical aspect of the IP transition process, as it enables the creation of a common framework for the transport of video, audio, and metadata over IP networks. The SMPTE ST 2110 standard is a key example of this, providing a comprehensive framework for the carriage of video, audio, and metadata over IP networks. Other important standards include AES67 for audio over IP, and HLS for the delivery of video over IP networks. By participating in the development of these standards, broadcasters can ensure that their needs and requirements are taken into account, and that the resulting standards meet their practical needs and requirements.
