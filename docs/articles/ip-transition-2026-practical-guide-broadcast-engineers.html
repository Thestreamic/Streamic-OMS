The IP Transition in 2026: A Practical Engineering Guide for Broadcasters

The Streamic Editorial Team March 22, 2026 Analysis

Introduction to the IP Transition

The transition from traditional SDI (Serial Digital Interface) to IP (Internet Protocol) infrastructure is a significant undertaking for broadcasters, requiring careful planning, execution, and investment. As the industry continues to evolve, the need for a more flexible, scalable, and cost-effective infrastructure has become increasingly important. Small and mid-market broadcasters, in particular, face unique challenges in this transition, as they often have limited resources and must balance the need for modernization with budget constraints. In this analysis, we will delve into the practical aspects of the IP transition, focusing on the use of SMPTE ST 2110, NMOS, and hybrid approaches to help broadcasters navigate this complex process.

Understanding SMPTE ST 2110

SMPTE ST 2110 is a suite of standards that defines the carriage, synchronization, and description of separate video, audio, and ancillary data streams over IP networks. This standard provides a framework for broadcasters to build a more flexible and scalable infrastructure, enabling the transport of uncompressed video and audio signals over IP networks. SMPTE ST 2110 is based on a suite of standards, including SMPTE ST 2110-10 for video, SMPTE ST 2110-30 for audio, and SMPTE ST 2110-40 for ancillary data. By adopting SMPTE ST 2110, broadcasters can take advantage of the many benefits of IP infrastructure, including improved flexibility, scalability, and cost-effectiveness.

Hybrid Approaches to IP Transition

For many small and mid-market broadcasters, a hybrid approach to the IP transition may be the most practical solution. This involves combining traditional SDI infrastructure with IP-based systems, allowing broadcasters to gradually migrate to IP while still supporting existing SDI equipment. Hybrid approaches can help reduce the upfront costs associated with a full IP migration and minimize disruption to existing workflows. By using SDI-to-IP gateways, broadcasters can convert SDI signals to IP streams, enabling them to integrate IP-based equipment and systems into their existing infrastructure.

NMOS and the Importance of Device Discovery

NMOS (Networked Media Open Specification) is an open standard that provides a framework for device discovery, registration, and control in IP-based media networks. NMOS is essential for broadcasters transitioning to IP, as it enables the automatic discovery and registration of devices on the network, simplifying the process of building and managing IP-based systems. Audio Over IP and the Role of AES67

In addition to video, audio is a critical component of broadcast operations, and the transition to IP infrastructure requires careful consideration of audio transport. AES67 is an open standard for audio over IP that provides a framework for the transport of high-quality audio signals over IP networks. By using AES67, broadcasters can ensure that their audio signals are transported reliably and with high quality, regardless of the underlying network infrastructure. AES67 is an essential component of the SMPTE ST 2110 suite of standards, providing a standardized approach to audio transport in IP-based broadcast systems.

Streaming and the Role of HLS, HEVC, and AV1

Streaming is a critical component of modern broadcast operations, and the transition to IP infrastructure requires careful consideration of streaming protocols and codecs. HLS (HTTP Live Streaming) is a widely-used streaming protocol that provides a flexible and scalable way to deliver live and on-demand content to a wide range of devices. HEVC (High Efficiency Video Coding) and AV1 are two popular video codecs that provide high-quality video compression and are widely supported by streaming devices and platforms. By using HLS, HEVC, and AV1, broadcasters can ensure that their streaming services are delivered with high quality and reliability, regardless of the underlying network infrastructure.

Reliable Transport and the Role of SRT and RIST

Reliable transport is a critical component of IP-based broadcast systems, and the transition to IP infrastructure requires careful consideration of transport protocols. SRT (Secure Reliable Transport) and RIST (Reliable Internet Stream Transport) are two popular transport protocols that provide a flexible and scalable way to transport live and on-demand content over IP networks. By using SRT and RIST, broadcasters can ensure that their content is transported reliably and with high quality, regardless of the underlying network infrastructure. SRT and RIST are essential components of modern broadcast systems, providing a standardized approach to reliable transport in IP-based systems.

NDI and the Future of IP-Based Broadcast Systems

NDI (Network Device Interface) is a popular IP-based protocol that provides a flexible and scalable way to transport live video and audio signals over IP networks. NDI is widely supported by broadcast devices and systems, providing a standardized approach to IP-based transport. By using NDI, broadcasters can create flexible and scalable IP-based systems that can support a wide range of devices and applications. As the broadcast industry continues to evolve, NDI is likely to play an increasingly important role in the development of IP-based broadcast systems.

As broadcasters navigate the complex process of transitioning from SDI to IP infrastructure, it is essential to consider the many technical and practical challenges involved. By understanding the role of SMPTE ST 2110, NMOS, AES67, HLS, HEVC, AV1, SRT, RIST, and NDI in IP-based broadcast systems, broadcasters can create flexible, scalable, and cost-effective infrastructure that supports a wide range of devices and applications. As the industry continues to evolve, it is likely that IP-based broadcast systems will play an increasingly important role in the creation and distribution of live and on-demand content.
