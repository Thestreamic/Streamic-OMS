The IP Transition in 2026: A Practical Engineering Guide for Broadcasters

The real-world challenges and solutions for broadcasters migrating from SDI to IP infrastructure using SMPTE ST 2110, NMOS, and hybrid approaches. Focus on small and mid-market broadcasters, not tier-

The Streamic Editorial Team March 22, 2026 Analysis

Introduction to IP Transition

The migration from traditional SDI (Serial Digital Interface) to IP (Internet Protocol) infrastructure is a significant undertaking for broadcasters, particularly for small and mid-market players. As the broadcast industry continues to evolve, the need for a more flexible, scalable, and cost-effective infrastructure has become increasingly important. The SMPTE ST 2110 standard has emerged as a critical component in this transition, providing a framework for the transport of video, audio, and ancillary data over IP networks. In this article, we will delve into the practical challenges and solutions for broadcasters navigating the IP transition, with a focus on the unique needs of small and mid-market broadcasters.

Understanding SMPTE ST 2110

SMPTE ST 2110 is a suite of standards that defines the carriage of video, audio, and ancillary data over IP networks. This standard is designed to provide a common framework for the transport of media signals, enabling the creation of more flexible and scalable broadcast infrastructures. SMPTE ST 2110 is composed of several parts, including SMPTE ST 2110-10 (system architecture and overview), SMPTE ST 2110-20 (uncompressed video), SMPTE ST 2110-30 (audio), and SMPTE ST 2110-40 (ancillary data). By adopting SMPTE ST 2110, broadcasters can take advantage of the benefits of IP technology, including improved flexibility, scalability, and cost savings.

Challenges in IP Transition

While the benefits of IP technology are clear, the transition from SDI to IP infrastructure is not without its challenges. One of the primary concerns for small and mid-market broadcasters is the cost of upgrading their existing infrastructure to support IP. This can include the purchase of new equipment, such as IP-enabled routers, switches, and endpoints, as well as the training of staff to operate and maintain the new infrastructure. Additionally, broadcasters must also consider the need for Quality of Service (QoS) and Quality of Experience (QoE) in their IP networks, to ensure that media signals are delivered reliably and with the required level of quality.

Hybrid Approaches

For many small and mid-market broadcasters, a hybrid approach to the IP transition may be the most practical solution. This involves implementing IP technology in certain areas of the broadcast infrastructure, while continuing to use SDI in other areas. For example, a broadcaster may choose to use IP for the transport of media signals between facilities, while continuing to use SDI for the connection of equipment within a facility. This hybrid approach can help to reduce the upfront costs of the transition, while still allowing broadcasters to take advantage of the benefits of IP technology.

The key to a successful hybrid approach is to identify the areas of the broadcast infrastructure where IP technology can provide the greatest benefits, and to implement IP solutions in those areas first.

NMOS and Device Discovery

Another critical component of the IP transition is the use of NMOS (Networked Media Open Specification) for device discovery and connection management. NMOS is an open standard that provides a common framework for the discovery and registration of IP-enabled devices on a network. This allows broadcasters to easily identify and connect devices, such as cameras, mixers, and routers, and to manage the flow of media signals between them. NMOS is particularly useful in hybrid environments, where IP and SDI devices may need to coexist. By using NMOS, broadcasters can simplify the process of device discovery and connection management, and reduce the risk of errors and downtime.

AES67 and Audio over IP

In addition to SMPTE ST 2110 and NMOS, another important standard for the IP transition is AES67 (Audio over IP). AES67 provides a common framework for the transport of audio signals over IP networks, and is widely used in the broadcast industry. By adopting AES67, broadcasters can take advantage of the benefits of audio over IP, including improved flexibility and scalability, and reduced costs. AES67 is also compatible with SMPTE ST 2110, allowing broadcasters to use a single IP infrastructure for both video and audio signals.

Streaming and Distribution

Once media signals have been transported and processed using IP technology, they must be streamed and distributed to viewers. This can be achieved using a variety of protocols, including HLS (HTTP Live Streaming), HLS (HTTP Live Streaming), and SRT (Secure Reliable Transport). HLS is a widely used protocol for streaming media over the internet, and is supported by a wide range of devices and platforms. SRT is a more recent protocol, designed to provide a secure and reliable way to transport media signals over the internet. By using these protocols, broadcasters can deliver high-quality media signals to viewers, while also ensuring the security and reliability of the stream.

Compression and Encoding

In order to stream and distribute media signals efficiently, broadcasters must also consider the use of compression and encoding technologies. HEVC (High Efficiency Video Coding) and AV1 (AOMedia Video 1) are two popular compression standards used in the broadcast industry. HEVC is a widely used standard for compressing video signals, and is known for its high efficiency and quality. AV1 is a more recent standard, designed to provide a royalty-free alternative to HEVC. By using these compression standards, broadcasters can reduce the bandwidth required to stream and distribute media signals, while also maintaining the required level of quality.

Real-World Implementations

While the IP transition can seem daunting, there are many real-world examples of broadcasters who have successfully made the transition. For example, a small-market broadcaster in the United States recently upgraded its infrastructure to support IP technology, using SMPTE ST 2110 and NMOS to transport and manage media signals. The broadcaster was able to reduce its costs and improve its flexibility, while also maintaining the required level of quality.

The key to a successful IP transition is to take a phased approach, starting with a small pilot project and gradually expanding to other areas of the broadcast infrastructure.

NDI and the Future of IP

Finally, another important technology to consider in the IP transition is NDI (Network Device Interface). NDI is a protocol developed by NewTek, designed to provide a simple and efficient way to transport video signals over IP networks. NDI is widely used in the broadcast industry, and is known for its high quality and low latency. By using NDI, broadcasters can take advantage of the benefits of IP technology, while also simplifying the process of transporting and managing media signals. As the broadcast industry continues to evolve, it is likely that NDI will play an increasingly important role in the IP transition.

Best Practices for IP Transition

As broadcasters navigate the IP transition, there are several best practices to keep in mind. First, it is essential to develop a clear plan and roadmap for the transition, including a detailed timeline and budget. Second, broadcasters should identify the areas of the broadcast infrastructure where IP technology can provide the greatest benefits, and prioritize those areas first. Third, broadcasters should consider the use of hybrid approaches, where IP and SDI technologies are used together. Finally, broadcasters should stay up-to-date with the latest developments in IP technology, including new standards and protocols. By following these best practices, broadcasters can ensure a successful IP transition, and take advantage of the benefits of IP technology.

RIST and the Future of Media Transport

In addition to SMPTE ST 2110 and NMOS, another important standard to consider in the IP transition is RIST (Reliable Internet Stream Transport). RIST is a protocol designed to provide a reliable and secure way to transport media signals over the internet. RIST is widely used in the broadcast industry, and is known for its high quality and low latency. By using RIST, broadcasters can take advantage of the benefits of IP technology, while also ensuring the security and reliability of their media streams. As the broadcast industry continues to evolve, it is likely that RIST will play an increasingly important role in the IP transition.

Training and Education

Finally, as broadcasters navigate the IP transition, it is essential to provide training and education to staff. This can include training on IP technology, as well as training on the specific protocols and standards used in the broadcast infrastructure. By providing this training, broadcasters can ensure that their staff have the skills and knowledge needed to operate and maintain the new IP infrastructure. This can help to reduce the risk of errors and downtime, and ensure a smooth transition to IP technology.

The key to a successful IP transition is to provide ongoing training and education to staff, and to stay up-to-date with the latest developments in IP technology.
