The IP Transition in 2026: A Practical Engineering Guide for Broadcasters

The Streamic Editorial Team March 22, 2026 Analysis

Introduction to IP Transition

The broadcast industry is undergoing a significant transformation as it migrates from traditional SDI (Serial Digital Interface) to IP (Internet Protocol) infrastructure. This transition is driven by the need for greater flexibility, scalability, and cost-effectiveness in broadcast operations. By 2030, it is expected that most broadcasters will have completed their transition to IP, with SMPTE ST 2110 emerging as the standard of choice for professional media over IP networks. For small and mid-market broadcasters, the IP transition poses unique challenges, but also offers opportunities for growth and improvement.

Understanding SMPTE ST 2110

SMPTE ST 2110 is a suite of standards that defines the carriage and synchronization of video, audio, and metadata over IP networks. It provides a framework for the transport of professional media, enabling broadcasters to build flexible and scalable IP-based systems. The standard consists of several parts, including SMPTE ST 2110-10 for video, SMPTE ST 2110-30 for audio, and SMPTE ST 2110-40 for metadata. By adopting SMPTE ST 2110, broadcasters can ensure interoperability between equipment from different manufacturers and facilitate the integration of new technologies and services.

Challenges and Solutions for Small and Mid-Market Broadcasters

Small and mid-market broadcasters face several challenges when migrating to IP infrastructure, including limited budgets, lack of expertise, and legacy equipment. To overcome these challenges, broadcasters can adopt a hybrid approach, combining SDI and IP technologies. This approach allows them to leverage their existing SDI infrastructure while introducing IP-based systems and services. NMOS (Networked Media Open Specification) is another key technology that can help small and mid-market broadcasters, providing a standardized interface for device discovery, registration, and connection management.

Case Study: Implementing SMPTE ST 2110 and NMOS

A mid-market broadcaster in the United States recently implemented SMPTE ST 2110 and NMOS as part of its IP transition. The broadcaster used SMPTE ST 2110 to transport video and audio signals over its IP network, while NMOS was used to manage device discovery and registration. The implementation was successful, with the broadcaster achieving significant cost savings and improvements in operational efficiency. As noted by the broadcaster's engineering director,

the key to a successful IP transition is careful planning and a phased approach, allowing broadcasters to test and refine their systems before going live.

Hybrid Approaches and Legacy Equipment

For many small and mid-market broadcasters, a hybrid approach to the IP transition is the most practical solution. This involves combining SDI and IP technologies, using SDI-to-IP gateways or converters to integrate legacy equipment into the IP-based system. AES67 is a key standard for audio over IP, providing a common protocol for professional audio equipment. By adopting AES67, broadcasters can ensure interoperability between audio devices from different manufacturers and facilitate the integration of new audio technologies and services.

Streaming and OTT Services

In addition to broadcast operations, many small and mid-market broadcasters are also delivering content over-the-top (OTT) and streaming services. HLS (HTTP Live Streaming) and DASH (Dynamic Adaptive Streaming over HTTP) are popular protocols for streaming media, providing a flexible and scalable way to deliver content to a wide range of devices. HEVC (High Efficiency Video Coding) and AV1 are also important codecs for streaming and OTT services, offering improved compression efficiency and reduced bandwidth requirements. By adopting these technologies, broadcasters can deliver high-quality video and audio streams to their audiences, while minimizing the impact on their networks and infrastructure.

Reliable Transport and Security

Reliable transport and security are critical considerations for broadcasters migrating to IP infrastructure. SRT (Secure Reliable Transport) and RIST (Reliable Internet Stream Transport) are two key protocols for reliable transport, providing a secure and resilient way to transport media streams over the internet. NDI (Network Device Interface) is another important technology, providing a standardized interface for IP-based devices and facilitating the integration of new equipment and services. By adopting these technologies, broadcasters can ensure the secure and reliable transport of their media streams, while minimizing the risk of errors or interruptions.

Best Practices for IP Transition

To ensure a successful IP transition, small and mid-market broadcasters should follow best practices such as careful planning, phased implementation, and thorough testing. They should also consider factors such as network design, device selection, and training and support. A well-planned and executed IP transition can bring significant benefits, including improved flexibility, scalability, and cost-effectiveness. As noted by a leading broadcast industry expert,

the key to a successful IP transition is a deep understanding of the technologies and standards involved, combined with a clear vision for the future of broadcast operations.

Future of Broadcast Engineering

By 2030, the broadcast industry is expected to be fully IP-based, with SMPTE ST 2110 and other related standards playing a central role. Small and mid-market broadcasters will need to continue adapting to new technologies and services, while ensuring the reliability, security, and quality of their broadcast operations. IP infrastructure will provide the foundation for a wide range of new services and applications, including virtualized broadcast systems, cloud-based media processing, and immersive audio and video experiences. As the broadcast industry continues to evolve, it is likely that new technologies and standards will emerge, further transforming the way broadcasters operate and deliver content to their audiences.

Emerging Trends and Technologies

Several emerging trends and technologies are likely to shape the future of broadcast engineering, including artificial intelligence (AI), machine learning (ML), and cloud-based services. These technologies will provide new opportunities for automation, optimization, and innovation in broadcast operations, while also introducing new challenges and complexities. By adopting a flexible and adaptable approach to the IP transition, small and mid-market broadcasters can position themselves for success in a rapidly changing media landscape. As the broadcast industry continues to evolve, it is likely that new technologies and standards will emerge, further transforming the way broadcasters operate and deliver content to their audiences.

  • SMPTE ST 2110 will continue to play a central role in the broadcast industry, providing a standardized framework for professional media over IP networks.
  • NMOS will become increasingly important, providing a standardized interface for device discovery, registration, and connection management.
  • AES67 will remain a key standard for audio over IP, ensuring interoperability between audio devices from different manufacturers.
  • HLS and DASH will continue to be widely used for streaming and OTT services, providing a flexible and scalable way to deliver content to a wide range of devices.
  • HEVC and AV1 will become increasingly important for streaming and OTT services, offering improved compression efficiency and reduced bandwidth requirements.

The IP transition is a complex and challenging process, but it also offers significant opportunities for small and mid-market broadcasters. By adopting a careful and phased approach, these broadcasters can ensure a successful transition to IP infrastructure, while minimizing the risks and disruptions associated with this process. As the broadcast industry continues to evolve, it is likely that new technologies and standards will emerge, further transforming the way broadcasters operate and deliver content to their audiences.
