The IP Transition in 2026: A Practical Engineering Guide for Broadcasters

The Streamic Editorial Team March 22, 2026 1,225 words · 6 min read Analysis

Introduction to the IP Transition

The broadcast industry is undergoing a significant transformation as it migrates from traditional **SDI (Serial Digital Interface)** to **IP (Internet Protocol)**-based infrastructure. This transition is driven by the need for greater flexibility, scalability, and cost-effectiveness in broadcast operations. By 2030, the majority of broadcasters are expected to have completed their transition to IP, with **SMPTE ST 2110** emerging as the dominant standard for professional media over IP networks. For small and mid-market broadcasters, the IP transition presents both opportunities and challenges, requiring a practical and phased approach to implementation.

Understanding SMPTE ST 2110

SMPTE ST 2110 is a suite of standards that defines the carriage, synchronization, and description of separate elementary essence streams over **IP** networks. This standard enables the transport of video, audio, and metadata as separate streams, allowing for greater flexibility and efficiency in broadcast operations. SMPTE ST 2110 is designed to work in conjunction with other standards, such as NMOS (Networked Media Open Specification), which provides a framework for the discovery, registration, and connection management of devices on an IP network. By adopting SMPTE ST 2110 and NMOS, broadcasters can create a more agile and responsive infrastructure, better suited to the demands of modern content creation and distribution.

Real-World Challenges and Solutions

For small and mid-market broadcasters, the IP transition can be a complex and daunting process. One of the primary challenges is the need to integrate IP-based systems with existing SDI infrastructure, which can require significant investment in new equipment and training. To address this challenge, many broadcasters are adopting a hybrid approach, which combines the benefits of IP with the familiarity of traditional SDI. This approach allows broadcasters to transition to IP at their own pace, while minimizing disruption to their operations. As noted by industry expert,

the key to a successful IP transition is to take a phased approach, focusing on specific areas of the broadcast chain, such as live production or playout, and then scaling up to other areas as needed.

Audio Over IP

Another critical aspect of the IP transition is the transport of audio signals over IP networks. AES67 is a widely adopted standard for audio over IP, which provides a common framework for the transport of audio signals between devices. AES67 is designed to work in conjunction with SMPTE ST 2110, allowing broadcasters to transport audio and video signals over the same IP network. This approach enables greater efficiency and flexibility in broadcast operations, while also reducing the complexity and cost of audio signal transport. By adopting AES67 and SMPTE ST 2110, broadcasters can create a more integrated and streamlined workflow, better suited to the demands of modern content creation and distribution.

Streaming and Distribution

The IP transition also has significant implications for streaming and distribution, as broadcasters seek to deliver high-quality content to a wide range of devices and platforms. HLS (HTTP Live Streaming) and DASH (Dynamic Adaptive Streaming over HTTP) are widely adopted standards for streaming, which provide a flexible and scalable framework for the delivery of live and on-demand content. By combining HLS and DASH with HEVC (High Efficiency Video Coding) and AV1 compression, broadcasters can deliver high-quality video streams with reduced bandwidth and latency. Additionally, SRT (Secure Reliable Transport) and RIST (Reliable Internet Stream Transport) are emerging as key standards for the secure and reliable transport of live video streams over IP networks, providing a more robust and resilient alternative to traditional UDP (User Datagram Protocol) and TCP (Transmission Control Protocol) protocols.

NDI and the Future of Live Production

NDI (Network Device Interface) is a widely adopted standard for live production, which provides a flexible and scalable framework for the transport of video and audio signals over IP networks. NDI is designed to work in conjunction with SMPTE ST 2110 and NMOS, allowing broadcasters to create a more integrated and streamlined workflow. By adopting NDI, broadcasters can reduce the complexity and cost of live production, while also increasing the flexibility and scalability of their operations. As the broadcast industry continues to evolve, NDI is likely to play an increasingly important role in the development of new live production workflows and applications.

Practical Considerations for Small and Mid-Market Broadcasters

For small and mid-market broadcasters, the IP transition requires careful planning and execution. One of the primary considerations is the need to assess existing infrastructure and determine which systems and devices can be upgraded or replaced with IP-based equivalents. This process can be complex and time-consuming, requiring significant investment in new equipment and training. To address this challenge, many broadcasters are working with systems integrators and technology vendors to develop customized IP transition plans, tailored to their specific needs and requirements. By taking a phased and pragmatic approach to the IP transition, small and mid-market broadcasters can minimize disruption to their operations, while also realizing the benefits of IP-based infrastructure.

Training and Education

Another critical aspect of the IP transition is the need for training and education. As broadcasters adopt IP-based systems and workflows, they require specialized skills and knowledge to operate and maintain these systems effectively. To address this challenge, many industry organizations and technology vendors are offering training and education programs, focused on IP-based broadcast engineering and operations. These programs provide broadcasters with the skills and knowledge they need to succeed in an IP-based environment, while also helping to develop a more skilled and adaptable workforce. By investing in training and education, broadcasters can ensure a smoother and more successful IP transition, while also realizing the full benefits of IP-based infrastructure.

The Future of Broadcast Engineering

By 2030, the broadcast industry is likely to be fully transitioned to IP-based infrastructure, with SMPTE ST 2110 and NMOS emerging as the dominant standards for professional media over IP networks. As the industry continues to evolve, we can expect to see new and innovative applications of IP-based technology, including the development of cloud-based workflows and virtualized broadcast systems. These developments will require broadcast engineers to have a deep understanding of IP-based systems and protocols, including SDN (Software-Defined Networking) and NFV (Network Functions Virtualization). By embracing these new technologies and workflows, broadcasters can create a more agile and responsive infrastructure, better suited to the demands of modern content creation and distribution. As noted by industry expert,

the future of broadcast engineering will be shaped by the need for greater flexibility, scalability, and cost-effectiveness, with IP-based infrastructure playing a central role in the development of new and innovative broadcast applications.

Key Takeaways

The IP transition is a complex and multifaceted process, requiring careful planning and execution. By adopting a phased and pragmatic approach, small and mid-market broadcasters can minimize disruption to their operations, while also realizing the benefits of IP-based infrastructure. Some key takeaways for broadcasters include:

  • Assess existing infrastructure and determine which systems and devices can be upgraded or replaced with IP-based equivalents
  • Develop a customized IP transition plan, tailored to specific needs and requirements
  • Invest in training and education programs, focused on IP-based broadcast engineering and operations
  • Consider adopting a hybrid approach, combining the benefits of IP with the familiarity of traditional SDI
  • Stay up-to-date with the latest developments in IP-based technology, including SDN and NFV
By following these guidelines and staying focused on the key challenges and opportunities of the IP transition, small and mid-market broadcasters can ensure a successful and sustainable transition to IP-based infrastructure.
