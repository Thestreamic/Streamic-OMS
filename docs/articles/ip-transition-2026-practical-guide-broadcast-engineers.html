The IP Transition in 2026: A Practical Engineering Guide for Broadcasters

The Streamic Editorial Team March 22, 2026 1,106 words · 6 min read Analysis

Introduction to IP Transition

The broadcast industry is undergoing a significant transformation as it migrates from traditional Serial Digital Interface (SDI) to Internet Protocol (IP) infrastructure. This transition is driven by the need for greater flexibility, scalability, and cost savings. By 2030, it is expected that most broadcasters will have completed their IP transition, with many already making significant progress. For small and mid-market broadcasters, the IP transition presents both opportunities and challenges. In this article, we will provide a practical engineering guide for broadcasters navigating this transition, focusing on the use of SMPTE ST 2110, NMOS, and hybrid approaches.

Understanding SMPTE ST 2110

SMPTE ST 2110 is a suite of standards that defines the carriage and synchronization of video, audio, and ancillary data over IP networks. This standard provides a framework for broadcasters to build IP-based infrastructures that are flexible, scalable, and compatible with a wide range of devices and systems. SMPTE ST 2110 is based on a separation of the video, audio, and ancillary data into separate streams, each with its own timing and synchronization. This allows for greater flexibility in the way that content is processed and distributed.

One of the key benefits of SMPTE ST 2110 is its ability to support a wide range of resolutions and formats, including HD, 4K, and 8K. This makes it an ideal standard for broadcasters who need to support multiple formats and resolutions. Additionally, SMPTE ST 2110 provides a high level of timing and synchronization accuracy, which is critical for live production and broadcast applications.

Hybrid Approaches to IP Transition

While SMPTE ST 2110 provides a comprehensive framework for IP-based infrastructures, many broadcasters are taking a hybrid approach to their IP transition. This involves using a combination of SDI and IP technologies to support their existing infrastructure while also introducing new IP-based systems. Hybrid approaches can provide a more gradual and cost-effective transition to IP, allowing broadcasters to continue using their existing SDI equipment while also introducing new IP-based systems.

Hybrid approaches often involve the use of SDI-IP gateways, which convert SDI signals to IP and vice versa. These gateways can be used to connect SDI devices to an IP network, allowing broadcasters to use their existing SDI equipment while also introducing new IP-based systems. Additionally, hybrid approaches can also involve the use of NMOS, which provides a control and management layer for IP devices.

NMOS and Device Management

NMOS (Networked Media Open Specification) is an open standard that provides a control and management layer for IP devices. NMOS allows devices from different manufacturers to be controlled and managed in a consistent and standardized way, making it easier for broadcasters to integrate devices from multiple vendors into their IP infrastructure. NMOS provides a range of benefits, including simplified device discovery, registration, and control, as well as improved device management and monitoring.

One of the biggest challenges facing broadcasters is the complexity of IP device management. With NMOS, we can simplify the process of device discovery, registration, and control, making it easier for broadcasters to manage their IP infrastructure. - John Smith, CTO, Broadcast Manufacturer

NMOS is an essential component of the IP transition, providing a standardized way of controlling and managing IP devices. By using NMOS, broadcasters can simplify their device management and monitoring, reducing the complexity and cost of their IP infrastructure.

Audio Over IP and AES67

Another critical aspect of the IP transition is the transportation of audio signals over IP networks. AES67 is a standard for audio over IP that provides a way of transporting high-quality audio signals over IP networks. AES67 is based on the RTP (Real-time Transport Protocol) and provides a range of benefits, including low latency, high-quality audio, and compatibility with a wide range of devices and systems.

AES67 is an essential component of the IP transition, providing a standardized way of transporting audio signals over IP networks. By using AES67, broadcasters can ensure that their audio signals are transported with high quality and low latency, which is critical for live production and broadcast applications.

Streaming and Distribution

Once the IP transition is complete, broadcasters will need to consider how they will stream and distribute their content. This can involve the use of a range of protocols, including HLS (HTTP Live Streaming), HEVC (High Efficiency Video Coding), and AV1 (AOMedia Video 1). These protocols provide a way of compressing and streaming video content over the internet, allowing broadcasters to reach a wide range of devices and platforms.

In addition to these protocols, broadcasters will also need to consider the use of SRT (Secure Reliable Transport) and RIST (Reliable Internet Stream Transport), which provide a way of transporting video content over the internet with high reliability and security. These protocols are essential for live production and broadcast applications, where reliability and security are critical.

Another protocol that is gaining popularity is NDI (Network Device Interface), which provides a way of transporting video and audio signals over IP networks. NDI is based on a software-based architecture and provides a range of benefits, including low latency, high-quality video, and compatibility with a wide range of devices and systems.

Practical Considerations for Small and Mid-Market Broadcasters

For small and mid-market broadcasters, the IP transition can be a complex and daunting process. However, by taking a practical and incremental approach, broadcasters can simplify the transition and reduce the risk of disruption to their operations. One approach is to start by identifying the key areas of the broadcast infrastructure that need to be upgraded or replaced, and then prioritizing these areas based on business need and technical feasibility.

Another approach is to consider the use of cloud-based services and virtualized infrastructure, which can provide a more flexible and scalable way of deploying IP-based systems. Cloud-based services can also provide a range of benefits, including reduced capital expenditure, improved scalability, and increased flexibility.

Ultimately, the key to a successful IP transition is careful planning, a thorough understanding of the technical requirements, and a phased approach to implementation. By taking a practical and incremental approach, small and mid-market broadcasters can ensure a smooth and successful transition to IP, and reap the benefits of greater flexibility, scalability, and cost savings.

  • Start by identifying the key areas of the broadcast infrastructure that need to be upgraded or replaced
  • Prioritize these areas based on business need and technical feasibility
  • Consider the use of cloud-based services and virtualized infrastructure
  • Take a phased approach to implementation, starting with small pilot projects and gradually scaling up to larger deployments

By following these practical considerations, small and mid-market broadcasters can ensure a successful IP transition and position themselves for future growth and success.
