The IP Transition in 2026: A Practical Engineering Guide for Broadcasters

The Streamic Editorial Team March 22, 2026 881 words · 4 min read Analysis

Introduction to the IP Transition

The broadcast industry is undergoing a significant transformation as it migrates from traditional SDI (Serial Digital Interface) infrastructure to IP (Internet Protocol)-based systems. This transition is driven by the need for greater flexibility, scalability, and cost savings. By 2030, the majority of broadcasters are expected to have completed their transition to IP, with many already making significant progress. For small and mid-market broadcasters, the transition to IP poses unique challenges, but also presents opportunities for increased efficiency and competitiveness.

Understanding SMPTE ST 2110

The SMPTE ST 2110 standard is a key component of the IP transition, providing a framework for the carriage of video, audio, and metadata over IP networks. SMPTE ST 2110 is based on AES67 for audio and SDP (Session Description Protocol) for signaling, and is designed to provide a common interface for IP-based broadcast systems. By adopting SMPTE ST 2110, broadcasters can ensure interoperability between different vendors and systems, simplifying the integration of IP-based equipment and reducing costs.

Practical Considerations for IP Migration

For small and mid-market broadcasters, the transition to IP requires careful planning and consideration of several factors, including network infrastructure, equipment compatibility, and staffing. One of the biggest challenges is the need to upgrade existing SDI infrastructure to support IP, which can be a costly and time-consuming process. However, by adopting a hybrid approach that combines SDI and IP, broadcasters can transition to IP at their own pace, while minimizing disruption to their operations.

The IP transition is not just about replacing SDI with IP, it's about changing the way we think about broadcast infrastructure and operations. It's about creating a more flexible, scalable, and efficient system that can support a wide range of services and applications. - John Smith, Chief Engineer, ABC Broadcasting

NMOS and the Importance of Device Discovery

NMOS (Networked Media Open Specification) is an open standard that provides a framework for device discovery and registration in IP-based broadcast systems. NMOS allows devices to automatically discover and register with the network, simplifying the configuration and management of IP-based systems. By adopting NMOS, broadcasters can reduce the complexity and costs associated with device discovery and registration, and improve the overall efficiency of their operations.

Hybrid Approaches to IP Migration

For many broadcasters, a hybrid approach to IP migration is the most practical and cost-effective solution. By combining SDI and IP, broadcasters can transition to IP at their own pace, while minimizing disruption to their operations. Hybrid approaches can also provide a stepping stone to full IP, allowing broadcasters to test and refine their IP systems before making a complete transition. Some broadcasters are also using HLS (HTTP Live Streaming) and DASH (Dynamic Adaptive Streaming over HTTP) to deliver IP-based services, while others are adopting HEVC (High Efficiency Video Coding) and AV1 for video compression.

Transport Protocols for IP-Based Broadcast Systems

The choice of transport protocol is critical in IP-based broadcast systems, as it can affect the quality and reliability of video and audio signals. Some popular transport protocols for IP-based broadcast systems include SRT (Secure Reliable Transport) and RIST (Reliable Internet Stream Transport), which provide secure and reliable transport of video and audio signals over IP networks. Other protocols, such as NDI (Network Device Interface), are also gaining popularity, particularly in live production and streaming applications.

Best Practices for IP Migration

For broadcasters migrating to IP, several best practices can help ensure a smooth and successful transition. These include:

  • Conducting a thorough assessment of existing infrastructure and equipment to identify areas that require upgrade or replacement
  • Developing a detailed migration plan and timeline, with clear goals and objectives
  • Providing training and support for staff to ensure they are familiar with IP-based systems and protocols
  • Testing and validating IP-based systems and protocols to ensure they meet performance and quality requirements
  • Monitoring and analyzing IP-based systems to identify areas for improvement and optimize performance

Case Study: IP Migration at a Mid-Market Broadcaster

A mid-market broadcaster in the United States recently completed a successful IP migration, using a hybrid approach that combined SDI and IP. The broadcaster upgraded its existing SDI infrastructure to support IP, and adopted SMPTE ST 2110 and NMOS to ensure interoperability and device discovery. The broadcaster also used SRT and RIST for transport, and HEVC for video compression. The result was a more flexible, scalable, and efficient broadcast system that supported a wide range of services and applications, including live production, streaming, and video-on-demand.

Future Directions for IP-Based Broadcast Systems

As the broadcast industry continues to evolve, IP-based systems are expected to play an increasingly important role. Some future directions for IP-based broadcast systems include the adoption of artificial intelligence (AI) and machine learning (ML) for automated content creation and distribution, and the use of cloud-based services for scalable and on-demand processing and storage. The development of new standards and protocols, such as SMPTE ST 2120 for immersive audio, is also expected to drive innovation and growth in the broadcast industry.

The future of broadcast is IP, and it's not just about technology, it's about creating new opportunities for content creation, distribution, and monetization. As broadcasters, we need to be prepared to adapt and innovate, and to take advantage of the many benefits that IP has to offer. - Jane Doe, CEO, Broadcast Engineering Inc.
