The IP Transition in 2026: A Practical Engineering Guide for Broadcasters

The Streamic Editorial Team March 22, 2026 Analysis

Introduction to the IP Transition

The migration from traditional SDI (Serial Digital Interface) to IP (Internet Protocol) infrastructure is a significant undertaking for broadcasters, particularly for small and mid-market players. The SMPTE ST 2110 standard has become the cornerstone of this transition, offering a professional media over managed IP networks. However, the journey to IP is fraught with challenges, including the need for new skills, equipment, and infrastructure. This article provides a practical guide for broadcasters navigating this complex landscape.

Understanding SMPTE ST 2110 and NMOS

SMPTE ST 2110 is a suite of standards that enables the carriage of professional media over IP networks. It covers video, audio, and metadata, providing a framework for broadcasters to build IP-based systems. NMOS (Networked Media Open Specifications), on the other hand, is a suite of open specifications that enable the registration and discovery of IP devices on a network. NMOS is crucial for building a flexible and scalable IP infrastructure, as it allows devices from different manufacturers to interoperate seamlessly.

Hybrid Approaches to IP Migration

For many small and mid-market broadcasters, a full IP migration may not be feasible in the short term. Hybrid approaches, which combine SDI and IP infrastructure, are becoming increasingly popular. These approaches allow broadcasters to leverage their existing SDI equipment while gradually introducing IP components. Hybrid routers, for example, can switch both SDI and IP signals, providing a flexible and cost-effective solution for broadcasters transitioning to IP.

Audio Over IP and AES67

Audio over IP is a critical component of the IP transition. AES67 is an audio over IP standard that provides a common protocol for audio interoperability. It is widely adopted in the broadcast industry and is often used in conjunction with SMPTE ST 2110. AES67 enables broadcasters to transport audio signals over IP networks, providing a flexible and scalable solution for audio routing and distribution.

Video Over IP and Compression

Video over IP is another critical component of the IP transition. HEVC (High Efficiency Video Coding) and AV1 are two popular video compression standards used in broadcast applications. HEVC offers improved compression efficiency compared to H.264, while AV1 is an open-source alternative that is gaining traction in the industry. Both standards are supported by SMPTE ST 2110, providing broadcasters with a range of options for video compression and transport.

Streaming and OTT Delivery

Streaming and OTT (Over-the-Top) delivery are critical components of the broadcast workflow. HLS (HTTP Live Streaming) is a widely adopted protocol for streaming media, while SRT (Secure Reliable Transport) and RIST (Reliable Internet Stream Transport) are protocols designed for low-latency, secure streaming over the internet. NDI (Network Device Interface) is another popular protocol for streaming and IP production, offering a flexible and scalable solution for live production applications.

Practical Considerations for IP Migration

When migrating to an IP infrastructure, broadcasters must consider several practical factors, including network design, device compatibility, and staffing. A well-designed network is critical for ensuring reliable and efficient IP transport, while device compatibility is essential for ensuring seamless interoperability between different devices and systems. Staffing is also a critical consideration, as broadcasters must ensure that their teams have the necessary skills and training to manage and maintain IP systems.

As we move to IP, we're not just talking about replacing SDI with IP, we're talking about a fundamental change in the way we approach broadcast engineering. It's a shift from a traditional, hardware-based approach to a more software-based, virtualized approach. This requires a new set of skills and a new way of thinking about broadcast systems.

Best Practices for IP Migration

Several best practices can help broadcasters navigate the IP transition. These include starting with a clear understanding of the business case for IP migration, developing a comprehensive migration plan, and ensuring that all stakeholders are aligned and informed throughout the process. It's also essential to test and validate IP systems thoroughly before deploying them in production environments. By following these best practices, broadcasters can ensure a smooth and successful transition to IP infrastructure.

The Future of Broadcast Engineering

By 2030, the broadcast industry is expected to be fully IP-based, with SDI becoming increasingly obsolete. This will enable broadcasters to take advantage of the flexibility, scalability, and cost savings offered by IP infrastructure. The IP transition will also drive innovation in areas such as virtualization, cloud production, and artificial intelligence, enabling broadcasters to create new and innovative content experiences for their audiences.

Key Takeaways for Broadcasters

The IP transition is a complex and challenging process, but it also offers significant benefits and opportunities for broadcasters. By understanding the key technologies and standards involved, including SMPTE ST 2110, NMOS, and AES67, broadcasters can navigate the transition with confidence. It's also essential to consider practical factors such as network design, device compatibility, and staffing, and to follow best practices for IP migration. By doing so, broadcasters can ensure a successful transition to IP infrastructure and position themselves for success in a rapidly changing media landscape.

The broadcast industry is undergoing a significant transformation, driven by the transition to IP infrastructure. As broadcasters navigate this complex landscape, they must consider a range of technical, practical, and strategic factors. By understanding the key technologies and standards involved, and by following best practices for IP migration, broadcasters can ensure a successful transition to IP infrastructure and thrive in a rapidly changing media environment.
