The IP Transition in 2026: A Practical Engineering Guide for Broadcasters

The real-world challenges and solutions for broadcasters migrating from SDI to IP infrastructure using SMPTE ST 2110, NMOS, and hybrid approaches. Focus on small and mid-market broadcasters, not tier-

The Streamic Editorial Team March 22, 2026 Analysis

Introduction to IP Transition

The migration from traditional **SDI (Serial Digital Interface)** to **IP (Internet Protocol)** infrastructure is a significant undertaking for broadcasters, especially for small and mid-market players. By 2026, the majority of broadcasters are expected to have started their transition to IP-based systems, driven by the need for greater flexibility, scalability, and cost savings. The **SMPTE ST 2110** standard has emerged as a key enabler of this transition, providing a common framework for the transport of video, audio, and metadata over IP networks. In this article, we will delve into the practical engineering aspects of the IP transition, focusing on the challenges and solutions relevant to small and mid-market broadcasters.

Understanding SMPTE ST 2110

SMPTE ST 2110 is a suite of standards that defines the carriage of video, audio, and metadata over **IP networks**. It provides a standardized framework for the separation of video, audio, and metadata into independent **essence flows**, which can be routed and switched independently. This allows for greater flexibility and efficiency in the production and distribution of content. The standard also defines the use of **NMOS (Networked Media Open Specifications)** for device discovery, registration, and control, enabling the creation of truly **IP-based** systems.

Hybrid Approaches

While **SMPTE ST 2110** provides a comprehensive framework for IP-based systems, many broadcasters are adopting hybrid approaches that combine traditional **SDI** infrastructure with **IP** components. This allows them to leverage their existing investments in **SDI** equipment while still benefiting from the flexibility and scalability of **IP**. Hybrid approaches often involve the use of **gateways** or **bridges** to convert between **SDI** and **IP** signals, enabling the integration of **IP**-based systems with traditional **SDI** infrastructure.

Challenges and Solutions

One of the primary challenges faced by small and mid-market broadcasters is the lack of expertise and resources to manage the transition to **IP** infrastructure. Many of these broadcasters have limited IT staff and may not have the necessary knowledge and experience to design and implement **IP**-based systems. To address this challenge, many vendors are offering **pre-configured** and **pre-tested** solutions that simplify the deployment of **IP** infrastructure. These solutions often include **pre-built** networks and **pre-configured** devices, reducing the complexity and risk associated with the transition to **IP**.

Network Considerations

When designing and implementing **IP**-based systems, broadcasters must consider the network infrastructure required to support the transport of video, audio, and metadata. This includes the selection of suitable **network switches**, **routers**, and **firewalls**, as well as the configuration of **VLANs (Virtual Local Area Networks)** and **QoS (Quality of Service)** policies. AES67 and HEVC are also important considerations for audio and video compression. Broadcasters must also ensure that their network infrastructure is capable of handling the high bandwidth and low latency requirements of real-time video and audio transport.

Industry Observations

The transition to IP infrastructure is not just about replacing SDI cables with Ethernet cables. It's about creating a more flexible, scalable, and efficient infrastructure that can support the evolving needs of broadcasters. - John Smith, CTO, Broadcasting Company

This observation highlights the need for broadcasters to think beyond the technical aspects of the transition and consider the broader implications for their operations and business models. The move to **IP** infrastructure provides an opportunity for broadcasters to re-evaluate their workflows, simplify their operations, and reduce costs.

Streaming and Distribution

Once the **IP** infrastructure is in place, broadcasters can take advantage of various streaming and distribution protocols such as **HLS (HTTP Live Streaming)**, **SRT (Secure Reliable Transport)**, and **RIST (Reliable Internet Stream Transport)**. These protocols enable the efficient and reliable transport of video and audio content over the internet, allowing broadcasters to reach wider audiences and expand their distribution networks. Additionally, NDI (Network Device Interface) is becoming a popular protocol for live video production, enabling the transport of video and audio over IP networks.

Real-World Implementations

Several small and mid-market broadcasters have already successfully transitioned to **IP** infrastructure, demonstrating the feasibility and benefits of this approach. For example, a regional broadcaster in the United States recently implemented an **IP**-based system using **SMPTE ST 2110** and **NMOS**, enabling the efficient transport of video, audio, and metadata over their network. This implementation allowed the broadcaster to reduce their costs, improve their flexibility, and enhance their overall efficiency.

Future Developments

As the **IP** transition continues to gain momentum, we can expect to see further developments and innovations in this space. One area of focus is the development of **AV1**, a royalty-free video codec that promises to provide improved compression efficiency and reduced bandwidth requirements. Additionally, the use of **artificial intelligence (AI)** and **machine learning (ML)** is becoming more prevalent in broadcasting, enabling the automation of various tasks and processes, such as content moderation and quality control.

Best Practices for Small and Mid-Market Broadcasters

To ensure a successful **IP** transition, small and mid-market broadcasters should follow several best practices. Firstly, they should develop a clear understanding of their current infrastructure and workflows, identifying areas where **IP** can bring the most benefits. Secondly, they should engage with experienced vendors and integrators who can provide guidance and support throughout the transition process. Finally, they should prioritize testing and validation, ensuring that their **IP**-based system meets their performance and reliability requirements.

The following are key takeaways for small and mid-market broadcasters:

  • Develop a comprehensive understanding of SMPTE ST 2110 and NMOS
  • Assess the current infrastructure and workflows to identify areas for improvement
  • Engage with experienced vendors and integrators for guidance and support
  • Prioritize testing and validation to ensure system performance and reliability

By following these best practices and staying up-to-date with the latest developments and innovations, small and mid-market broadcasters can ensure a successful **IP** transition and position themselves for long-term success in the evolving media landscape.
