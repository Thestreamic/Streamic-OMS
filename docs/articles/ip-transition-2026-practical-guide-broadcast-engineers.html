The IP Transition in 2026: A Practical Engineering Guide for Broadcasters

The Streamic Editorial Team March 22, 2026 1,076 words · 5 min read Analysis

Introduction to IP Transition

The broadcast industry is undergoing a significant transformation as it migrates from traditional SDI (Serial Digital Interface) infrastructure to IP (Internet Protocol) based systems. This transition is driven by the need for greater flexibility, scalability, and cost-effectiveness in broadcast operations. By 2030, it is expected that most broadcasters will have completed their transition to IP, with SMPTE ST 2110 emerging as the standard of choice for professional media over IP networks. In this article, we will delve into the practical engineering aspects of the IP transition, focusing on the challenges and solutions for small and mid-market broadcasters.

Understanding SMPTE ST 2110

SMPTE ST 2110 is a suite of standards that defines the carriage of professional media over IP networks. It provides a framework for the transport of video, audio, and ancillary data over IP networks, enabling the creation of more flexible and scalable broadcast systems. SMPTE ST 2110 is based on AES67 for audio and SDP (Session Description Protocol) for signaling, ensuring interoperability between different vendors' equipment. As the industry adopts SMPTE ST 2110, broadcasters can expect to see significant improvements in their operations, including reduced cabling, increased flexibility, and improved signal quality.

Challenges in IP Transition

While the benefits of IP based systems are clear, the transition from SDI to IP is not without its challenges. One of the primary concerns for small and mid-market broadcasters is the cost of upgrading their existing infrastructure. The investment required to replace SDI equipment with IP capable devices can be significant, and many broadcasters may not have the budget to make this investment all at once. Additionally, there are also concerns about the complexity of IP networks and the need for specialized expertise to manage and maintain them.

Hybrid Approaches

To address these challenges, many broadcasters are adopting hybrid approaches that combine SDI and IP technologies. These hybrid systems allow broadcasters to continue using their existing SDI equipment while also introducing IP capable devices into their workflows. This approach enables broadcasters to transition to IP at their own pace, reducing the upfront cost and complexity of the transition.

The key to a successful hybrid approach is to ensure that the SDI and IP systems can interact seamlessly, which is where standards like NMOS (Networked Media Open Specification) come into play.
NMOS provides a standardized way of registering and discovering devices on the network, making it easier to manage hybrid systems and ensure interoperability between different vendors' equipment.

Real-World Solutions

Several real-world solutions are emerging to support the IP transition. For example, some vendors are offering IP to SDI gateways that enable broadcasters to connect their existing SDI equipment to IP networks. These gateways provide a flexible and cost-effective way to integrate SDI devices into IP workflows, allowing broadcasters to transition to IP at their own pace. Other vendors are offering IP based solutions for specific applications, such as live production or playout, which can be used to replace traditional SDI based systems.

Streaming and Distribution

In addition to the transition from SDI to IP in broadcast facilities, there is also a growing trend towards streaming and distribution over IP networks. This is driven by the increasing demand for online content and the need for broadcasters to reach audiences on multiple platforms. To support this trend, broadcasters are adopting standards like HLS (HTTP Live Streaming) and DASH (Dynamic Adaptive Streaming over HTTP) for streaming, and HEVC (High Efficiency Video Coding) and AV1 (AOMedia Video 1) for compression. These standards enable broadcasters to deliver high-quality video content over IP networks, while also ensuring that the content is protected and secure.

Security and Reliability

As broadcasters transition to IP based systems, security and reliability become increasingly important. IP networks are more vulnerable to cyber threats than traditional SDI systems, and broadcasters must take steps to protect their systems and content from unauthorized access. This includes implementing robust security measures, such as firewalls and encryption, and ensuring that all devices on the network are properly secured. Additionally, broadcasters must also ensure that their IP networks are reliable and can withstand failures or outages. This can be achieved through the use of redundant systems, backup power supplies, and SRT (Secure Reliable Transport) or RIST (Reliable Internet Stream Transport) protocols for streaming.

NDI and the Future of IP

Finally, it's worth noting the role of NDI (Network Device Interface) in the IP transition. NDI is a royalty-free protocol developed by NewTek that enables the transport of video and audio over IP networks. NDI is gaining popularity in the broadcast industry due to its ease of use and flexibility, and it is expected to play a significant role in the future of IP based systems. As the industry continues to evolve, we can expect to see more widespread adoption of NDI and other IP based protocols, enabling broadcasters to create more flexible, scalable, and cost-effective systems.

As small and mid-market broadcasters navigate the IP transition, they must carefully consider their options and develop a strategy that meets their specific needs and budget. By understanding the challenges and solutions outlined in this article, broadcasters can ensure a successful transition to IP and remain competitive in an increasingly complex and rapidly evolving media landscape. The future of broadcast engineering will be shaped by the adoption of IP based systems, and it is essential for broadcasters to be prepared to take advantage of the opportunities and challenges that this presents.

The adoption of IP based systems will also enable broadcasters to take advantage of new technologies and innovations, such as cloud based services, artificial intelligence, and machine learning. These technologies will enable broadcasters to create more efficient, automated, and personalized workflows, and to deliver more engaging and interactive content to their audiences. As the broadcast industry continues to evolve, it is clear that IP based systems will play a critical role in shaping the future of broadcast engineering.

In the coming years, we can expect to see significant advancements in IP based technologies, including the development of new standards and protocols, such as SMPTE ST 2110 and NMOS. These advancements will enable broadcasters to create more complex and sophisticated IP based systems, and to take advantage of new opportunities for innovation and growth. As the industry continues to evolve, it is essential for broadcasters to stay up-to-date with the latest developments and advancements in IP based technologies, and to be prepared to adapt and innovate in response to changing market conditions and consumer demands.
