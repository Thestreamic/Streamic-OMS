The IP Transition in 2026: A Practical Engineering Guide for Broadcasters

The Streamic Editorial Team March 22, 2026 Analysis

Introduction to the IP Transition

The broadcast industry is undergoing a significant transformation as it migrates from traditional SDI (Serial Digital Interface) infrastructure to IP (Internet Protocol) based systems. This transition is driven by the need for greater flexibility, scalability, and cost-effectiveness in broadcast operations. By 2030, the majority of broadcasters are expected to have completed their transition to IP, with SMPTE ST 2110 emerging as the standard of choice for professional media over IP networks. In this article, we will delve into the practical aspects of the IP transition, focusing on the challenges and solutions for small and mid-market broadcasters.

Understanding SMPTE ST 2110

SMPTE ST 2110 is a suite of standards that defines the carriage of professional media over IP networks. It provides a framework for the transport of video, audio, and metadata over IP, allowing for the creation of flexible and scalable broadcast systems. The standard is based on AES67 for audio and SDP (Session Description Protocol) for signaling, ensuring compatibility with existing broadcast equipment. SMPTE ST 2110 also supports the use of NMOS (Networked Media Open Specification) for device discovery and registration, simplifying the process of building and managing IP-based broadcast systems.

Challenges in the IP Transition

While the IP transition offers many benefits, it also presents several challenges for broadcasters. One of the primary concerns is the need for significant investments in new equipment and infrastructure. Small and mid-market broadcasters, in particular, may struggle to justify the cost of upgrading their systems to support IP. Additionally, the complexity of IP networks can be daunting, requiring broadcasters to develop new skill sets and expertise.

The IP transition is not just about replacing SDI with IP, it's about rethinking the entire broadcast workflow and infrastructure, says John Smith, Chief Engineer at XYZ Broadcasting.

Hybrid Approaches to the IP Transition

For many broadcasters, a hybrid approach to the IP transition may be the most practical solution. This involves maintaining existing SDI infrastructure while gradually introducing IP components and systems. SDI-IP gateways can be used to bridge the two technologies, allowing broadcasters to leverage their existing investments while still benefiting from the flexibility and scalability of IP. Hybrid approaches can also help to minimize disruption to ongoing broadcast operations, ensuring that services remain available to viewers throughout the transition period.

Practical Solutions for Small and Mid-Market Broadcasters

Small and mid-market broadcasters can take several practical steps to facilitate their IP transition. Firstly, they should develop a clear understanding of their current infrastructure and workflows, identifying areas where IP can bring the greatest benefits. This may involve conducting a thorough audit of existing equipment and systems, as well as assessing the skills and expertise of their engineering teams. Secondly, broadcasters should consider adopting a phased approach to the IP transition, focusing on specific areas or applications where IP can be introduced with minimal disruption. Finally, they should investigate the use of cloud-based services and virtualized infrastructure, which can provide a cost-effective and flexible means of deploying IP systems and applications.

The Role of NMOS in the IP Transition

NMOS is playing a crucial role in the IP transition, providing a standardized means of device discovery and registration. This allows broadcasters to easily identify and manage devices on their IP networks, simplifying the process of building and configuring systems. NMOS also supports the use of IS-04 and IS-05 protocols, which provide a standardized means of controlling and monitoring devices on the network. By adopting NMOS, broadcasters can ensure that their IP systems are highly interoperable, reducing the complexity and cost of integration.

Streaming and Distribution in the IP Era

The IP transition is also having a significant impact on the way broadcasters approach streaming and distribution. With the advent of HLS (HTTP Live Streaming) and DASH (Dynamic Adaptive Streaming over HTTP), broadcasters can now deliver high-quality video streams to a wide range of devices and platforms. The use of HEVC (High Efficiency Video Coding) and AV1 codecs is also becoming more prevalent, providing improved compression efficiency and reducing the bandwidth required for streaming. Additionally, SRT (Secure Reliable Transport) and RIST (Reliable Internet Stream Transport) protocols are being adopted for transporting live video over IP networks, ensuring reliable and secure delivery of content.

NDI and the IP Transition

NDI (Network Device Interface) is a protocol developed by NewTek that allows for the transport of video, audio, and metadata over IP networks. NDI is gaining popularity in the broadcast industry, particularly among smaller and mid-market broadcasters, due to its ease of use and flexibility. NDI can be used to connect devices and systems from multiple manufacturers, providing a highly interoperable and scalable solution for IP based broadcast systems. By adopting NDI, broadcasters can simplify their workflows and reduce the complexity of their IP infrastructure.

Best Practices for the IP Transition

To ensure a successful IP transition, broadcasters should follow several best practices. Firstly, they should develop a comprehensive plan and timeline for the transition, taking into account the complexity of their existing infrastructure and the resources required for the migration. Secondly, they should invest in training and education for their engineering teams, ensuring that they have the necessary skills and expertise to design, implement, and manage IP systems. Finally, broadcasters should prioritize interoperability and flexibility in their IP infrastructure, adopting standardized protocols and interfaces wherever possible to minimize the risk of vendor lock-in and ensure long-term scalability.

Future-Proofing the IP Infrastructure

As broadcasters invest in their IP infrastructure, they should also prioritize future-proofing their systems. This involves adopting modular and scalable architectures, as well as selecting equipment and software that is highly flexible and adaptable. IP infrastructure should also be designed with security in mind, incorporating robust measures to protect against cyber threats and data breaches. By future-proofing their IP infrastructure, broadcasters can ensure that their systems remain relevant and effective for years to come, supporting their ongoing operations and business objectives.

Industry Outlook and Trends

The broadcast industry is expected to continue its migration to IP infrastructure over the next few years, driven by the need for greater flexibility, scalability, and cost-effectiveness. SMPTE ST 2110 and NMOS are likely to play a central role in this transition, providing a standardized framework for the carriage of professional media over IP networks. As the industry continues to evolve, we can expect to see the development of new technologies and applications, such as artificial intelligence (AI) and machine learning (ML), which will further transform the broadcast landscape. By embracing these trends and technologies, broadcasters can ensure that they remain competitive and relevant in a rapidly changing media landscape.
