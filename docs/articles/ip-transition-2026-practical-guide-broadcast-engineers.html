The IP Transition in 2026: A Practical Engineering Guide for Broadcasters

The migration from traditional SDI (Serial Digital Interface) to IP (Internet Protocol) infrastructure is well underway in the broadcast industry. By 2026, small and mid-market broadcasters will need to have made significant progress in this transition to remain competitive. The SMPTE ST 2110 standard has emerged as the cornerstone of IP-based broadcast infrastructure, offering a flexible and scalable framework for the transport of video, audio, and metadata over IP networks. However, the journey to IP is not without its challenges, particularly for smaller broadcasters with limited resources and expertise.

One of the primary concerns for small and mid-market broadcasters is the complexity of IP networking. Unlike SDI, which is a relatively straightforward, point-to-point technology, IP requires a deep understanding of network architecture, protocols, and traffic management. This can be daunting for broadcasters who have traditionally focused on video and audio engineering rather than IT (Information Technology). To address this challenge, many broadcasters are turning to hybrid approaches that combine the benefits of IP with the familiarity of SDI.

Hybrid Approaches to IP Migration

Hybrid approaches to IP migration involve the use of SDI-to-IP gateways and converters to enable the integration of SDI equipment with IP-based systems. This allows broadcasters to gradually transition their infrastructure to IP while still supporting their existing SDI-based workflows. NMOS (Networked Media Open Specification) is another key technology that is being used to facilitate the transition to IP. NMOS provides a standardized framework for the discovery, registration, and control of IP-based devices, making it easier for broadcasters to manage their networks and ensure interoperability between different devices.

According to

the recent survey by the Society of Motion Picture and Television Engineers (SMPTE), the majority of broadcasters are planning to use a hybrid approach to IP migration, with 70% of respondents indicating that they will use a combination of SDI and IP technologies in their facilities.
This approach allows broadcasters to take advantage of the flexibility and scalability of IP while minimizing disruption to their existing workflows.

Technical Considerations for IP Migration

When migrating to an IP-based infrastructure, there are several technical considerations that broadcasters must take into account. One of the most critical is the choice of IP transport protocol. SRT (Secure Reliable Transport) and RIST (Reliable Internet Stream Transport) are two popular protocols that offer reliable and secure transport of video and audio over IP networks. Another important consideration is the choice of video codec, with HEVC (High Efficiency Video Coding) and AV1 (AOMedia Video 1) being two of the most widely used codecs for IP-based video transport.

In addition to these technical considerations, broadcasters must also think about the overall architecture of their IP-based infrastructure. This includes the design of the network, the choice of switches and routers, and the implementation of traffic management and quality of service (QoS) policies. AES67 (Audio over IP) is another important standard that is being used to transport audio over IP networks, providing a standardized framework for the transport of audio signals.

Best Practices for IP Migration

To ensure a successful IP migration, broadcasters should follow several best practices. First, they should conduct a thorough assessment of their existing infrastructure and workflows to identify areas where IP can bring the most benefit. They should also develop a detailed plan for the migration, including timelines, budgets, and resource allocation. Additionally, broadcasters should consider the use of HLS (HTTP Live Streaming) and other streaming protocols to transport video and audio over IP networks, particularly for OTT (over-the-top) and online streaming applications.

Another important best practice is to ensure that all IP-based devices are properly configured and registered with the NMOS controller. This includes the use of NDI (Network Device Interface) and other IP-based protocols to enable the discovery and registration of devices on the network. By following these best practices, broadcasters can ensure a smooth and successful transition to an IP-based infrastructure.

As the broadcast industry continues to evolve, it is clear that IP will play an increasingly important role in the production, processing, and distribution of video and audio content. By understanding the technical considerations and best practices for IP migration, small and mid-market broadcasters can ensure that they are well-positioned to take advantage of the many benefits that IP has to offer.

The use of IP in broadcast infrastructure is not limited to video and audio transport. It also enables the use of cloud-based services and virtualized workflows, which can bring significant benefits in terms of flexibility, scalability, and cost savings. As broadcasters continue to migrate to IP-based infrastructure, they will need to consider how to integrate these cloud-based services and virtualized workflows into their operations.

Future-Proofing Broadcast Infrastructure

As the broadcast industry continues to evolve, it is essential that broadcasters future-proof their infrastructure to ensure that they can continue to compete and thrive in a rapidly changing landscape. This means investing in IP-based technologies and workflows that can adapt to changing requirements and opportunities. It also means staying up-to-date with the latest developments in IP standards and protocols, such as SMPTE ST 2110 and NMOS.

According to industry experts,

the key to future-proofing broadcast infrastructure is to focus on flexibility and scalability, rather than trying to predict exactly what the future will hold.
By investing in IP-based technologies and workflows, broadcasters can ensure that they have the flexibility and scalability to adapt to changing requirements and opportunities, whether that involves the adoption of new video codecs, the integration of cloud-based services, or the development of new business models.

Ultimately, the transition to IP-based infrastructure is a complex and challenging process, but it also offers many benefits and opportunities for broadcasters. By understanding the technical considerations and best practices for IP migration, and by investing in flexible and scalable IP-based technologies and workflows, small and mid-market broadcasters can ensure that they are well-positioned to thrive in a rapidly changing landscape.

In the years to come, we can expect to see significant advancements in IP-based broadcast technologies, including the development of new IP standards and protocols, the introduction of new video codecs and streaming protocols, and the increased use of cloud-based services and virtualized workflows. By staying at the forefront of these developments, broadcasters can ensure that they remain competitive and continue to deliver high-quality content to their audiences.
