The IP Transition in 2026: A Practical Engineering Guide for Broadcasters

The Streamic Editorial Team March 22, 2026 1,148 words · 6 min read Analysis

Introduction to the IP Transition

The migration from traditional SDI (Serial Digital Interface) to IP (Internet Protocol) infrastructure is a significant undertaking for broadcasters, particularly for small and mid-market players. As the industry continues to evolve, the need for flexible, scalable, and cost-effective solutions has become paramount. The SMPTE ST 2110 standard has emerged as a cornerstone for this transition, offering a framework for the carriage of video, audio, and ancillary data over IP networks. However, the journey to a fully IP-based infrastructure is complex, with numerous considerations for broadcasters aiming to balance immediate needs with future-proofing their operations.

Understanding SMPTE ST 2110 and NMOS

SMPTE ST 2110 is a suite of standards that defines the transport of video, audio, and ancillary data over IP networks. This standard is crucial for the broadcast industry as it ensures that different vendors' equipment can interoperate, thereby facilitating a smooth transition to IP. Alongside SMPTE ST 2110, NMOS (Networked Media Open Specification) plays a vital role in the IP transition by providing a mechanism for the registration and discovery of IP streams and devices on the network. NMOS is particularly useful for managing the complexity of IP networks and ensuring that all components can communicate effectively.

Real-World Challenges for Broadcasters

One of the primary challenges faced by small and mid-market broadcasters is the cost associated with migrating to an IP-based infrastructure. The investment in new equipment, training for staff, and potential downtime during the transition can be daunting. Furthermore, these broadcasters often have limited IT expertise in-house, which can make the management of IP networks and troubleshooting issues more complicated.

As noted by industry experts, "The transition to IP is not just about replacing SDI cables with Ethernet; it's about changing the way we think about broadcast infrastructure and the skill sets required to manage it."
This change in mindset and the acquisition of new skills are critical for the successful implementation of IP-based systems.

Hybrid Approaches to the IP Transition

Given the challenges associated with a full-scale migration to IP, many broadcasters are adopting hybrid approaches. These involve integrating IP and SDI systems to create a hybrid infrastructure that can support both IP and SDI signals. This strategy allows broadcasters to transition at their own pace, using gateways and converters to facilitate communication between IP and SDI domains. For example, AES67 can be used for audio over IP, while SDI continues to be used for video transport. This hybrid model helps in minimizing disruption to ongoing operations and allows for a more gradual transition to a fully IP-based infrastructure.

Technologies Facilitating the IP Transition

Beyond SMPTE ST 2110 and NMOS, several other technologies are playing significant roles in the IP transition. For instance, HLS (HTTP Live Streaming) and HEVC (High Efficiency Video Coding), as well as the emerging AV1 codec, are being used for video encoding and streaming over IP networks. Additionally, SRT (Secure Reliable Transport) and RIST (Reliable Internet Stream Transport) are being utilized for the secure and reliable transport of video streams over the internet. NDI (Network Device Interface) is another technology that has gained popularity for its ability to encode, transmit, and receive video and audio over IP networks, making it easier to integrate different devices and systems within a broadcast environment.

Best Practices for Small and Mid-Market Broadcasters

For small and mid-market broadcasters, adopting best practices during the IP transition is crucial. This includes conducting thorough network assessments to ensure that the existing infrastructure can support the demands of IP-based broadcasting. Moreover, investing in staff training is essential to develop the necessary IT skills for managing and troubleshooting IP networks. It's also important to develop a phased transition plan, allowing for the gradual introduction of IP technologies alongside existing SDI systems. Collaboration with industry peers and seeking guidance from experienced system integrators can also provide valuable insights and help in avoiding common pitfalls during the transition process.

Future Outlook: 2030 and Beyond

Looking ahead to 2030 and beyond, the broadcast industry is expected to become even more IP-centric. The use of cloud services for broadcast operations will become more prevalent, with an increased focus on remote production and virtualization. The adoption of 5G networks will also play a significant role in the broadcast industry, offering the potential for low-latency and high-bandwidth connectivity. As broadcasters continue to navigate the IP transition, they must remain adaptable and open to embracing new technologies and workflows that will shape the future of the industry. By doing so, they can position themselves for success in a rapidly changing media landscape.

Preparation is Key

Preparing for the future involves not only investing in the right technologies but also in the people and processes that will drive the transition to IP-based broadcasting. Broadcasters must be proactive in seeking out solutions that meet their specific needs, whether through the adoption of SMPTE ST 2110, NMOS, and other relevant standards, or by leveraging hybrid approaches to manage the transition. The journey to an IP-based infrastructure is complex, but with careful planning, a focus on staff development, and a commitment to embracing change, small and mid-market broadcasters can navigate this transition successfully and thrive in the years to come.

Industry Collaboration and Standards

The success of the IP transition also depends on the collaboration among industry players. The development and adoption of open standards like SMPTE ST 2110 and NMOS are testaments to the power of industry collaboration. As new technologies emerge, continued collaboration will be essential to ensure interoperability and to address the evolving needs of broadcasters. Organizations such as the Society of Motion Picture and Television Engineers (SMPTE) and the Internet Engineering Task Force (IETF) play critical roles in facilitating this collaboration and in driving the development of standards that support the IP transition.

Training and Education

Finally, the importance of training and education cannot be overstated. As the broadcast industry becomes increasingly IP-centric, the need for professionals with IT skills and knowledge of IP technologies will continue to grow. Broadcasters must invest in the development of their staff, providing them with the training and resources needed to manage and maintain IP-based systems. This includes not only technical training but also education on the business and operational aspects of IP-based broadcasting. By prioritizing training and education, broadcasters can ensure that they have the skilled workforce necessary to navigate the challenges and opportunities of the IP transition.

As the broadcast industry moves forward with the IP transition, it's clear that this journey will be marked by both challenges and opportunities. By understanding the technologies, strategies, and best practices outlined here, small and mid-market broadcasters can better navigate this transition and position themselves for success in the evolving media landscape. The path to a fully IP-based infrastructure may be complex, but with the right approach, broadcasters can harness the power of IP to enhance their operations, improve their services, and thrive in the years to come.
