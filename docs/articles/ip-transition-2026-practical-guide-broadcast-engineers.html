The IP Transition in 2026: A Practical Engineering Guide for Broadcasters

The Streamic Editorial Team March 22, 2026 1,129 words · 6 min read Analysis

Introduction to IP Transition

The broadcast industry is undergoing a significant transformation as it migrates from traditional SDI (Serial Digital Interface) to IP (Internet Protocol) infrastructure. This transition is driven by the need for greater flexibility, scalability, and cost-effectiveness in broadcast operations. By 2030, it is expected that most broadcasters will have completed their transition to IP, with SMPTE ST 2110 emerging as the de facto standard for professional media over IP networks. In this article, we will delve into the practical aspects of the IP transition, focusing on the challenges and solutions for small and mid-market broadcasters.

Understanding SMPTE ST 2110

SMPTE ST 2110 is a suite of standards that defines the carriage of professional media over IP networks. It provides a framework for the transport of video, audio, and metadata over IP, enabling broadcasters to build flexible and scalable media networks. The standard consists of several parts, including SMPTE ST 2110-10 for video, SMPTE ST 2110-30 for audio, and SMPTE ST 2110-40 for metadata. By adopting SMPTE ST 2110, broadcasters can take advantage of the benefits of IP infrastructure, including reduced costs, increased flexibility, and improved scalability.

Challenges in IP Transition

The transition from SDI to IP infrastructure is not without its challenges. One of the main hurdles is the need for significant investments in new equipment and infrastructure. Many small and mid-market broadcasters may not have the resources to upgrade their entire infrastructure at once, making a phased approach to the transition necessary. Additionally, the use of IP infrastructure requires a different set of skills and expertise than traditional SDI systems, which can be a challenge for broadcasters with limited technical resources.

Hybrid Approaches

To mitigate the challenges associated with a full IP transition, many broadcasters are adopting hybrid approaches that combine SDI and IP infrastructure. These hybrid systems allow broadcasters to continue using their existing SDI equipment while also taking advantage of the benefits of IP infrastructure. For example, a broadcaster may use SDI for their existing production equipment while using IP for their distribution and playout systems. This approach enables broadcasters to transition to IP at their own pace, reducing the upfront costs and minimizing disruptions to their operations.

NMOS and Device Discovery

Another important aspect of the IP transition is device discovery and registration. The NMOS (Networked Media Open Specifications) initiative provides a set of open standards for device discovery and registration, making it easier for broadcasters to manage their IP infrastructure. NMOS provides a framework for devices to register themselves on the network, allowing for automatic discovery and configuration of IP devices. This simplifies the process of setting up and managing IP infrastructure, reducing the complexity and costs associated with device discovery and registration.

The key to a successful IP transition is a well-planned and phased approach. Broadcasters need to carefully evaluate their current infrastructure and operations, identifying areas where IP can bring the most benefits. By adopting a hybrid approach and leveraging open standards like SMPTE ST 2110 and NMOS, broadcasters can ensure a smooth transition to IP, minimizing disruptions to their operations and reducing costs.

AES67 and Audio over IP

Audio over IP is another critical aspect of the IP transition. The AES67 standard provides a framework for transporting audio over IP networks, enabling broadcasters to take advantage of the benefits of IP infrastructure for their audio operations. AES67 provides a high-quality, low-latency audio transport mechanism, making it suitable for a wide range of broadcast applications. By adopting AES67, broadcasters can simplify their audio infrastructure, reducing the complexity and costs associated with traditional audio routing and distribution systems.

Streaming and Distribution

The IP transition also has significant implications for streaming and distribution. The use of IP infrastructure enables broadcasters to take advantage of new streaming protocols like HLS (HTTP Live Streaming) and SRT (Secure Reliable Transport). These protocols provide a robust and scalable mechanism for delivering live and on-demand content over the internet, enabling broadcasters to reach a wider audience and expand their revenue streams. Additionally, the use of IP infrastructure enables broadcasters to take advantage of new compression standards like HEVC (High Efficiency Video Coding) and AV1, which provide improved video quality and reduced bandwidth requirements.

RIST and Reliable Streaming

The RIST (Reliable Internet Stream Transport) protocol is another important development in the area of streaming and distribution. RIST provides a robust and reliable mechanism for transporting live and on-demand content over the internet, enabling broadcasters to deliver high-quality video and audio streams to their audiences. RIST is designed to work in conjunction with other streaming protocols like SRT and HLS, providing a flexible and scalable solution for broadcasters looking to distribute their content over the internet.

NDI and the Future of IP Infrastructure

The NDI (Network Device Interface) protocol is a rapidly emerging standard for IP infrastructure. NDI provides a flexible and scalable mechanism for transporting video and audio over IP networks, enabling broadcasters to build high-performance IP infrastructure. NDI is designed to work in conjunction with other IP protocols like SMPTE ST 2110 and NMOS, providing a comprehensive solution for broadcasters looking to transition to IP infrastructure. As the broadcast industry continues to evolve, it is likely that NDI will play an increasingly important role in the development of IP infrastructure.

Best Practices for IP Transition

To ensure a successful IP transition, broadcasters should follow a number of best practices. These include carefully evaluating their current infrastructure and operations, identifying areas where IP can bring the most benefits, and developing a phased approach to the transition. Broadcasters should also prioritize the use of open standards like SMPTE ST 2110 and NMOS, which provide a flexible and scalable framework for building IP infrastructure. Additionally, broadcasters should consider the use of hybrid approaches, which can help to reduce the upfront costs and minimize disruptions to their operations.

By following these best practices and adopting a well-planned and phased approach to the IP transition, broadcasters can ensure a smooth and successful transition to IP infrastructure. This will enable them to take advantage of the many benefits of IP infrastructure, including reduced costs, increased flexibility, and improved scalability, and to remain competitive in an increasingly complex and rapidly evolving media landscape.

The future of broadcast engineering will be shaped by the IP transition, with SMPTE ST 2110, NMOS, and other open standards playing a critical role in the development of IP infrastructure. As the broadcast industry continues to evolve, it is likely that we will see new and innovative applications of IP technology, enabling broadcasters to deliver high-quality content to their audiences in new and innovative ways. By understanding the challenges and opportunities associated with the IP transition, broadcasters can ensure that they are well-positioned to take advantage of these developments and to remain at the forefront of the broadcast industry.
