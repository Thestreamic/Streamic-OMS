The IP Transition in 2026: A Practical Engineering Guide for Broadcasters

The Streamic Editorial Team March 22, 2026 Analysis

Introduction to the IP Transition

The migration from traditional SDI (Serial Digital Interface) to IP (Internet Protocol) infrastructure is a significant undertaking for broadcasters, particularly for small and mid-market organizations. As the industry continues to evolve, the need for flexible, scalable, and cost-effective solutions has become paramount. The SMPTE ST 2110 standard has emerged as a cornerstone for IP-based broadcast infrastructure, offering a pathway for broadcasters to transition from legacy SDI systems. However, the journey is not without its challenges, and broadcasters must carefully consider their approach to ensure a successful transition.

Understanding SMPTE ST 2110

SMPTE ST 2110 is a suite of standards that defines the carriage of video, audio, and metadata over IP networks. This standard provides a framework for broadcasters to build IP-based infrastructure, enabling the efficient transport of media content over IP networks. The standard consists of several parts, including SMPTE ST 2110-10 for video, SMPTE ST 2110-30 for audio, and SMPTE ST 2110-40 for metadata. By adopting SMPTE ST 2110, broadcasters can take advantage of the flexibility and scalability of IP infrastructure, while also ensuring interoperability with existing SDI systems.

Challenges in the IP Transition

One of the primary challenges broadcasters face when transitioning to IP infrastructure is the need for significant changes to their existing workflows and systems. This can include the replacement of SDI routers, switches, and other equipment, as well as the retraining of staff to work with new IP-based systems. Additionally, broadcasters must also consider the need for quality of service (QoS) and redundancy in their IP networks, to ensure reliable and uninterrupted transmission of media content. According to

John Mailhot, CTO at Imagine Communications, "The biggest challenge for broadcasters is not the technology itself, but rather the cultural shift required to adopt IP-based workflows and the need for IT-like skills in the broadcast engineering team."

Hybrid Approaches to the IP Transition

For many small and mid-market broadcasters, a complete transition to IP infrastructure may not be feasible in the short term. In these cases, a hybrid approach can provide a practical solution, allowing broadcasters to maintain their existing SDI infrastructure while also introducing IP-based systems. This can be achieved through the use of SDI-IP gateways, which enable the conversion of SDI signals to IP and vice versa. Additionally, broadcasters can also leverage NMOS (Networked Media Open Specifications) to enable the discovery and registration of IP-based devices on their network, simplifying the process of building and managing hybrid SDI-IP systems.

Audio Over IP

The transition to IP infrastructure also requires broadcasters to reconsider their approach to audio transport. AES67 (Audio over IP) has emerged as a widely adopted standard for audio over IP, providing a common protocol for the transport of audio signals over IP networks. By adopting AES67, broadcasters can ensure interoperability with a wide range of audio equipment and systems, while also taking advantage of the flexibility and scalability of IP infrastructure. Additionally, broadcasters can also leverage other audio over IP protocols, such as Dante and Ravenna, to build robust and reliable audio transport systems.

Streaming and Distribution

The IP transition also has significant implications for streaming and distribution workflows. HLS (HTTP Live Streaming) and DASH (Dynamic Adaptive Streaming over HTTP) have become widely adopted protocols for streaming media content over IP networks. By leveraging these protocols, broadcasters can deliver high-quality, adaptive bitrate streaming to a wide range of devices and platforms. Additionally, broadcasters can also utilize HEVC (High Efficiency Video Coding) and AV1 (AOMedia Video 1) codecs to reduce bandwidth requirements and improve streaming efficiency. Furthermore, the use of SRT (Secure Reliable Transport) and RIST (Reliable Internet Stream Transport) protocols can provide secure and reliable transport of streaming media content over IP networks.

NDI and the Future of IP Infrastructure

NDI (Network Device Interface) is a protocol developed by NewTek that enables the transport of video, audio, and metadata over IP networks. NDI has gained widespread adoption in the broadcast industry, particularly among small and mid-market broadcasters, due to its ease of use and flexibility. According to

Dr. Andrew Cross, President of Research and Development at NewTek, "NDI is not just a protocol, but a platform that enables the creation of IP-based workflows and the integration of multiple devices and systems."
As the broadcast industry continues to evolve, it is likely that NDI will play an increasingly important role in the development of IP infrastructure, particularly in the areas of live production and streaming.

Best Practices for the IP Transition

To ensure a successful IP transition, broadcasters should follow several best practices. First, they should conduct a thorough assessment of their existing infrastructure and workflows to identify areas that require upgrading or replacement. Second, they should develop a comprehensive plan for the transition, including timelines, budgets, and resource allocation. Third, they should prioritize the training and education of their staff to ensure that they have the necessary skills to work with IP-based systems. Finally, they should consider the need for quality of service (QoS) and redundancy in their IP networks, to ensure reliable and uninterrupted transmission of media content. By following these best practices, broadcasters can minimize the risks associated with the IP transition and ensure a smooth and successful migration to IP infrastructure.

The Future of Broadcast Engineering

By 2030, the broadcast industry is likely to be entirely IP-based, with SDI infrastructure relegated to legacy systems. The transition to IP will have significant implications for broadcast engineering, requiring engineers to develop new skills and expertise in areas such as networking, cybersecurity, and software development. Additionally, the use of cloud-based services and artificial intelligence (AI) will become increasingly prevalent, enabling broadcasters to automate workflows, improve efficiency, and enhance the viewer experience. As the broadcast industry continues to evolve, it is essential that engineers and broadcasters stay ahead of the curve, embracing new technologies and innovations to remain competitive and relevant in a rapidly changing media landscape.
