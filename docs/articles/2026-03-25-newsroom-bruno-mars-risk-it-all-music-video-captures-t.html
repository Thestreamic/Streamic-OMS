Bruno Mars’ “Risk It All” Music Video Captures Timeless Texture with Pro8mm

The Streamic Editorial Team March 25, 2026 867 words · 4 min read

Time-Sensitive Signal Transport Takes Center Stage in Live Production

As live production continues to push the boundaries of what's possible, the importance of real-time signal transport and latency has never been more critical. The recent collaboration between Pro8mm and Bruno Mars on his "Risk It All" music video is a prime example of how timeless texture can be captured with the right equipment and expertise. For broadcast engineers, this development means that the focus on live signal transport and latency is about to get even more intense. With the rise of remote and distributed production, the need for reliable and low-latency signal transport has become a major priority.

Domain Signal Extraction

  • Broadcast technologies: ST 2110, NDI, SRT, Genlock, 12G-SDI, JPEG XS, AES67, and RIST are all relevant to this discussion, as they relate to live signal transport and latency. The use of these technologies can significantly impact the quality and reliability of live productions.
  • Architecture layer: This development lands squarely in the signal transport and processing stage of the media chain, where the focus is on getting high-quality, low-latency signals from the camera to the production switcher and ultimately to the viewer.
  • Cybersecurity surface: No direct security vector is present in this specific development, but as live productions become more distributed and reliant on IP-based signal transport, the potential for security risks increases.
  • Business signal: The underlying commercial pressure driving this development is the need for high-quality, low-latency live productions that can compete with the best in the industry. This is particularly important for sports and entertainment events, where every millisecond counts.

Technology Intelligence

The use of Pro8mm's equipment and expertise in the production of Bruno Mars' music video demonstrates the importance of high-quality, low-latency signal transport in live production. This is particularly relevant in the context of ST 2110, which provides a standardized framework for the transport of professional media over IP networks. The ability to maintain Genlock and ensure frame-accurate signal transport is critical in live production, and technologies like SRT and RIST are well-suited to meet this challenge. As the industry continues to evolve, we can expect to see even more emphasis on low-latency signal transport and the use of technologies like JPEG XS to reduce bandwidth requirements. In practice, this means that broadcast engineers will need to be increasingly familiar with IP-based signal transport and the various technologies that support it.

Why This Matters

  • Broadcasters: The impact of this development on broadcasters will be significant, particularly in terms of reduced transmission costs and lower latency than traditional satellite or fiber-based signal transport. Regional and national broadcasters will feel this most acutely, as they seek to maintain high-quality live productions while reducing costs and improving efficiency.
  • Streaming platforms: For OTT operators, the impact will be felt in terms of ABR ladder decisions and origin infrastructure. The ability to maintain low latency and high-quality signal transport will be critical in delivering a high-quality viewing experience to users.
  • Cybersecurity risk: While there is no direct security vector present in this development, the increasing use of IP-based signal transport in live production does introduce some security risks. These can be mitigated through the use of secure protocols like SRT and RIST, as well as careful network design and management.
  • Market impact: The market impact of this development will be significant, with vendors like Pro8mm and others that specialize in live production equipment and expertise likely to benefit. Standards bodies like SMPTE will also play a critical role in shaping the future of live signal transport and latency.

Key Technologies in Context

  • ST 2110: This standard provides a critical framework for the transport of professional media over IP networks, enabling the low-latency and high-quality signal transport required in live production.
  • SRT: This protocol is well-suited to meet the challenges of low-latency signal transport, particularly in distributed and remote production environments.
  • Genlock: The ability to maintain Genlock is critical in live production, ensuring that all equipment is synchronized and that frame-accurate signal transport is maintained.

Hidden Implications

  • The integration of IP-based signal transport with existing SDI-based infrastructure will be a significant challenge for many broadcasters, requiring careful planning and execution to ensure a smooth transition.
  • The increasing use of IP-based signal transport in live production will also drive the adoption of new technologies like NMOS, which provides a standardized framework for the discovery and registration of devices on IP networks.

Editorial Perspective

For engineering teams, the real challenge is not just about adopting new technologies, but also about ensuring that they can be integrated with existing infrastructure and workflows. The trade-off here is between the benefits of low-latency signal transport and the potential risks and complexities associated with IP-based signal transport. What gets missed in coverage like this is the importance of careful planning and execution in ensuring a successful transition to IP-based signal transport. In practice, this means that broadcast engineers will need to be increasingly familiar with IP-based signal transport and the various technologies that support it, as well as the potential risks and challenges associated with it.
