TAG Video Systems and Oracle Cloud Infrastructure Partner to Bring Broadcast Monitoring to OCI

Why This Matters Right Now

For broadcast engineers and operations leads, the pressure to monitor and analyze broadcast feeds in real-time is intensifying. As media companies move towards more complex, IP-based infrastructures like SMPTE ST 2110, the need for flexible and scalable monitoring solutions has grown. Traditional on-premise monitoring systems can be expensive, complex, and difficult to scale, which is why the partnership between TAG Video Systems and Oracle Cloud Infrastructure is significant. By bringing broadcast monitoring to the cloud, TAG's solutions can be easily integrated with other cloud-based broadcast systems, such as playout, ingest, and media asset management (MAM) systems. In practice, this means that broadcast teams can now monitor their feeds in real-time, without the need for expensive and complex on-premise infrastructure. This is particularly important for live production and PCR workflows, where real-time monitoring is critical to ensuring the quality and reliability of the broadcast. With cloud-based monitoring, teams can also take advantage of the scalability and flexibility of the cloud to handle large volumes of data and complex workflows. For CTOs, the partnership between TAG and Oracle Cloud Infrastructure represents a key opportunity to simplify and streamline broadcast workflows. By leveraging the cloud's scalability and flexibility, media companies can reduce the complexity and cost associated with traditional on-premise monitoring systems, and focus on content creation and delivery rather than infrastructure management. This is particularly important in today's fast-paced media landscape, where the ability to quickly and easily deploy new services and applications is critical to staying competitive.

Under the Hood: How It Actually Works

The partnership between TAG Video Systems and Oracle Cloud Infrastructure allows TAG's monitoring solutions to be hosted on Oracle Cloud Infrastructure (OCI), providing a cloud-based platform for media companies to monitor their broadcast feeds. This is made possible by the use of cloud-based infrastructure, such as virtual machines, containers, and microservices, which enable the deployment of scalable and flexible monitoring systems. In terms of the signal path, the monitoring solution sits between the ingest and playout stages, allowing for real-time analysis and quality control of the broadcast feed. For example, in a live production workflow, the signal path might look like this: live feed → TAG monitoring solution → Oracle Cloud Infrastructure → playout. This allows for real-time monitoring and analysis of the feed, enabling broadcast teams to quickly identify and resolve any issues that may arise. The use of cloud-based infrastructure also enables the integration of TAG's monitoring solutions with other cloud-based broadcast systems, such as Avid MediaCentral or Vizrt graphics, allowing for a more streamlined and automated workflow. In terms of data flow, the monitoring solution can ingest data from a variety of sources, including live feeds, VOD content, and social media platforms. This data is then processed and analyzed in real-time, using machine learning and artificial intelligence algorithms to identify trends and patterns. The output of this analysis can then be used to inform decision-making and optimize the broadcast workflow. For example, the output might be used to trigger alerts and notifications, or to automate the deployment of new services and applications. The use of cloud-based infrastructure also enables the deployment of containerized or microservice-based workflows, which can be easily scaled and integrated with existing broadcast systems. This is particularly important for media companies that are looking to deploy new services and applications quickly and easily, without the need for complex and expensive infrastructure deployments. For example, a media company might use Docker containers to deploy a cloud-based monitoring solution, which can then be easily integrated with other cloud-based broadcast systems, such as AWS Media Services or Google Cloud Media.

What This Looks Like in a Real Broadcast Operation

In a real broadcast operation, the partnership between TAG Video Systems and Oracle Cloud Infrastructure might look like this: a media company is producing a live sports event, and needs to monitor the feed in real-time to ensure that it is of high quality and meets the required standards. The company uses TAG's monitoring solution, hosted on Oracle Cloud Infrastructure, to ingest the live feed and analyze it in real-time. The solution is integrated with other cloud-based broadcast systems, such as Avid MediaCentral and Vizrt graphics, allowing for a streamlined and automated workflow. The monitoring solution is used to trigger alerts and notifications, which are sent to the production team in real-time. This enables the team to quickly identify and resolve any issues that may arise, ensuring that the broadcast is of high quality and meets the required standards. The use of cloud-based infrastructure also enables the company to easily scale the monitoring solution, as needed, to handle large volumes of data and complex workflows. In a newsroom environment, the monitoring solution might be used to analyze social media feeds and identify trends and patterns. This information can then be used to inform decision-making and optimize the broadcast workflow. For example, the solution might be used to trigger alerts and notifications when a particular topic or hashtag is trending, allowing the production team to quickly respond and adjust the broadcast accordingly.

The Practical Impact: Cost, Speed, and Scale

The partnership between TAG Video Systems and Oracle Cloud Infrastructure has significant implications for the cost, speed, and scale of broadcast monitoring. By leveraging the cloud's scalability and flexibility, media companies can reduce the complexity and cost associated with traditional on-premise monitoring systems, and focus on content creation and delivery rather than infrastructure management. This can result in significant cost savings, as well as improved speed and agility in deploying new services and applications. In terms of speed, the use of cloud-based infrastructure enables media companies to quickly and easily deploy new services and applications, without the need for complex and expensive infrastructure deployments. This can result in faster time-to-market, as well as improved responsiveness to changing market conditions. For example, a media company might use the cloud to quickly deploy a new monitoring solution, which can then be used to analyze and optimize the broadcast workflow in real-time. The use of cloud-based infrastructure also enables media companies to scale their monitoring solutions, as needed, to handle large volumes of data and complex workflows. This can result in improved reliability and uptime, as well as reduced risk of outages and downtime. For example, a media company might use the cloud to scale its monitoring solution to handle a large sports event, which requires real-time analysis and optimization of the broadcast feed.

Media companies must carefully evaluate these risks and consider the potential implications for their broadcast workflows.
