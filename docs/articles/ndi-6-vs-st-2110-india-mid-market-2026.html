NDI 6 vs ST 2110 for India's Mid-Market Broadcasters: The Pragmatic Read

ST 2110 is the standard. NDI 6 is what most Indian regional broadcasters will actually deploy. The honest engineering comparison — latency, compression, network cost, and where each earns its place.

The Streamic Editorial Team March 15, 2026 436 words · 2 min read Analysis

The IP video transport debate in India's broadcast market is increasingly binary: SMPTE ST 2110 for organisations with the capital and engineering capacity to deploy it correctly, and NDI for everyone else. The honest answer is more nuanced than either camp admits.

The ST 2110 case

ST 2110 is uncompressed, standards-compliant, and interoperable across vendors who have implemented the suite correctly. For facilities handling premium content — national news, sports rights-holders, major entertainment broadcasters — the uncompressed signal chain and the clear standards lineage are non-negotiable. The engineering investment is significant: dedicated network infrastructure, PTP timing, NMOS deployment, and staff with IP networking skills alongside broadcast knowledge. The operating cost is justified by the signal quality and interoperability it delivers.

The NDI 6 case

NDI 6 delivers visually lossless compressed video over standard IP networks — the same switched Ethernet infrastructure that carries data traffic. For regional broadcasters, state-level channels, and production facilities that cannot justify dedicated media network infrastructure, NDI removes the capital barrier to IP-based production. The compression is a trade-off: perceptually transparent at normal viewing distances, but not mathematically lossless, and not suitable for multi-generation use cases where quality accumulates across encoding stages.

NDI 6's bandwidth reduction over NDI 5 makes it viable on 1GbE networks that are standard in most mid-market facilities. A single HD channel over NDI 6 requires approximately 100–150 Mbps, compared to 1.5 Gbps for uncompressed ST 2110. For a facility with twenty video signals, this is the difference between a standard data network upgrade and a dedicated 25GbE media fabric.

Latency comparison

ST 2110 with a correctly configured receiver buffer delivers sub-frame latency — typically one to two frames on a well-engineered network. NDI 6 introduces additional latency from the compression and decompression stages: typically three to six frames in normal operating conditions. For most production workflows, this difference is imperceptible and operationally irrelevant. For workflows that require tight talent monitoring, IFB systems, or confidence monitoring with sub-frame accuracy, ST 2110's latency advantage matters.

Where each standard earns its place

The pragmatic answer for India's mid-market is not a binary choice. ST 2110 is appropriate for the signal chain between critical production infrastructure: vision mixers, master control, playout, and archive ingest. NDI is appropriate for contribution from remote locations, for inter-building connectivity where laying dedicated fibre is not cost-effective, and for production environments where flexibility and ease of deployment matter more than absolute signal quality.

The engineering teams that are most effective in this market are those that understand both standards well enough to deploy each where it genuinely fits, rather than treating either as a universal answer.
