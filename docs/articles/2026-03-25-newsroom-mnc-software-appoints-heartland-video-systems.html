Orchestration Platforms Gain Traction: What Broadcasters Need to Know About Scalability and Cost

As the broadcast industry continues to shift towards more efficient and scalable workflows, the appointment of a new reseller for an orchestration platform is a significant development. This move indicates a growing demand for solutions that can streamline media workflows, reduce operational overhead, and improve content delivery. With the rise of cloud infrastructure and object storage, broadcasters are looking for ways to optimize their workflows and minimize egress costs. The ability to automate workflows and integrate with existing systems, such as MAM and API orchestration, is becoming increasingly important.

  • Broadcast technologies: The development of orchestration platforms touches on several broadcast technologies, including microservices, Kubernetes, and cloud-based workflows. Adjacent technologies affected include content delivery networks (CDNs), streaming protocols such as HLS and DASH, and digital rights management (DRM) systems.
  • Architecture layer: This development lands in the workflow automation and orchestration layer, specifically in the areas of ingest, processing, and delivery. It also has implications for the integration of cloud-based services, such as object storage and egress cost management.
  • Cybersecurity surface: The cybersecurity surface of this development is relatively low, as it primarily involves the automation of workflows and integration with existing systems. However, the use of microservices and cloud-based infrastructure does introduce some security considerations, such as the need for secure API orchestration and access control.
  • Business signal: The underlying commercial pressure driving this development is the need for broadcasters to reduce costs, improve efficiency, and increase scalability. The industry is responding to the growing demand for online content and the need to deliver high-quality experiences to viewers across multiple platforms.
