On Air 2025: A Global Broadcast Powered by Students and Vizrt

Technical Analysis: Vizrt's IP-Based Live Graphics Solution Reduces Latency and Increases Signal Integrity

Vizrt, a leading provider of graphics and live video production systems, has announced its participation in the On Air 2025 project, a global broadcast powered by students and Vizrt's IP-based live graphics solution. This project involves the use of Vizrt's graphics systems, which are designed to work with the SMPTE ST 2110 standard for professional media over managed IP networks. The solution also incorporates the NMOS (Networked Media Open Specification) protocol for device discovery and registration.

Operational ROI

The use of Vizrt's IP-based live graphics solution in the On Air 2025 project is expected to save time and money for broadcast CTOs by reducing transmission costs and latency. With the ability to transport signals over IP, the need for traditional SDI (Serial Digital Interface) infrastructure is reduced, resulting in lower costs and a smaller hardware rack footprint. This solution also simplifies signal routing, as signals can be easily switched and routed using IP-based protocols such as SRT (Secure Reliable Transport) and RIST (Reliable Internet Stream Transport). The workflow step most affected by this technology is the transmission of live graphics and video signals from the studio to the broadcast center.

In comparison to traditional SDI-based systems, Vizrt's IP-based solution offers lower latency and increased signal integrity. Traditional SDI systems often require multiple stages of signal conversion and processing, which can introduce latency and degrade signal quality. In contrast, IP-based systems can transport signals in their native format, reducing the need for conversion and processing. This results in a more efficient and reliable signal transport system, with latency as low as 1-2 milliseconds. Additionally, the use of IP-based protocols such as JPEG XS (JPEG Extended Range) for compression and AES67 (Audio over IP) for audio transport further reduces latency and improves signal quality.

Security & Integration

Vizrt's IP-based live graphics solution is designed to integrate seamlessly with existing broadcast IT stacks, using standards such as ST 2110 and NMOS to ensure compatibility and security. The solution also incorporates cybersecurity measures such as encryption and secure authentication to protect against unauthorized access and data breaches. The use of IP-based protocols such as SRT and RIST also provides an additional layer of security, as these protocols are designed to ensure reliable and secure transport of signals over IP networks. Additionally, the solution is compliant with relevant standards and regulations, including SMPTE ST 2110-20 and NMOS IS-04.

Engineering Takeaways

  • Deployment: The deployment of Vizrt's IP-based live graphics solution requires a 10GbE or 25GbE network infrastructure, with a minimum of 2-3 milliseconds of latency. The solution also requires a compatible graphics system, such as Vizrt's Viz Engine, and a supported IP-based protocol such as SRT or RIST.
  • Compatibility: The solution is compliant with SMPTE ST 2110-20 and NMOS IS-04, ensuring compatibility with other IP-based systems and devices. The solution also supports JPEG XS compression and AES67 audio transport, ensuring compatibility with a wide range of IP-based devices and systems.
  • What to Watch: The upcoming update to the SMPTE ST 2110 standard, which is expected to include new features and improvements for IP-based signal transport, is an important development for engineers to track. This update is expected to further improve the efficiency and reliability of IP-based signal transport systems, and may introduce new features and capabilities for live graphics and video production.

The use of Vizrt's IP-based live graphics solution in the On Air 2025 project signals a significant shift towards IP-based signal transport in the broadcast industry. Engineering teams should begin to investigate and plan for the deployment of IP-based systems, including the evaluation of compatible devices and systems, and the development of strategies for ensuring security and integration with existing IT stacks.

Analysis: On Air 2025 utilizes ST 2110 for IP-based video transport, enabling flexible, scalable broadcast workflows with reduced latency and optimized bitrate allocation, facilitated by NMOS protocol for device discovery and registration.
