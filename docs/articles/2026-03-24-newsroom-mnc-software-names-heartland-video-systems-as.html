MNC Software Names Heartland Video Systems As North American Reseller

MNC Software Names Heartland Video Systems As North American Reseller

The Streamic Editorial Team March 24, 2026 466 words · 2 min read

Why Real-Time Signal Transport Is Forcing Broadcasters to Rethink Their Infrastructure

As broadcasters continue to navigate the complexities of live signal transport, a critical inflection point has been reached. The need for reduced latency and improved signal integrity has become a pressing concern, driving the adoption of new technologies and architectures. With the rise of IP-based signal transport, broadcasters are being forced to reassess their infrastructure and make strategic decisions about the best approach for their operations.

What This Development Actually Means

The recent announcement of a new partnership between MNC Software and Heartland Video Systems marks a significant development in the live signal transport space. This partnership brings together MNC's expertise in real-time signal processing with Heartland's experience in broadcast systems integration, enabling the delivery of high-quality, low-latency signals over IP networks. The solution is based on the ST 2110 standard, which provides a framework for the transport of professional media over managed IP networks.

This development is part of a broader trend in the industry, as broadcasters seek to leverage the benefits of IP-based signal transport, including reduced costs, increased flexibility, and improved signal quality. With the growing demand for live content and the need for faster signal processing, the adoption of ST 2110 and other IP-based standards is becoming increasingly widespread.

Why It Matters for Broadcast Operations

The implications of this development are far-reaching, with significant impacts on broadcast operations. For engineers, the decision to adopt IP-based signal transport will require careful consideration of factors such as network architecture, signal processing, and system integration. Vendors like Matrox, with their ConvertIP gateway, and Riedel, with their MediorNet solution, are already providing innovative solutions for IP-based signal transport. Meanwhile, companies like Evertz, with their Mediator-X platform, are developing comprehensive solutions for live production and playout. As the industry continues to evolve, broadcasters will need to navigate the complex landscape of IP-based signal transport, weighing the benefits of reduced latency and improved signal quality against the potential risks and challenges of integration and deployment.

Impact by Stakeholder

  • Regional and national broadcasters: Will need to budget for the deployment of IP-based signal transport solutions, which will enable them to reduce transmission costs and improve signal quality. They can eliminate the need for traditional satellite or fiber-based transmission methods, simplifying signal routing and reducing hardware rack footprint.
  • OTT and streaming platforms: Will need to reassess their content delivery strategies, as the adoption of IP-based signal transport will enable them to reduce latency and improve signal quality. This will have a direct impact on their ABR ladder, CDN cost per GB, and SSAI complexity, allowing them to optimize their delivery workflows and improve the overall viewer experience.
