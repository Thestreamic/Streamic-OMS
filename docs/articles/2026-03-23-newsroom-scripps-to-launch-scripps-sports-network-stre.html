Technical Analysis: Scripps Sports Network Streaming Channel to Leverage Low-Latency JPEG XS for Real-Time Transport

Scripps recently announced the launch of Scripps Sports Network, a new streaming channel that will utilize the JPEG XS compression standard for real-time transport of live signals. This decision is significant, as it marks one of the first major deployments of JPEG XS in a live broadcast environment. The technology is expected to provide a number of benefits, including reduced latency and improved signal integrity.

Operational ROI

The use of JPEG XS compression is expected to save Scripps significant amounts of money in transmission costs, as it will allow for the transport of high-quality video signals over existing network infrastructure. Specifically, the reduced bitrate requirements of JPEG XS will enable Scripps to transmit multiple channels of video over a single 12G-SDI link, reducing the need for costly fiber optic upgrades. This, in turn, will simplify signal routing and reduce the hardware rack footprint required for signal transport.

In comparison to traditional uncompressed video transport, JPEG XS offers a number of operational advantages. For example, it eliminates the need for costly and complex signal processing equipment, such as those required for SMPTE ST 2110-20 uncompressed video transport. Additionally, JPEG XS is designed to work seamlessly with existing broadcast equipment, including those that support the NMOS IS-04 protocol for device discovery and registration.

Security & Integration

From a security perspective, the use of JPEG XS compression is expected to have a positive impact on the overall security posture of the Scripps Sports Network. This is because JPEG XS is designed to work with existing security protocols, such as AES67 for audio transport and RIST for reliable internet streaming. Additionally, the use of JPEG XS will enable Scripps to take advantage of the security features built into the NMOS IS-05 protocol for secure device authentication and authorization. In terms of integration, JPEG XS is designed to work with a wide range of broadcast equipment, including those that support the SRT protocol for secure and reliable video transport.

Engineering Takeaways

  • Deployment: To deploy JPEG XS compression, Scripps will need to ensure that its network infrastructure can support the required bitrate and latency requirements. This may involve upgrading existing network equipment, such as switches and routers, to support the higher bandwidth requirements of JPEG XS.
  • Compatibility: The JPEG XS compression standard is designed to be compatible with a wide range of broadcast equipment, including those that support the SMPTE ST 2110-22 standard for compressed video transport. Additionally, JPEG XS is designed to work with existing security protocols, such as AES67 and RIST.
  • What to Watch: One area to watch in the coming months is the development of new standards and protocols for JPEG XS compression, such as the upcoming SMPTE ST 2110-23 standard for JPEG XS transport over IP networks. This standard is expected to provide a framework for the transport of JPEG XS compressed video over IP networks, and will likely have a significant impact on the broadcast industry.

The launch of the Scripps Sports Network streaming channel is a significant development for the broadcast industry, as it marks one of the first major deployments of JPEG XS compression for real-time transport of live signals. As the industry continues to evolve, it is likely that we will see wider adoption of JPEG XS compression, particularly in applications where low-latency and high-quality video transport are critical. Engineering teams should closely track the development of new standards and protocols for JPEG XS compression, and begin planning for the deployment of this technology in their own networks.
