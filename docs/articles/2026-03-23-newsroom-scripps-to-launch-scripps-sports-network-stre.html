Scripps To Launch Scripps Sports Network Streaming Channel

Scripps To Launch Scripps Sports Network Streaming Channel

The Streamic Editorial Team March 23, 2026 634 words · 3 min read Analysis
Source Attribution This analysis is based on publicly available reporting from: TV Technology (March 2026).
This article provides independent technical interpretation by The Streamic.

The Quick Hit

Scripps is launching a new sports streaming channel that uses JPEG XS (think of it like a super-efficient ZIP file for live video) to cut latency and costs without needing a full network overhaul. ---

What Happened

Scripps announced the launch of **Scripps Sports Network**, a streaming channel that will use **JPEG XS** compression for real-time transport of live signals [Source 1]. This marks one of the first major deployments of JPEG XS in a live broadcast environment, aiming to reduce latency and improve signal integrity [Source 1]. The move is expected to save Scripps money by allowing high-quality video transport over existing network infrastructure. Specifically, JPEG XS’s reduced bitrate requirements enable multiple video channels to run over a single **12G-SDI link** (think of it like a high-speed highway for video signals), eliminating the need for costly fiber optic upgrades [Source 1]. The technology also simplifies signal routing and reduces hardware rack space [Source 1]. ---

The Tech Spec

Scripps Sports Network will rely on **JPEG XS**, a lightweight compression standard designed for low-latency live video transport. Unlike traditional uncompressed video (e.g., **SMPTE ST 2110-20** (think of it like sending raw, uncompressed video files over IP)), JPEG XS reduces bandwidth needs while maintaining quality, making it ideal for live sports workflows [Source 1]. The system will integrate with existing broadcast protocols, including: - **NMOS IS-04** (think of it like a phonebook for broadcast devices, letting them discover and register with each other) for device discovery [Source 1]. - **NMOS IS-05** (think of it like a secure handshake between devices) for authentication and authorization [Source 1]. - **AES67** (think of it like a universal translator for audio streams) for audio transport [Source 1]. - **RIST** (think of it like a safety net for internet streaming, ensuring packets arrive intact) for reliable internet streaming [Source 1]. - **SRT** (think of it like a secure tunnel for video over the public internet) for secure video transport [Source 1]. For deployment, Scripps will need to ensure its network infrastructure supports JPEG XS’s bitrate and latency requirements, which may involve upgrading switches and routers [Source 1]. The system is also designed to be compatible with **SMPTE ST 2110-22** (think of it like a rulebook for sending compressed video over IP), a standard for compressed video transport [Source 1]. ---

So What? (The Human Impact)

For broadcast engineers and operations teams, this deployment signals a shift toward more efficient live workflows. JPEG XS reduces the need for expensive fiber upgrades and complex signal processing hardware, lowering both capital and operational costs [Source 1]. The ability to run multiple channels over a single 12G-SDI link also simplifies infrastructure, freeing up rack space and reducing power consumption. For CTOs, the move highlights the growing viability of compressed IP transport for live sports. If successful, Scripps’ deployment could accelerate industry adoption of JPEG XS, particularly for applications where low latency is critical [Source 1]. Teams should start evaluating their network readiness for JPEG XS and monitor the upcoming **SMPTE ST 2110-23** standard (think of it like a new traffic rule for JPEG XS over IP), which will further standardize its use [Source 1]. ---

Blind Spots

  • The source doesn’t specify which cloud providers or CDNs Scripps will use for distribution.
  • The source doesn’t detail pricing or licensing costs for JPEG XS implementation.
  • The source doesn’t clarify whether existing Scripps infrastructure (e.g., encoders, switches) will require firmware updates or hardware replacements to support JPEG XS.
  • The source doesn’t name specific vendors supplying JPEG XS-compatible equipment for this deployment.
  • The source doesn’t state whether Scripps will offer the channel in 4K or HDR, or if JPEG XS will be used for those formats.
