Technical Analysis: Utah Scientific's Hybrid SDI-IP Portfolio Reduces Latency and Increases Signal Integrity

Utah Scientific has announced its plans to showcase its hybrid SDI-IP portfolio at the 2026 NAB Show, featuring a range of products that combine the benefits of traditional SDI infrastructure with the flexibility of IP-based signal transport. This move is expected to appeal to broadcast engineers and CTOs looking to migrate their facilities to IP while still supporting existing SDI equipment. The portfolio includes products that support ST 2110, NMOS, and other relevant standards for live signal transport and latency management.

Operational ROI

The hybrid SDI-IP portfolio from Utah Scientific offers significant operational savings for broadcast CTOs, primarily through reduced transmission costs and lower latency compared to traditional satellite or fiber-based signal transport methods. By leveraging IP-based transport, broadcasters can take advantage of lower-cost infrastructure and reduced hardware rack footprint, resulting in significant cost savings. This is particularly relevant in the context of live event production, where signal latency can have a direct impact on the quality of the broadcast. For example, a reduction in latency of just 10ms can significantly improve the viewer experience, especially in applications such as live sports or news broadcasts.

In comparison to existing approaches, the hybrid SDI-IP portfolio simplifies signal routing and reduces the complexity of signal distribution, allowing for more efficient use of resources and personnel. This is particularly important in large-scale live productions, where signal routing and distribution can be a major challenge. By streamlining these processes, broadcasters can reduce the risk of signal loss or degradation, resulting in a more reliable and high-quality broadcast. Additionally, the use of IP-based transport enables more flexible and scalable signal distribution, making it easier to adapt to changing production requirements.

Security & Integration

The hybrid SDI-IP portfolio from Utah Scientific is designed to integrate seamlessly with existing broadcast IT stacks, supporting relevant standards such as ST 2110, NMOS, and AES67. This ensures that the products can be easily incorporated into existing workflows, minimizing disruption and ensuring compliance with relevant security and regulatory requirements. In particular, the use of NMOS IS-04 and IS-05 protocols enables secure and reliable device registration and connection management, while support for AES67 ensures interoperability with other audio-over-IP devices. The portfolio also supports JPEG XS compression, enabling efficient and high-quality video transport over IP networks.

Engineering Takeaways

  • Deployment: The hybrid SDI-IP portfolio from Utah Scientific requires a minimum of 10GbE network infrastructure to support IP-based signal transport, and is compatible with a range of hardware and software platforms, including those from major broadcast equipment manufacturers.
  • Compatibility: The portfolio is compliant with SMPTE ST 2110-20 and NMOS IS-04 and IS-05 protocols, ensuring seamless integration with other IP-based broadcast equipment and systems.
  • What to Watch: Engineers should track the development of new standards and protocols, such as the upcoming updates to ST 2110, which are expected to further enhance the efficiency and reliability of IP-based signal transport in live broadcast applications.

The introduction of Utah Scientific's hybrid SDI-IP portfolio is expected to have a significant impact on the broadcast market, as it provides a flexible and scalable solution for migrating to IP-based signal transport while still supporting existing SDI equipment. Engineering teams should carefully evaluate the portfolio's capabilities and consider how it can be integrated into their existing workflows to improve signal integrity and reduce latency.

Analysis: Utah Scientific's hybrid SDI-IP portfolio supports ST 2110, enabling efficient IP-based workflows with reduced latency and increased bitrate flexibility, suitable for broadcast and streaming applications.
