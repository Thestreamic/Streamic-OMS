The Streamic Editorial Team March 23, 2026

Technical Analysis: Vizrt's JPEG XS Implementation Reduces Latency in Live Graphics Transport

Vizrt recently announced its adoption of JPEG XS for live graphics transport, allowing for reduced latency and improved signal integrity in broadcast environments. This development is significant for broadcast CTOs and infrastructure engineers, as it enables more efficient and cost-effective live production workflows. The implementation of JPEG XS is particularly notable for its ability to compress and transport high-quality video signals over standard IP networks.

Operational ROI

The use of JPEG XS in live graphics transport can significantly reduce transmission costs and latency compared to traditional methods. By leveraging JPEG XS, broadcast engineers can achieve latency as low as 1-2 milliseconds, which is substantially lower than satellite or fibre-based transmission methods. This reduction in latency can be particularly beneficial in live production environments where real-time graphics and video signals are critical. The simplified signal routing and reduced hardware rack footprint resulting from JPEG XS implementation can also lead to significant operational cost savings.

In comparison to existing approaches, the adoption of JPEG XS enables a more streamlined workflow, eliminating the need for cumbersome and expensive hardware-based compression solutions. This shift towards software-based compression and transport can also facilitate more flexible and scalable live production architectures, allowing broadcast engineers to easily adapt to changing production requirements. Furthermore, the reduced latency and improved signal integrity afforded by JPEG XS can enhance the overall quality and reliability of live graphics transport, resulting in a better viewing experience for audiences.

Security & Integration

The integration of JPEG XS into live graphics transport workflows must be carefully considered in terms of security and compliance with relevant broadcast standards. Vizrt's implementation of JPEG XS is designed to be compatible with existing IP-based broadcast infrastructures, including those based on ST 2110 and NMOS. The use of JPEG XS also aligns with the requirements of AES67 for audio over IP, ensuring seamless integration with existing audio transport systems. From a security perspective, the implementation of JPEG XS must be carefully evaluated to ensure that it does not introduce any vulnerabilities or risks to the broadcast network, particularly in terms of data encryption and access control.

Engineering Takeaways

  • Deployment: To deploy JPEG XS for live graphics transport, broadcast engineers will require a minimum of 1GbE network infrastructure, with 10GbE or higher recommended for high-bandwidth applications. Additionally, compatible hardware or software encoders and decoders will be necessary to support JPEG XS compression and transport.
  • Compatibility: Vizrt's JPEG XS implementation is designed to be compliant with SMPTE ST 2110-22, ensuring seamless integration with existing IP-based broadcast systems. Compatibility with NMOS IS-04 and IS-05 is also essential for discovery and registration of JPEG XS streams in live production environments.
  • What to Watch: Broadcast engineers should track the upcoming updates to the JPEG XS standard, which are expected to include new features and improvements for live graphics transport, such as enhanced compression ratios and reduced latency. Additionally, the development of new hardware and software solutions supporting JPEG XS will be crucial in driving adoption and innovation in live production workflows.

The adoption of JPEG XS for live graphics transport marks a significant development in the broadcast industry, enabling more efficient and cost-effective live production workflows. As the technology continues to evolve, engineering teams should prioritize the evaluation and implementation of JPEG XS-compatible solutions to stay ahead of the curve and capitalize on the benefits of reduced latency and improved signal integrity.

Analysis: Implementing ST 2110 for IP-based video transport can reduce cabling complexity in budget-friendly studio builds, allowing for more flexible and scalable workflows with NMOS control.
