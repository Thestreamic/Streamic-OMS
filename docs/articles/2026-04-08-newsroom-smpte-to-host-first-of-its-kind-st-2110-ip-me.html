Why This Matters Right Now

The broadcast industry is in the midst of a significant transition from traditional SDI-based infrastructures to IP-based architectures. This shift is driven by the need for greater flexibility, scalability, and cost-effectiveness in media production and distribution. As a result, broadcast engineers, operations leads, and CTOs are under pressure to navigate this complex transition while minimizing disruption to existing workflows and operations. The SMPTE ST 2110 standard has emerged as a key enabler of this transition, providing a framework for the transport of video, audio, and metadata over IP networks. However, the practical implementation and operational aspects of ST 2110 remain a significant challenge for many organizations. The move to IP-based infrastructures is not just a technical issue, but also a strategic one. As the industry continues to evolve, broadcast and media organizations must be able to deliver high-quality content across multiple platforms, including traditional linear TV, online streaming services, and social media platforms. The ability to efficiently and effectively manage and distribute content across these platforms is critical to remaining competitive. The SMPTE ST 2110 standard is a key part of this equation, providing a standardized framework for the transport of professional media over managed IP networks. The pressure to adopt IP-based infrastructures is also driven by the need for greater flexibility and scalability in live production environments. As the demand for live content continues to grow, broadcast and media organizations must be able to quickly and easily deploy new services and applications without being constrained by traditional SDI-based infrastructures. Under the Hood: How It Actually Works

The SMPTE ST 2110 standard represents a fundamental shift in how professional media is transported over IP networks. At its core, ST 2110 provides a framework for the transport of video, audio, and metadata over managed IP networks, using common off-the-shelf (COTS) IT equipment. This approach enables significant cost savings and easier integration with other IP-based systems. The standard also facilitates more efficient workflows, especially in live production environments, by allowing for the separation of video, audio, and metadata essence, which can be routed and processed independently. The SMPTE ST 2110 standard is designed to work in conjunction with other IP-based standards, such as AES67 for audio over IP. The standard provides a framework for the transport of professional media over IP networks, from ingest to playout, and is designed to be flexible and scalable. The use of COTS IT equipment and IP-based networks enables the creation of more efficient and cost-effective workflows, which can be easily integrated with other IP-based systems.
