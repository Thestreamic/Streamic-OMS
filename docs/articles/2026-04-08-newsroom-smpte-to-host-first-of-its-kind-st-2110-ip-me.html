SMPTE to Host First-of-Its-Kind ST 2110 IP Media Roadshow at 2026 NAB Show

What Was Announced

The Society of Motion Picture and Television Engineers (SMPTE) has announced plans to host a first-of-its-kind ST 2110 IP Media Roadshow at the 2026 NAB Show, focusing on the implementation and operational aspects of the SMPTE ST 2110 standard for professional media over managed IP networks. This event is significant as it underscores the industry's ongoing transition from traditional SDI-based infrastructures to IP-based architectures, with ST 2110 emerging as a key standard for the transport of video, audio, and metadata over IP networks. By hosting this roadshow, SMPTE aims to provide a comprehensive overview of the standard, its applications, and the best practices for its implementation, which is crucial for broadcast and media professionals looking to leverage IP technology for their operations.

The Engineering and Operational Significance

The SMPTE ST 2110 standard represents a fundamental shift in how professional media is transported over IP networks, offering advantages such as greater flexibility, scalability, and reduced cabling compared to traditional SDI infrastructures. From an engineering perspective, the adoption of ST 2110 enables the use of common off-the-shelf (COTS) IT equipment, which can lead to significant cost savings and easier integration with other IP-based systems. Operationally, ST 2110 facilitates more efficient workflows, especially in live production environments, by allowing for the separation of video, audio, and metadata essence, which can be routed and processed independently. This separation enables more flexible and efficient use of resources, such as easier addition of multilingual audio tracks or the insertion of targeted advertisements, thereby enhancing the overall viewing experience.

Industry Context

The move towards IP-based broadcast infrastructures, as exemplified by the SMPTE ST 2110 standard, is part of a broader industry trend towards greater flexibility, interoperability, and cost-effectiveness in media production and distribution. This trend is driven by the increasing demand for multi-platform delivery, where content needs to be prepared and delivered to various outlets, including traditional linear TV, online streaming services, and social media platforms. Other standards and technologies, such as AES67 for audio over IP and the ongoing development of specifications for carrying HDR and UHD signals over IP, are also playing critical roles in this transition. The SMPTE ST 2110 IP Media Roadshow comes at a time when the industry is seeking practical guidance on how to navigate this transition, ensuring that the benefits of IP technology are realized while minimizing disruption to existing workflows and operations.

Implications for Broadcast and Media Teams

For broadcast and media teams, the SMPTE ST 2110 IP Media Roadshow presents an opportunity to gain hands-on experience and deeper understanding of the standard and its implications for their operations. Engineers and operations managers should be particularly interested in how ST 2110 can be integrated into existing workflows, the potential for interoperability with other IP-based systems, and the strategies for managing and monitoring IP-based media networks. Moreover, the roadshow's focus on practical implementation and operational aspects of ST 2110 will provide valuable insights into how to address the challenges associated with the transition to IP, such as ensuring reliable and secure transmission, managing latency, and maintaining high-quality video and audio. By attending the roadshow and engaging with the SMPTE community, professionals can better position their organizations to leverage the benefits of IP technology, enhance their operational efficiency, and improve their ability to deliver high-quality content across multiple platforms.
