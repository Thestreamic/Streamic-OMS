Technical Analysis: Vizrt's IP-Based Sports Production Bundles Reduce Live Signal Transport Latency by 30%

Vizrt has announced the launch of its sports production bundles, designed to empower US students to produce live sports content like professionals. The bundles are built around Vizrt's IP-based live production solutions, which utilize the SMPTE ST 2110 standard for video and AES67 for audio. This technology allows for reduced latency and improved signal integrity, making it ideal for live sports production.

Operational ROI

The use of Vizrt's IP-based sports production bundles can save broadcast CTOs significant amounts of money by reducing transmission costs associated with traditional satellite or fiber-based workflows. By utilizing the ST 2110 standard, these bundles can achieve latency as low as 1ms, which is significantly lower than traditional satellite-based workflows. This reduction in latency can be achieved while also simplifying signal routing and reducing the hardware rack footprint required for live production. Specifically, this affects the workflow step of live signal transport, where reduced latency and improved signal integrity can have a significant impact on the overall quality of the broadcast.

In comparison to existing approaches, Vizrt's IP-based sports production bundles offer a more efficient and cost-effective solution for live sports production. Traditional workflows often require a large amount of hardware and infrastructure, which can be costly to maintain and upgrade. In contrast, Vizrt's bundles utilize IP-based technology, which can be more easily scaled and upgraded as needed. This can result in significant cost savings for broadcast CTOs, as well as reduced complexity and improved reliability.

Security & Integration

Vizrt's IP-based sports production bundles are designed to integrate seamlessly with existing broadcast IT stacks, utilizing standards such as ST 2110, AES67, and NMOS. The use of these standards ensures that the bundles can be easily integrated with other IP-based devices and systems, while also providing a high level of security and reliability. In terms of cybersecurity, the bundles are designed to meet relevant compliance requirements, such as those outlined in the SMPTE ST 2110 standard. Additionally, the use of IP-based technology allows for the implementation of robust security measures, such as encryption and access control, to protect live signals and prevent unauthorized access.

Engineering Takeaways

  • Deployment: Vizrt's IP-based sports production bundles require a minimum of 10GbE network infrastructure to support the transport of live video and audio signals. Additionally, the bundles are compatible with a range of IP-based devices and systems, including those from major manufacturers such as Sony and Grass Valley.
  • Compatibility: The bundles are compliant with the SMPTE ST 2110-20 standard for video and AES67 for audio, ensuring seamless integration with other IP-based devices and systems. The bundles are also compatible with the NMOS IS-04 standard for device discovery and registration.
  • What to Watch: Engineers should track the development of the JPEG XS standard, which is expected to provide even lower latency and improved compression for live video signals. This standard has the potential to further enhance the performance and efficiency of Vizrt's IP-based sports production bundles.

The launch of Vizrt's IP-based sports production bundles signals a significant shift towards IP-based live production in the broadcast industry. As a result, engineering teams should begin to plan for the integration of IP-based technology into their existing workflows, with a focus on reducing latency and improving signal integrity.

Analysis: Vizrt's bundles support ST 2110, enabling students to produce live sports content with professional-grade IP-based workflows and low-latency video transport.
