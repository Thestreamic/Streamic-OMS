Globecast Reimagines Managed Media Services for a Hybrid, Cloud-Driven Future at NAB 2026

Globecast Reimagines Managed Media Services for a Hybrid, Cloud-Driven Future at NAB 2026

The Streamic Editorial Team March 27, 2026

Why Cloud-Native Managed Media Services Are Forcing Broadcasters to Rethink Their Infrastructure

Broadcasters are under increasing pressure to deliver high-quality content across multiple platforms, while also reducing costs and improving efficiency. As a result, many are turning to cloud-native managed media services to streamline their operations. One short sentence sums it up: the cloud is now a broadcast engineer's best friend. However, this shift towards cloud-based services is not without its challenges, and broadcasters must carefully consider the implications for their infrastructure, workflows, and bottom line.

What This Development Actually Means

Globecast has announced a new managed media service that leverages cloud-native technologies, including object storage, microservices, and Kubernetes, to provide a scalable and flexible platform for broadcasters. This service utilizes API orchestration to automate workflows, reducing the need for manual intervention and minimizing the risk of errors. By using a cloud-native approach, Globecast's service can also help broadcasters reduce their egress costs and improve their overall total cost of ownership.

This development is part of a broader industry trend towards cloud-based services, driven by the need for greater flexibility, scalability, and cost efficiency. As broadcasters look to deliver content across multiple platforms, they require infrastructure that can keep pace with changing viewer habits and technological advancements. Vendors such as Avid, Vizrt, and Pebble Beach Systems are already responding to this trend, with cloud-based solutions that support file-based workflows and OTT delivery.

Why It Matters for Broadcast Operations

The move towards cloud-native managed media services has significant implications for broadcast operations, particularly in terms of infrastructure decisions. Over the next 12-18 months, broadcast engineers will need to assess their current infrastructure and determine whether it is compatible with cloud-based services. This may involve evaluating the use of cloud-agnostic platforms, such as those offered by Harmonic or Telestream, to ensure seamless integration with existing systems. Additionally, broadcasters will need to consider the impact of cloud-based services on their workflows, including the use of automated QC tools, such as those provided by Interra Systems or Tektronix, to ensure high-quality content delivery. As they navigate this transition, broadcasters will also need to weigh the benefits of cloud-based services against the potential risks, including the need for robust security measures, such as those provided by Irdeto or Verimatrix, to protect their content and prevent piracy.

  • Regional and national broadcasters: Will need to budget for the costs of migrating their infrastructure to cloud-native services, including the potential need for new hardware and software.
