Turning archives into intelligence: Vizrt and DeepVA introduce AI-driven metadata enrichment in Viz One media asset management

The Streamic Editorial Team March 26, 2026 941 words · 5 min read

Why AI-Driven Metadata Enrichment Is Forcing Broadcasters to Rethink Media Asset Management

Broadcasters are under pressure to extract more value from their archives, and the latest development in AI-driven metadata enrichment is a significant inflection point. As the industry shifts toward more efficient and automated workflows, the ability to accurately and automatically enrich metadata will become a critical differentiator. With the sheer volume of content being produced and archived, manual metadata tagging is no longer a viable option.

What This Development Actually Means

The introduction of AI-driven metadata enrichment in media asset management (MAM) systems, such as Viz One, represents a significant advancement in the field. This capability, made possible through partnerships like the one between Vizrt and DeepVA, enables the automated analysis and tagging of media assets with relevant metadata, such as object detection, facial recognition, and speech-to-text transcription. By leveraging machine learning algorithms and natural language processing, these systems can accurately identify and extract meaningful information from large datasets, reducing the need for manual intervention and increasing the overall efficiency of the workflow.

This development is particularly significant in the context of the current media landscape, where the proliferation of content and the need for personalized experiences are driving the demand for more sophisticated and automated metadata management. As broadcasters and media companies look to monetize their archives and create new revenue streams, the ability to accurately and efficiently enrich metadata will become a critical component of their overall strategy.

Why It Matters for Broadcast Operations

The implications of AI-driven metadata enrichment for broadcast operations are far-reaching, with potential impacts on everything from content discovery and recommendation to advertising and monetization. For example, broadcasters like BBC and NBCUniversal, who are already investing heavily in IP-based workflows and cloud infrastructure, will need to consider how to integrate AI-driven metadata enrichment into their existing MAM systems, such as those provided by Avid and Dalet. Similarly, OTT platforms like Netflix and Hulu, who are driving the demand for more personalized and interactive experiences, will need to evaluate how AI-driven metadata enrichment can help them better understand their audiences and deliver more targeted content. As the industry continues to evolve, vendors like Amazon Web Services (AWS) and Microsoft Azure, who are providing the underlying cloud infrastructure for many of these workflows, will play a critical role in supporting the development and deployment of AI-driven metadata enrichment capabilities.

Impact by Stakeholder

  • Regional and national broadcasters: Will need to budget for the integration of AI-driven metadata enrichment into their existing MAM systems, which could potentially reduce operational overhead and improve content discovery. For example, they can eliminate manual QC effort and reduce the time spent on metadata tagging, allowing them to focus on higher-value tasks like content creation and curation.
  • OTT and streaming platforms: Will need to consider how AI-driven metadata enrichment can help them better understand their audiences and deliver more targeted content, which could lead to increased engagement and revenue. For instance, they can use AI-driven metadata enrichment to analyze viewer behavior and preferences, and create personalized content recommendations that drive higher engagement and conversion rates.
  • Post-production and MAM teams: Will need to evaluate how AI-driven metadata enrichment can improve their workflows, particularly in terms of ingest, QC, and archive operations. For example, they can use AI-driven metadata enrichment to automate the tagging of media assets with relevant metadata, such as object detection and facial recognition, which can improve the accuracy and efficiency of their workflows.

Deep Insight: The Broader Industry Shift

The development of AI-driven metadata enrichment is part of a broader industry shift toward more automated and efficient workflows, driven by the need for greater scalability, flexibility, and personalization. As the media landscape continues to evolve, we expect to see a growing demand for AI-driven solutions that can help broadcasters and media companies extract more value from their archives and create new revenue streams. The vendors who will struggle here are those who fail to invest in AI-driven metadata enrichment and automated workflow solutions, such as those provided by companies like Blackbird and GrayMeta. By 2025, we expect AI-driven metadata enrichment to become a standard feature of MAM systems, and for cloud-based infrastructure, such as AWS and Azure, to play a critical role in supporting the development and deployment of these capabilities. The real risk is not the adoption of AI-driven metadata enrichment, but rather the failure to integrate it into existing workflows and systems, which could lead to significant disruptions and inefficiencies.
