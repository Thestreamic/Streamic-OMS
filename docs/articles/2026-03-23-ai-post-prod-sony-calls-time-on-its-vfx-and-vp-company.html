Technical Analysis: Sony's Withdrawal from Pixomondo Impacts Live Signal Transport and VFX Collaboration

Sony has announced its decision to close Pixomondo, its visual effects and virtual production company. This move affects the live signal transport and collaboration workflows that relied on Pixomondo's expertise in VFX and VP. The company's work involved the use of various technologies, including ST 2110, NDI, and SRT, to ensure low-latency and high-quality signal transport.

Operational ROI

The closure of Pixomondo will likely lead to changes in the operational workflows of broadcast companies that relied on its services. One potential area of impact is the transmission cost of live signals. With Pixomondo's expertise in VFX and VP, broadcast companies may have been able to reduce their transmission costs by using more efficient signal transport protocols, such as SRT or RIST, which offer lower latency and higher quality than traditional satellite or fibre transmission methods. For example, a broadcast company that previously used Pixomondo's services to transport live signals may now need to invest in additional hardware or software to maintain the same level of quality and latency, potentially increasing their transmission costs.

In comparison to the existing approach, the closure of Pixomondo may lead to a shift towards more traditional signal transport methods, such as 12G-SDI, which may not offer the same level of efficiency and quality as newer protocols like ST 2110 or JPEG XS. This could result in increased hardware rack footprint and power consumption, as well as higher maintenance costs. Additionally, the loss of Pixomondo's expertise in VFX and VP may lead to a decrease in the overall quality of live productions, potentially affecting the viewer experience and ultimately, the revenue of broadcast companies.

Security & Integration

The closure of Pixomondo also raises concerns about the security and integration of live signal transport workflows. Broadcast companies that relied on Pixomondo's services will need to ensure that their new signal transport protocols and hardware are compliant with relevant standards, such as ST 2110, NMOS, and AES67. This may require additional investment in cybersecurity measures, such as encryption and access control, to protect sensitive content and prevent unauthorized access. Furthermore, the integration of new signal transport protocols and hardware will need to be carefully planned and executed to ensure seamless collaboration between different teams and departments, using standards like Genlock and JPEG XS to maintain signal integrity and frame-accuracy.

Engineering Takeaways

  • Deployment: Broadcast companies will need to assess their current signal transport infrastructure and determine the necessary hardware and software upgrades to maintain the same level of quality and latency. This may include investing in new encoders, decoders, and network infrastructure, as well as ensuring that their systems are compatible with relevant standards, such as SMPTE ST 2110-20.
  • Compatibility: Engineering teams will need to ensure that their new signal transport protocols and hardware are compatible with existing systems and workflows, using standards like NMOS IS-04 and SRT to facilitate interoperability. This may require careful planning and testing to ensure that the new systems can integrate with existing infrastructure, such as 12G-SDI and AES67.
  • What to Watch: The development of new signal transport protocols and standards, such as the upcoming updates to ST 2110, will be crucial for broadcast companies to track. These updates may offer new opportunities for improving signal quality, reducing latency, and increasing efficiency, and engineering teams will need to stay informed to take advantage of these developments.

The closure of Pixomondo signals a significant shift in the live signal transport and VFX collaboration landscape, and broadcast companies will need to adapt quickly to maintain their competitive edge. Engineering teams should prioritize assessing their current infrastructure and planning for necessary upgrades to ensure they can continue to deliver high-quality live productions with low latency and signal integrity.
