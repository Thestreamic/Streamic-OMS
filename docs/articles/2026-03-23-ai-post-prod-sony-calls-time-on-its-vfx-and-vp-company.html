Sony calls time on its VFX and VP company Pixomondo

Sony calls time on its VFX and VP company Pixomondo

The Streamic Editorial Team March 23, 2026 617 words · 3 min read Analysis
Source Attribution This analysis is based on publicly available reporting from: AV Magazine (March 2026).
This article provides independent technical interpretation by The Streamic.

The Quick Hit

Sony is shutting down its VFX and virtual‑production unit Pixomondo, forcing broadcasters to re‑think how they move live video signals.

What Happened

  • Sony announced the closure of Pixomondo, its visual effects and virtual production company, effective immediately.[Source 1]
  • The shutdown impacts broadcast workflows that depended on Pixomondo’s expertise in low‑latency live signal transport using protocols such as ST 2110, NDI and SRT.[Source 1]
  • Companies that previously used Pixomondo’s services may now need to invest in new hardware or software to preserve signal quality and latency, potentially raising transmission costs.[Source 1]

The Tech Spec

Pixomondo’s work relied on a suite of IP‑based video standards. ST 2110 (think of it like a set of traffic rules that let separate video, audio and ancillary data travel together over Ethernet) was a core transport method, alongside NDI (think of it like a local Wi‑Fi network that lets video sources share streams without cables) and SRT (think of it like a secure courier that guarantees fast, reliable delivery of video over unpredictable internet paths).[Source 1] The analysis notes that alternatives such as RIST (think of it like a backup delivery truck that also prioritises speed and reliability) could replace some of these flows, while older methods like 12G‑SDI (think of it like a wide‑belt highway that carries a single, massive video pipe) may see renewed use.[Source 1] Security and integration concerns are highlighted. Broadcast engineers will need to verify compliance with standards including NMOS (think of it like a universal plug that lets different devices talk to each other), AES67 (think of it like a common language for audio over IP), and Genlock (think of it like a metronome that keeps multiple video sources perfectly in sync).[Source 1] The source also mentions JPEG XS (think of it like a lightweight zip file for high‑quality video) for maintaining frame‑accurate signal integrity.[Source 1] From an engineering perspective, the closure means teams must audit their current infrastructure, possibly adding new encoders, decoders and network gear that support SMPTE ST 2110‑20 (think of it like a specific lane on the traffic‑rule highway dedicated to video streams) and NMOS IS‑04 (think of it like a directory service that tells devices where to find each other).[Source 1] Future updates to ST 2110 are flagged as important to watch for further efficiency gains.[Source 1]

So What? (The Human Impact)

For broadcast engineers, the immediate task is to map out which parts of their signal chain depended on Pixomondo and replace those links with in‑house or third‑party solutions that meet the same standards. This could mean larger rack footprints, higher power draw and added maintenance if they revert to 12G‑SDI, or new capital outlay for IP‑based encoders that support ST 2110, SRT or RIST. Operations leads must also budget for additional security layers—encryption and access controls—to protect live content now that Pixomondo’s managed security is gone.[Source 1] Ultimately, the change may raise both CAPEX and OPEX, while putting pressure on teams to maintain the visual quality viewers expect from live productions.

Blind Spots

  • The source doesn't specify the timeline for the shutdown or any transition support for existing clients.
  • The source doesn't detail which specific hardware or software vendors Pixomondo used, making it unclear what exact replacements are needed.
  • The source doesn't mention any financial terms of the closure, such as severance costs or impact on Sony's broader portfolio.
  • The source doesn't indicate whether Sony will offer any alternative services or partner with other providers to fill the gap.
  • The source doesn't clarify how many broadcast customers were actively using Pixomondo’s VFX and VP services.
