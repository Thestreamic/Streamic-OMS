Technical Analysis: Vizrt's JPEG XS Implementation Reduces Latency in Live Graphics Production

Vizrt, a leading provider of graphics solutions, has announced the integration of JPEG XS into their live graphics production workflow. This development aims to reduce latency and improve signal integrity in live broadcast environments. The implementation of JPEG XS, a compression standard designed for low-latency video transport, is expected to have a significant impact on the broadcast industry.

Operational ROI

The integration of JPEG XS into Vizrt's live graphics production workflow is expected to save broadcast CTOs significant amounts of money by reducing transmission costs. With JPEG XS, broadcasters can achieve latency as low as 1-2 milliseconds, which is significantly lower than traditional satellite or fibre transmission methods. This reduction in latency will simplify signal routing and reduce the hardware rack footprint required for live production, resulting in lower operational costs. Specifically, this affects the signal transport workflow step, where broadcasters can now use a single 1GbE link to transport multiple HD or 4K video streams.

In comparison to the existing approach, which often relies on traditional uncompressed video transport methods such as 12G-SDI, the use of JPEG XS compression reduces the required bandwidth and infrastructure. This change operationally simplifies the setup and configuration of live production environments, allowing for more flexible and scalable deployments. Additionally, the reduced latency enabled by JPEG XS allows for more accurate synchronization of live graphics and video elements, resulting in improved overall production quality.

Security & Integration

The integration of JPEG XS into Vizrt's live graphics production workflow is designed to fit seamlessly into existing broadcast IT stacks, with support for industry-standard protocols such as ST 2110, NMOS, and AES67. From a cybersecurity perspective, the use of JPEG XS compression does not introduce any new security risks, as it is a standardized and widely adopted compression format. Broadcasters can ensure compliance with relevant standards and regulations, such as SMPTE ST 2110-20 and NMOS IS-04, by implementing JPEG XS in their live production environments.

Engineering Takeaways

  • Deployment: To deploy JPEG XS in a live production environment, broadcasters will require a minimum of 1GbE network infrastructure and compatible hardware, such as JPEG XS-enabled video capture cards or IP-based video routers.
  • Compatibility: Vizrt's implementation of JPEG XS is compliant with the SMPTE ST 2110-22 standard, ensuring seamless integration with other ST 2110-based devices and systems. Additionally, the solution supports NMOS IS-05 for device registration and control.
  • What to Watch: Engineers should track the upcoming update to the SMPTE ST 2110 standard, which is expected to include new specifications for JPEG XS compression and transport. This update will provide further guidance on the implementation and deployment of JPEG XS in live production environments.

The integration of JPEG XS into Vizrt's live graphics production workflow signals a significant shift towards more efficient and low-latency live production environments. Engineering teams should begin evaluating the use of JPEG XS in their own live production workflows, focusing on the potential benefits of reduced latency and improved signal integrity.

Analysis: Implementing ST 2110 for IP-based video transport can reduce infrastructure costs in budget-constrained studio builds, enabling more efficient signal routing and processing with lower latency and bitrate requirements.
