Operations & Logistics Focus

The Hidden Logistics Behind Celebrity Ad Campaigns

Looking beyond the creative to the operational teams managing rights, approvals, asset versioning, and technical delivery under real-time pressure.

By Graham McKenna, CMO • June 23, 2026 • 5 min read

The FIFA World Cup is well underway, and much of the advertising industry’s conversation off the pitch has revolved around standout creative, breakout campaigns, and a question scientists are still working to answer: just how many ads for just how many brands Messi and Beckham can appear in simultaneously.

According to XR data, 75% of U.S. campaigns feature celebrities, athletes, or both. Messi has appeared in ads for at least six brands; Beckham a staggering nine, nearly 30% of all World Cup ads featuring celebrities have him in the cast. Add Timothée Chalamet, Jason Sudeikis, Kim Kardashian, and Billy Bob Thornton, and the ad roster is starting to look deeper than some national team benches.

“For the ad operations teams behind the scenes, every famous face brings a long list of rights, approvals, and compliance requirements that must be managed before a campaign ever reaches a World Cup audience.”

For marketers, celebrity-led creative offers a powerful way to break through with audiences whether they are football fans or not. Behind every campaign, however, operations teams must navigate a complex web of rights, approvals, and compliance requirements before a single advertisement reaches viewers.

More talent means more complexity

Many marketers assume the hard work ends when the director calls cut. In reality, the biggest challenges often begin afterward, as campaigns are adapted, approved, and delivered across a growing number of platforms throughout their run.

A single ad featuring celebrity talent can quickly multiply into dozens of versions as it’s cut for different terms, territories, and platforms. Rights agreements vary by market, duration, and channel, creating a web of requirements that must be carefully managed behind the scenes. Nike’s “Rip the Script” and Adidas’s “Backyard Legends” illustrate how fast complexity scales: both campaigns bring together football legends, rising stars, and A-list talent whose combined appeal spans fandoms and geographies, but every additional personality adds another layer of rights to manage.

Rights management is now an ad ops problem

Historically, rights management was considered a legal issue. Today, it’s just as much an operational one. Under SAG-AFTRA rules, brands must generally renew talent rights every 13 weeks to keep a campaign running, and missing a renewal can trigger substantial penalties. As campaigns stay in market longer (often beyond the World Cup) and run across more channels, tracking these rules has become a core operational challenge, not an afterthought.

That challenge intensifies during major sporting events, when campaigns launch across multiple markets at once. The advantage of using global football stars and celebrities is that a single campaign can run in more regions without requiring separate shoots. However, when rights are not managed correctly, campaigns can be pulled on short notice, delivery schedules disrupted, and operations teams left scrambling to make last-minute fixes under real-time pressure.

From celebrity rights to technical requirements

The modern advertising ecosystem has also dramatically increased the number of endpoints that campaigns must support. A World Cup campaign may run across multiple platforms at once, each with its own technical specifications, approval requirements, and delivery timelines. Operations teams must now manage a matrix of versions, each with its own compliance considerations.

Recent industry research from XR found that asset versioning has become one of the leading causes of campaign delays, with nearly 40% of marketers identifying it as a major operational bottleneck. And there’s no flexibility on launch windows tied to specific sports events or sponsorship commitments—the tournament waits for no one.

Visibility is the differentiator

As complexity grows, so does the need for clear visibility across the entire delivery chain. Operations teams need confidence that assets are not only technically correct but also aligned with the rights, approvals, and platform requirements attached to each version. Without that visibility, issues surface too late, appearing as delivery delays, compliance failures, or last-minute firefighting. In football, a VAR team can review a goal-line decision in seconds; an ad operations team without proper tooling doesn’t have that luxury.

In today’s environment, delivering the right message is only half the challenge. Getting the right version to the right audience, with the right rights attached, at exactly the right moment, is what increasingly separates successful campaigns from expensive mistakes.

For brands investing millions in global sporting events, operational excellence has become just as important as creative excellence. The campaigns audiences remember are often powered by invisible teams ensuring every asset reaches every market accurately, compliantly, and on time.

By the time you finish reading this, Beckham has probably appeared in three more ads. And Messi has scored another hat trick.
