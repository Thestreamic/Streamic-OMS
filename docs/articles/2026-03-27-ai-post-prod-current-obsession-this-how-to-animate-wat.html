Milano Cortina 2026: How the Olympics Built a “Go-Anywhere, Do-Anything” Broadcast System

The Milano Cortina 2026 Winter Olympics showed how cloud production, AI highlights, JPEG-XS transport, and virtual OB vans can transform live sports broadcasting at global scale.

The Streamic Editorial Team March 29, 2026 349 words · 2 min read Analysis

The Milano Cortina 2026 Winter Olympics didn't just deliver elite sport. It delivered a glimpse of what the next generation of live broadcasting looks like.

For the first time at this scale, the entire broadcast operation behaved like one giant, flexible, cloud-powered machine. Whether a camera was positioned high in the mountains of Cortina or inside the main broadcast center in Milan, the system connected everything with remarkable speed and precision — almost as if it were all happening in the same room.

1. The Scale: More Cameras, More Sound, More Coverage Than Ever

Olympic Broadcasting Services (OBS) captured the Games on a massive scale:

  • 6,500+ hours of live and recorded content
  • 810+ camera systems across all venues
  • 1,800 microphones picking up everything from ski edges to crowd roars

2. AI Highlights and 3D Replays

Milano Cortina 2026 was the first Winter Olympics to fully use AI to help create content — automatically creating highlights within seconds, and powering 3D “Spacetime Slice” replays that separated athletes from snow or ice in under 20 seconds.

3. Connectivity: Solving the Mountain Distance Problem

The biggest challenge was geography. OBS used JPEG-XS ultra-fast compression to send video from the Alps to Milan with less than one frame of delay, backed by a 100-gigabit fiber network and 5G Broadcast trials led by Rai and the EBU.

4. Sustainability: The Vanishing IBC and Virtual OB Vans

The “Vanishing IBC” model reduced the physical broadcast center size by about 40%, with production tools running in the cloud instead of physical trucks at every venue — greener, cheaper, and more flexible.

5. A New Era of Anywhere-to-Anywhere Broadcasting

Milano Cortina 2026 proved that live sports no longer need to be tied to one location. Everything — cameras, replays, editing, commentary, distribution — can now happen from anywhere in the world, pointing the way to smaller crews, faster production, and lower environmental impact.
