Why Real-Time Transport Protocol Is Forcing Broadcasters to Rethink Signal Integrity

Latency and signal integrity are under scrutiny as broadcasters navigate the complexities of live signal transport.

The shift towards IP-based infrastructures, such as ST 2110 and NDI, has introduced new challenges in maintaining frame-accuracy and Genlock synchronization, while also reducing transmission costs and hardware rack footprint. As a result, broadcasters are re-evaluating their signal transport protocols, including SRT, RIST, and AES67, to ensure reliable and low-latency signal delivery.

What This Development Actually Means

The adoption of real-time transport protocols, such as SRT and RIST, is gaining momentum, with vendors like Matrox, Riedel, and Evertz incorporating these protocols into their IP gateways and signal processing equipment. This development is significant, as it enables broadcasters to transmit high-quality, low-latency signals over IP networks, while also providing improved security and reliability.

The industry context for this development is the increasing demand for live and interactive content, which requires broadcasters to deliver high-quality signals with minimal latency. The use of real-time transport protocols is a response to this trend, as it allows broadcasters to maintain signal integrity and frame-accuracy, even in the presence of network congestion or packet loss.

Why It Matters for Broadcast Operations

The adoption of real-time transport protocols has significant implications for broadcast operations, particularly in terms of CapEx and OpEx. By using protocols like SRT and RIST, broadcasters can reduce their transmission costs and minimize the need for expensive satellite or fiber links. Additionally, the use of IP-based infrastructures can simplify signal routing and reduce the hardware rack footprint, leading to lower energy costs and improved maintenance efficiency. Vendors like Harmonic, with their VOS360 platform, and Telestream, with their Vantage system, are already incorporating real-time transport protocols into their encoding and transcoding solutions, making it easier for broadcasters to integrate these protocols into their existing workflows.

Impact by Stakeholder

  • Regional and national broadcasters: Will need to budget for the deployment of IP-based infrastructures, including ST 2110 and NDI, to take advantage of real-time transport protocols. This will require significant investment in new equipment and training, but will ultimately lead to reduced transmission costs and improved signal quality.
  • OTT and streaming platforms: Will need to reassess their ABR ladder and CDN cost per GB, as the use of real-time transport protocols can impact the complexity of their SSAI and latency budget. Platforms like Netflix and Amazon Prime will need to work closely with their CDN partners to ensure that their signals are delivered with minimal latency and maximum quality.
