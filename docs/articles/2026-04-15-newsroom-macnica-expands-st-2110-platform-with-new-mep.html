Macnica Expands ST 2110 Platform with New MEP25 for 25GbE Media Workflows

Macnica Expands ST 2110 Platform with New MEP25 for 25GbE Media Workflows

The Streamic Editorial Team April 15, 2026 413 words · 2 min read Analysis
Source Attribution This analysis is based on publicly available reporting from: BroadcastBeat (April 2026).
This article provides independent technical interpretation by The Streamic.

The Quick Hit

Macnica has introduced the MEP25, a 25GbE-capable addition to its ST 2110 platform, targeting higher-throughput IP media workflows without altering the core timing or redundancy model. For engineering leads, this expands the cost-performance envelope of uncompressed IP routing while maintaining interoperability with existing 10GbE ST 2110 fabrics.

What Happened

  • Macnica announced the MEP25, a new module within its ST 2110 platform, designed to support 25GbE network interfaces for media transport.
  • The expansion preserves ST 2110 timing, redundancy, and IP routing behavior, ensuring compatibility with existing 10GbE ST 2110 deployments.
  • The update targets infrastructure-level workflows, including signal routing between cameras, production switchers, graphics engines, and distribution paths.

The Tech Spec

The MEP25 integrates into Macnica’s ST 2110 platform, which orchestrates uncompressed video, audio, and ancillary data transport over IP networks. Key technical attributes include:

  • Throughput: 25GbE interfaces, doubling the per-link capacity of 10GbE ST 2110 fabrics while maintaining the same PTP-based timing model.
  • Compatibility: Designed to federate with existing 10GbE ST 2110 nodes, allowing phased upgrades without full fabric replacement.
  • Workflow Scope: Addresses signal routing between production-critical endpoints—cameras, switchers, graphics engines, and distribution paths—without altering redundancy or failover mechanisms.

The source does not specify whether the MEP25 supports NMOS IS-04/IS-05 for device discovery and connection management, or if it requires proprietary control planes.

So What?

For broadcast engineers, the MEP25 reduces the per-channel cost of uncompressed IP transport by consolidating more essence streams onto fewer 25GbE links. This lowers switch port counts and simplifies cable management in greenfield builds or expansions. The backward compatibility with 10GbE ST 2110 fabrics also mitigates rip-and-replace risks for facilities already invested in IP-based production.

Operationally, the upgrade accelerates time-to-air for high-bandwidth workflows—such as 4K/UHD or multi-camera HDR productions—by eliminating bottlenecks in the signal routing layer. The source does not quantify latency improvements or egress cost implications for cloud-adjacent deployments.

Blind Spots

  • The source does not disclose whether the MEP25 supports NMOS IS-04/IS-05 for automated device discovery and connection management, or if it relies on proprietary control protocols.
  • No details are provided on power consumption, heat dissipation, or physical form factor (e.g., 1RU appliance, PCIe card, or OCP-style sled).
  • The announcement lacks pricing or licensing models (per-port, perpetual, or subscription-based).
  • There is no mention of interoperability with third-party ST 2110 stacks (e.g., Grass Valley, Imagine Communications, or open-source implementations).
  • The source does not specify whether the MEP25 includes built-in monitoring or telemetry for PTP sync status, packet loss, or jitter.
