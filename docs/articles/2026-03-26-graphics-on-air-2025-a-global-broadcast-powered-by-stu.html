On Air 2025: A Global Broadcast Powered by Students and Vizrt

Why Real-Time Graphics Rendering Is Forcing Broadcasters to Rethink Live Production Architectures

Live broadcast production is under pressure to deliver higher quality, more engaging experiences to viewers. One short sentence sums it up: latency matters. The shift to IP-based infrastructures, such as those based on ST 2110, has opened up new possibilities for real-time graphics rendering and live signal transport, but it also introduces complexity and new challenges for broadcast engineers, particularly when it comes to ensuring frame-accuracy and signal integrity across the entire production chain.

What This Development Actually Means

The recent collaboration between students and Vizrt on a global broadcast project demonstrates the potential of real-time graphics rendering in live production, leveraging Vizrt's graphics platform to deliver high-quality, interactive graphics in real-time. This development is significant because it showcases the capabilities of modern graphics rendering engines in live broadcast environments, where traditionally, graphics rendering has been a pre-rendered, offline process. The use of NDI and SRT protocols for low-latency signal transport and the integration with AES67 audio over IP further highlight the industry's move towards more flexible, IP-based production workflows.

This sits within the broader context of the broadcast industry's transition to IP-based infrastructures, driven by the need for greater flexibility, scalability, and cost-effectiveness in live production. As broadcasters adopt IP-based systems, they are looking for ways to leverage these new infrastructures to enhance the viewer experience, and real-time graphics rendering is a key area of focus, with vendors like Matrox, Riedel, and Evertz developing solutions to support these new workflows.

Why It Matters for Broadcast Operations

The ability to render graphics in real-time during live broadcasts has significant implications for broadcast operations, particularly in terms of the potential for increased viewer engagement and the ability to respond quickly to changing events. For broadcast engineers, this means rethinking their infrastructure decisions, including the choice of IP gateways, such as those from Matrox or Riedel, and the design of their signal transport networks, which may involve the use of protocols like SRT or RIST. The integration of real-time graphics rendering with other live production elements, such as video switching and audio mixing, will also require careful consideration, with vendors like Avid, Grass Valley, and Sony developing solutions to support these new workflows. Additionally, the use of standards like ST 2110 and NMOS will be crucial in ensuring interoperability and reliability in these complex systems.

Impact by Stakeholder

  • Regional and national broadcasters: Will need to budget for the deployment of IP-based infrastructures, including the purchase of new equipment, such as IP gateways and switches, and the training of staff to operate these systems. They can eliminate the need for traditional, baseband video routing and reduce their transmission costs by leveraging the flexibility of IP-based signal transport.
  • OTT and streaming platforms: Will need to consider the impact of real-time graphics rendering on their content delivery networks (CDNs) and the potential for increased latency in their streaming workflows. They may need to adjust their ABR ladder and CDN cost per GB to accommodate the increased bandwidth requirements of real-time graphics rendering.
  • Post-production and MAM teams: Will need to consider the implications of real-time graphics rendering for their ingest, QC, and archive workflows, particularly in terms of the potential for increased metadata complexity and the need for more sophisticated content management systems, such as those from Avid or Dalet.

Deep Insight: The Broader Industry Shift

The move towards real-time graphics rendering in live production is part of a broader industry shift towards more flexible, IP-based workflows, driven by the need for greater efficiency, scalability, and cost-effectiveness. This shift is also being driven by the increasing demand for high-quality, interactive content, particularly in the areas of sports and news production, where vendors like Vizrt, ChyronHego, and Ross Video are developing solutions to support these new workflows. By 2025, we expect to see widespread adoption of IP-based infrastructures in live production, with a corresponding increase in the use of real-time graphics rendering and other advanced production technologies. The vendors who will struggle here are those who fail to adapt to these new workflows and standards, such as ST 2110 and NMOS, and who do not develop solutions that can integrate seamlessly with other IP-based systems.
