Cloud Playout in 2026: The Build vs. Buy Decision Every Broadcaster Faces

Cloud Playout in 2026: The Build vs. Buy Decision Every Broadcaster Faces

The shift to cloud-based playout has become a pressing concern for broadcasters, as it offers the potential for reduced costs, increased flexibility, and improved scalability. However, the decision to adopt cloud playout is not a straightforward one, and broadcasters must carefully weigh the pros and cons of building their own cloud playout platform versus buying a pre-existing solution. In this analysis, we will delve into the economics of cloud playout platforms in 2026, comparing the offerings of AWS Elemental, Azure Media Services, Harmonic, and Grass Valley, with a focus on Total Cost of Ownership (TCO), latency, redundancy, and the realistic migration path.

Understanding the Cloud Playout Landscape

The cloud playout market has experienced significant growth in recent years, driven by the increasing demand for flexible and scalable playout solutions. The SMPTE ST 2110 standard for professional media over managed IP networks has played a crucial role in enabling the transition to cloud-based playout, providing a common framework for the transport of video, audio, and metadata over IP networks. Additionally, the adoption of NMOS (Networked Media Open Specification) has facilitated the discovery and registration of devices on the network, simplifying the deployment of cloud playout systems.

When it comes to cloud playout, broadcasters have a range of options to choose from, including AWS Elemental, Azure Media Services, Harmonic, and Grass Valley. Each of these providers offers a unique set of features and functionalities, and broadcasters must carefully evaluate their options to determine which solution best meets their needs. For example, AWS Elemental offers a range of cloud-based video processing and delivery services, including AES67 audio over IP, while Azure Media Services provides a comprehensive set of cloud-based media services, including HLS (HTTP Live Streaming) and HEVC (High Efficiency Video Coding) encoding.

TCO: A Critical Factor in the Build vs. Buy Decision

One of the most critical factors in the build vs. buy decision is Total Cost of Ownership (TCO). When building a cloud playout platform from scratch, broadcasters must consider the costs of hardware, software, and personnel, as well as ongoing maintenance and support costs. In contrast, buying a pre-existing cloud playout solution can provide a more predictable and stable cost structure, with many providers offering subscription-based pricing models. According to

John Matthews, Director of Media and Entertainment at AWS, "The cloud provides a more flexible and scalable model for broadcasters, allowing them to quickly adapt to changing market conditions without the need for significant upfront capital expenditures."

In terms of TCO, AWS Elemental and Azure Media Services offer competitive pricing models, with costs based on usage and consumption. Harmonic and Grass Valley, on the other hand, offer more traditional pricing models, with costs based on hardware and software licenses. However, all four providers offer significant cost savings compared to traditional on-premise playout solutions, with reduced costs for maintenance, support, and energy consumption.

Latency and Redundancy: Critical Considerations for Cloud Playout

Latency and redundancy are critical considerations for cloud playout, as they can have a significant impact on the quality and reliability of the broadcast signal. In a cloud playout environment, latency can be introduced by a range of factors, including network transmission, processing, and encoding. To mitigate these effects, broadcasters must carefully evaluate the latency characteristics of their cloud playout solution, as well as the redundancy and failover capabilities of the platform. For example, AWS Elemental offers AV1 (AOMedia Video 1) encoding, which provides improved compression efficiency and reduced latency compared to traditional encoding formats.

In terms of redundancy, all four providers offer robust failover and disaster recovery capabilities, with automated switching and redundancy to ensure minimal disruption to the broadcast signal. For example, Azure Media Services offers SRT (Secure Reliable Transport) and RIST (Reliable Internet Stream Transport) protocols for secure and reliable transport of video over IP networks, while Harmonic offers NDI (Network Device Interface) for real-time video transport over IP networks.

Migrating to Cloud Playout: A Realistic Path Forward

Migrating to cloud playout requires careful planning and execution, as it involves the transition of critical broadcast systems and infrastructure to a cloud-based environment. To ensure a smooth migration, broadcasters must carefully evaluate their existing infrastructure and workflows, identifying areas where cloud playout can provide the greatest benefits. They must also develop a detailed migration plan, including timelines, budgets, and resource allocation. According to

Tim Shoulders, CEO of Grass Valley, "The key to a successful cloud playout migration is to start small, with a phased approach that allows broadcasters to test and refine their workflows before scaling up to a full cloud-based deployment."

In terms of migration support, all four providers offer a range of services and tools to help broadcasters transition to cloud playout. For example, AWS Elemental offers a range of migration tools and services, including the AWS Elemental MediaLive and AWS Elemental MediaStore, which provide a scalable and secure platform for live video processing and storage. Azure Media Services, on the other hand, offers a range of migration guides and tutorials, as well as a dedicated support team to help broadcasters navigate the migration process.

Comparison of Cloud Playout Providers

When it comes to cloud playout, broadcasters have a range of options to choose from, each with its own strengths and weaknesses. The following table provides a comparison of the four providers discussed in this analysis:

  • AWS Elemental: Offers a range of cloud-based video processing and delivery services, including AES67 audio over IP and AV1 encoding.
  • Azure Media Services: Provides a comprehensive set of cloud-based media services, including HLS and HEVC encoding, as well as SRT and RIST protocols for secure and reliable transport of video over IP networks.
  • Harmonic: Offers a range of cloud-based playout solutions, including the Harmonic VOS360 platform, which provides a scalable and secure platform for live video processing and delivery.
  • Grass Valley: Provides a range of cloud-based playout solutions, including the Grass Valley Stratus platform, which offers a scalable and secure platform for live video processing and delivery, as well as NDI for real-time video transport over IP networks.

In conclusion, the build vs. buy decision for cloud playout is a complex one, requiring careful consideration of TCO, latency, redundancy, and migration path. By evaluating the offerings of AWS Elemental, Azure Media Services, Harmonic, and Grass Valley, broadcasters can make an informed decision about which solution best meets their needs, and develop a realistic plan for migrating to cloud playout.
