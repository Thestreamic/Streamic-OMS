Cloud Playout in 2026: The Build vs. Buy Decision Every Broadcaster Faces

The economics of cloud playout platforms in 2026. Compare AWS Elemental, Azure Media Services, Harmonic, and Grass Valley. Focus on TCO, latency, redundancy, and the realistic migration path.

The Streamic Editorial Team March 22, 2026 917 words · 5 min read Analysis

Introduction to Cloud Playout Economics

The year 2026 marks a significant milestone in the adoption of cloud playout solutions for broadcasters. As the industry continues to shift towards more agile and cost-effective operations, the decision to build or buy a cloud playout platform has become a critical one. With several prominent players in the market, including AWS Elemental, Azure Media Services, Harmonic, and Grass Valley, broadcasters are faced with a complex evaluation process. This analysis will delve into the economics of cloud playout platforms, comparing the total cost of ownership (TCO), latency, redundancy, and the realistic migration path for each solution.

Understanding TCO in Cloud Playout

When evaluating the TCO of a cloud playout platform, broadcasters must consider several factors, including infrastructure costs, software licensing fees, and operational expenses. AWS Elemental, for example, offers a cloud-based playout solution that leverages its Medialive and MediaStore services. This approach allows broadcasters to avoid upfront infrastructure costs and instead pay for only what they use. In contrast, Azure Media Services provides a more comprehensive suite of tools, including live encoding, streaming, and content protection, which may result in higher software licensing fees.

The key to minimizing TCO is to carefully assess the specific needs of your organization and select a cloud playout platform that aligns with those requirements. As one industry expert noted, "The biggest mistake broadcasters make is trying to force a square peg into a round hole – they need to find a solution that fits their unique workflow and business model."

Comparing Cloud Playout Platforms

In addition to TCO, latency is another critical factor in the evaluation of cloud playout platforms. Harmonic, for example, has developed a cloud-based playout solution that utilizes its VOS360 platform, which is designed to provide ultra-low latency and high-quality video streaming. Grass Valley, on the other hand, offers a cloud-based playout solution that leverages its GV AMPP platform, which provides a more flexible and scalable architecture. When it comes to redundancy, AWS Elemental and Azure Media Services both offer built-in redundancy and failover capabilities, ensuring that broadcasters can maintain continuous operation in the event of an outage.

Migration Path and Interoperability

For broadcasters considering a migration to a cloud playout platform, the path forward is not always clear. NDI (Network Device Interface) and NMOS (Networked Media Open Specifications) have emerged as key standards for facilitating interoperability between different systems and platforms. SMPTE ST 2110 is another critical standard that enables the transport of video, audio, and metadata over IP networks. By supporting these standards, cloud playout platforms can ensure a more seamless migration process for broadcasters. Harmonic, for example, has developed a comprehensive migration plan that includes support for SMPTE ST 2110 and NMOS, making it easier for broadcasters to transition to its cloud-based playout solution.

Furthermore, the use of AES67 for audio over IP and HLS (HTTP Live Streaming) for streaming video content has become increasingly prevalent in cloud playout platforms. The adoption of HEVC (High Efficiency Video Coding) and AV1 compression standards has also improved the efficiency of video streaming, allowing for higher quality and lower latency. Additionally, SRT (Secure Reliable Transport) and RIST (Reliable Internet Stream Transport) have emerged as key protocols for ensuring secure and reliable transport of video content over the internet.

Build vs. Buy: The Ultimate Decision

Ultimately, the decision to build or buy a cloud playout platform depends on the specific needs and resources of the broadcaster. For smaller organizations with limited resources, a cloud-based playout solution may be the most cost-effective option. AWS Elemental and Azure Media Services offer a range of pre-built templates and workflows that can be easily customized to meet the needs of smaller broadcasters. Larger organizations, on the other hand, may prefer to build their own cloud playout platform, leveraging their existing infrastructure and expertise. Harmonic and Grass Valley offer a range of tools and services that can be used to build a customized cloud playout solution.

  • TCO: Evaluate the total cost of ownership, including infrastructure costs, software licensing fees, and operational expenses.
  • Latency: Consider the latency requirements of your organization and select a cloud playout platform that can meet those needs.
  • Redundancy: Ensure that the cloud playout platform you select provides built-in redundancy and failover capabilities.
  • Migration path: Develop a comprehensive migration plan that includes support for key standards such as SMPTE ST 2110 and NMOS.
  • Interoperability: Select a cloud playout platform that supports key standards such as NDI, NMOS, and SMPTE ST 2110.

Looking Ahead to 2030

As the broadcast industry continues to evolve, the importance of cloud playout platforms will only continue to grow. By 2030, it is likely that cloud playout will become the norm, with most broadcasters having transitioned away from traditional on-premise solutions. The key to success will be finding a cloud playout platform that can meet the unique needs of your organization, while also providing a scalable and flexible architecture that can adapt to changing market conditions. As one industry expert noted,

The future of cloud playout is all about flexibility and scalability – broadcasters need to be able to quickly respond to changing market conditions and viewer preferences.

In the context of cloud playout, the use of artificial intelligence (AI) and machine learning (ML) will also become more prevalent, enabling broadcasters to automate many of the manual processes involved in playout and streaming. The integration of AI and ML will also enable the development of more personalized and interactive streaming experiences, further enhancing the overall quality of the viewer experience.
