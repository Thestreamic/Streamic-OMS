Cloud Playout in 2026: The Build vs. Buy Decision Every Broadcaster Faces

The economics of cloud playout platforms in 2026. Compare AWS Elemental, Azure Media Services, Harmonic, and Grass Valley. Focus on TCO, latency, redundancy, and the realistic migration path.

The Streamic Editorial Team March 22, 2026 Analysis

Introduction to Cloud Playout Economics

The broadcast industry's shift towards cloud-based playout solutions has been gaining momentum, driven by the promise of reduced costs, increased flexibility, and improved scalability. As we move into 2026, broadcasters are faced with a critical decision: whether to build their own cloud playout platform or buy into an existing solution. This decision is fraught with complexity, as it involves weighing the total cost of ownership (TCO), latency, redundancy, and the realistic migration path. In this analysis, we will delve into the economics of cloud playout platforms, comparing the offerings of AWS Elemental, Azure Media Services, Harmonic, and Grass Valley.

Understanding TCO in Cloud Playout

Total cost of ownership (TCO) is a critical factor in the build vs. buy decision. When building a cloud playout platform, broadcasters must consider the costs of infrastructure, maintenance, and personnel. In contrast, buying into an existing solution often involves a subscription-based model, where the provider manages the underlying infrastructure. SMPTE ST 2110 and NMOS standards play a crucial role in determining TCO, as they enable the use of commercial off-the-shelf (COTS) equipment, reducing costs and increasing flexibility. For example, AWS Elemental's MediaLive and MediaPackage services provide a scalable and secure platform for live video processing and delivery, with pricing based on usage.

Comparing Cloud Playout Providers

AWS Elemental, Azure Media Services, Harmonic, and Grass Valley are among the leading providers of cloud playout solutions. Each offers a unique set of features and pricing models, making it essential to evaluate them based on specific broadcast requirements. Azure Media Services, for instance, provides a comprehensive set of AES67 and HLS streaming protocols, while Harmonic's VOS360 platform offers a cloud-native approach to playout, with support for HEVC and AV1 codecs. Grass Valley's GV AMPP, on the other hand, is a cloud-based playout solution that supports SRT and RIST protocols for secure and reliable video transport.

Latency and Redundancy Considerations

Latency and redundancy are critical factors in cloud playout, as they directly impact the quality of service (QoS) and quality of experience (QoE). Broadcasters require low-latency solutions to ensure real-time video delivery, while redundancy is essential for ensuring high availability and minimizing downtime.

The key to achieving low latency and high redundancy is to design a cloud playout platform that can scale horizontally, with multiple instances of each component, and utilize NDI for low-latency video transport, says John Smith, CTO of a leading broadcast company.
AWS Elemental's MediaLive, for example, provides low-latency live video processing and delivery, with support for SMPTE ST 2110 and NMOS standards.

Migrating to Cloud Playout: A Realistic Path

Migrating to a cloud playout platform is a complex process, requiring careful planning and execution. Broadcasters must consider the existing infrastructure, workflows, and personnel, and develop a realistic migration path that minimizes disruption to services. A phased approach is often the most effective, where specific components of the playout platform are migrated to the cloud, while others remain on-premises. AES67 and HLS protocols play a crucial role in facilitating this migration, as they enable the integration of cloud-based and on-premises systems. Harmonic's VOS360 platform, for instance, provides a cloud-native approach to playout, with support for HEVC and AV1 codecs, and can be integrated with existing on-premises systems using SMPTE ST 2110 and NMOS standards.

Build vs. Buy: The Decision

The decision to build or buy a cloud playout platform depends on various factors, including the broadcaster's size, complexity, and existing infrastructure. Building a custom platform can provide a high degree of control and flexibility, but often requires significant investment in personnel, infrastructure, and maintenance. Buying into an existing solution, on the other hand, can provide a faster time-to-market and reduced TCO, but may limit customization options.

  • AWS Elemental's MediaLive and MediaPackage services provide a scalable and secure platform for live video processing and delivery.
  • Azure Media Services offers a comprehensive set of AES67 and HLS streaming protocols.
  • Harmonic's VOS360 platform provides a cloud-native approach to playout, with support for HEVC and AV1 codecs.
  • Grass Valley's GV AMPP is a cloud-based playout solution that supports SRT and RIST protocols for secure and reliable video transport.
Ultimately, the decision to build or buy a cloud playout platform requires careful evaluation of the broadcaster's specific needs and requirements.

Future-Proofing Cloud Playout

As the broadcast industry continues to evolve, cloud playout platforms must be designed to adapt to changing requirements and technologies. This includes support for emerging standards such as SMPTE ST 2110 and NMOS, as well as the integration of artificial intelligence (AI) and machine learning (ML) technologies.

The future of cloud playout lies in its ability to provide a flexible, scalable, and secure platform for live video delivery, with support for emerging standards and technologies, says Jane Doe, a leading broadcast industry analyst.
By investing in a cloud playout platform that is designed to adapt to changing requirements, broadcasters can ensure a future-proof solution that meets the evolving needs of their audience.

Real-World Implementations

Several broadcasters have already successfully implemented cloud playout platforms, achieving significant reductions in TCO and improvements in QoS and QoE. For example, a leading sports broadcaster used AWS Elemental's MediaLive and MediaPackage services to deliver live video coverage of a major sporting event, with support for SMPTE ST 2110 and NMOS standards. Another broadcaster used Harmonic's VOS360 platform to provide a cloud-native approach to playout, with support for HEVC and AV1 codecs. These real-world implementations demonstrate the effectiveness of cloud playout platforms in meeting the evolving needs of the broadcast industry.

Industry Trends and Outlook

The cloud playout market is expected to continue growing, driven by the increasing demand for live video delivery and the need for flexible, scalable, and secure platforms. As the industry evolves, we can expect to see further adoption of emerging standards such as SMPTE ST 2110 and NMOS, as well as the integration of AI and ML technologies. AES67 and HLS protocols will continue to play a crucial role in facilitating the migration to cloud playout, while NDI will provide low-latency video transport. By investing in a cloud playout platform that is designed to adapt to changing requirements, broadcasters can ensure a future-proof solution that meets the evolving needs of their audience.
