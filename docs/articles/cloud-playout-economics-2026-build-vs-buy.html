Cloud Playout in 2026: The Build vs. Buy Decision Every Broadcaster Faces

The shift to cloud playout has become a strategic imperative for broadcasters seeking to reduce costs, enhance scalability, and improve flexibility. As of 2026, the cloud playout market is dominated by a few major players, including AWS Elemental, Azure Media Services, Harmonic, and Grass Valley. Each of these vendors offers a unique set of features, functionalities, and pricing models, making the build vs. buy decision a complex and daunting task for broadcasters. In this article, we will delve into the economics of cloud playout platforms, comparing the total cost of ownership (TCO), latency, redundancy, and realistic migration paths for each of these vendors.

Total Cost of Ownership: A Key Differentiator

TCO is a critical factor in the build vs. buy decision, as it encompasses not only the initial investment but also ongoing expenses, such as maintenance, support, and upgrades. AWS Elemental, for example, offers a cloud-based playout solution that can be deployed on a pay-as-you-go basis, allowing broadcasters to scale up or down as needed. This approach can help reduce capital expenditures and minimize the risk of overprovisioning. In contrast, Azure Media Services provides a more comprehensive suite of media services, including encoding, streaming, and content protection, which can result in higher TCO but also offers greater flexibility and customization.

The cloud has become a strategic imperative for broadcasters, but the TCO of cloud playout platforms is a major concern. As broadcasters, we need to carefully evaluate the costs and benefits of each vendor's solution to ensure that it aligns with our business goals and budget.
According to industry experts, the TCO of cloud playout platforms can vary significantly depending on the vendor, deployment model, and usage patterns. Harmonic, for instance, offers a cloud-based playout solution that is designed to be more cost-effective for large-scale deployments, while Grass Valley provides a more modular approach that allows broadcasters to build and deploy their own cloud playout systems.

Latency and Redundancy: Critical Factors in Cloud Playout

Latency and redundancy are critical factors in cloud playout, as they directly impact the quality and reliability of the broadcast signal. SMPTE ST 2110 has emerged as a key standard for professional media over managed IP networks, enabling the transport of uncompressed video and audio over IP networks with minimal latency. AWS Elemental and Azure Media Services both support SMPTE ST 2110, ensuring that broadcasters can maintain high-quality video and audio signals with low latency. Harmonic and Grass Valley also support this standard, but may require additional configuration and setup.

In terms of redundancy, AWS Elemental and Azure Media Services offer built-in redundancy and failover capabilities, ensuring that the playout signal remains available even in the event of a failure. Harmonic and Grass Valley also provide redundancy and failover capabilities, but may require additional hardware and software components. NMOS (Networked Media Open Specification) is another key standard that enables the discovery and registration of media devices on a network, simplifying the deployment and management of cloud playout systems.

Realistic Migration Paths: A Gradual Approach

Migrating to a cloud playout platform can be a complex and time-consuming process, requiring significant planning, testing, and validation. AWS Elemental and Azure Media Services offer a range of tools and services to simplify the migration process, including AES67 (Audio over IP) and HLS (HTTP Live Streaming) support. Harmonic and Grass Valley also provide migration tools and services, but may require more customization and configuration.

A gradual approach to migration is often the most realistic and effective, involving the deployment of a cloud playout platform in parallel with existing on-premises systems. This approach allows broadcasters to test and validate the cloud playout platform, identify and resolve any technical issues, and gradually transition to the cloud-based system. HEVC (High Efficiency Video Coding) and AV1 (AOMedia Video 1) are two emerging video codecs that offer improved compression efficiency and are well-suited for cloud-based playout applications.

Secure and Reliable Transport: SRT and RIST

Secure and reliable transport of video and audio signals is critical in cloud playout, particularly when dealing with live events and real-time broadcasts. SRT (Secure Reliable Transport) and RIST (Reliable Internet Stream Transport) are two emerging protocols that enable secure and reliable transport of video and audio signals over IP networks. AWS Elemental and Azure Media Services both support SRT and RIST, ensuring that broadcasters can maintain high-quality video and audio signals with minimal latency and packet loss.

NDI (Network Device Interface) is another key protocol that enables the transport of video and audio signals over IP networks, and is widely supported by cloud playout vendors. NDI is particularly useful for live events and real-time broadcasts, where low latency and high-quality video and audio are critical. By supporting SRT, RIST, and NDI, cloud playout vendors can provide broadcasters with a range of options for secure and reliable transport of video and audio signals.

Comparing Cloud Playout Vendors: A Summary

The following table summarizes the key features and functionalities of each cloud playout vendor:

  • AWS Elemental: Pay-as-you-go pricing, support for SMPTE ST 2110, AES67, and HLS, built-in redundancy and failover capabilities
  • Azure Media Services: Comprehensive suite of media services, support for SMPTE ST 2110, AES67, and HLS, built-in redundancy and failover capabilities
  • Harmonic: Cost-effective for large-scale deployments, support for SMPTE ST 2110, AES67, and HLS, modular approach to building and deploying cloud playout systems
  • Grass Valley: Modular approach to building and deploying cloud playout systems, support for SMPTE ST 2110, AES67, and HLS, cost-effective for small- to medium-scale deployments

Ultimately, the choice of cloud playout vendor will depend on the specific needs and requirements of the broadcaster, including TCO, latency, redundancy, and migration path. By carefully evaluating these factors and considering the strengths and weaknesses of each vendor, broadcasters can make an informed decision and ensure a successful transition to cloud playout.
