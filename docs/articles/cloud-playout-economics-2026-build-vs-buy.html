Cloud Playout in 2026: The Build vs. Buy Decision Every Broadcaster Faces

Cloud Playout in 2026: The Build vs. Buy Decision Every Broadcaster Faces

The broadcasting industry is at a crossroads, with the majority of broadcasters considering a move to cloud playout platforms. The economics of cloud playout in 2026 are becoming increasingly attractive, with the promise of reduced Total Cost of Ownership (TCO), increased flexibility, and improved scalability. However, the decision to build or buy a cloud playout platform is a complex one, with several factors to consider, including latency, redundancy, and migration path.

When it comes to cloud playout platforms, there are several options available, including AWS Elemental, Azure Media Services, Harmonic, and Grass Valley. Each of these platforms has its own strengths and weaknesses, and the choice of which one to use will depend on the specific needs of the broadcaster. AWS Elemental, for example, offers a range of cloud-based video processing and delivery services, including SMPTE ST 2110 support for IP-based video transport. Azure Media Services, on the other hand, provides a cloud-based platform for encoding, streaming, and content protection, with support for HLS, HEVC, and AV1 codecs.

TCO and the Economics of Cloud Playout

One of the main drivers for broadcasters to move to cloud playout platforms is the potential to reduce TCO. By moving to the cloud, broadcasters can avoid the upfront costs of purchasing and maintaining hardware, and instead pay only for the resources they use. This can be particularly attractive for smaller broadcasters or those with variable workloads. However, the TCO of cloud playout platforms can be complex, and broadcasters need to consider factors such as data transfer costs, storage costs, and labor costs when calculating the total cost of ownership. In terms of TCO, Harmonic and Grass Valley offer a more traditional approach to cloud playout, with a focus on providing a complete, end-to-end solution that includes hardware and software. This can be more expensive than the cloud-native approach offered by AWS Elemental and Azure Media Services, but it can also provide a more comprehensive and integrated solution. Harmonic, for example, offers a range of cloud-based playout solutions, including its VOS platform, which provides a comprehensive set of tools for channel playout, content preparation, and delivery. Grass Valley, on the other hand, offers its GV AMPP platform, which provides a cloud-based solution for live production, playout, and distribution.

Latency and Redundancy in Cloud Playout

Latency and redundancy are critical factors to consider when evaluating cloud playout platforms. Latency refers to the delay between the time a signal is sent and the time it is received, and it can have a significant impact on the quality of the broadcast. Redundancy, on the other hand, refers to the ability of the platform to continue operating in the event of a failure or outage. Both of these factors are critical to ensuring a high-quality broadcast, and broadcasters need to carefully evaluate the latency and redundancy of any cloud playout platform they are considering.

AWS Elemental and Azure Media Services both offer low-latency solutions for cloud playout, with support for SRT and RIST protocols for secure and reliable video transport. Harmonic and Grass Valley also offer low-latency solutions, with support for NDI and NMOS protocols for IP-based video transport. In terms of redundancy, all four platforms offer a range of options, including redundant servers, storage, and network connections. However, the specific redundancy options and configurations will depend on the needs of the broadcaster and the specific use case.

Migration Path and Integration with Existing Systems

When moving to a cloud playout platform, broadcasters need to consider the migration path and how it will integrate with their existing systems. This can be a complex process, and broadcasters need to carefully evaluate the migration options and ensure that the new platform can integrate with their existing infrastructure. AWS Elemental and Azure Media Services both offer a range of tools and services to support migration, including AES67 support for audio over IP and SMPTE ST 2110 support for IP-based video transport.

Harmonic and Grass Valley also offer a range of migration options, including support for SDI and IP interfaces, as well as integration with existing automation and scheduling systems. In terms of integration with existing systems, all four platforms offer a range of options, including support for REST APIs and SDKs for custom integration. AWS Elemental and Azure Media Services also offer a range of pre-built integrations with popular media systems, including Adobe Creative Cloud and Avid MediaCentral. Harmonic and Grass Valley also offer a range of pre-built integrations, including support for MediaGenix and IBM Aspera.

Realistic Expectations for Cloud Playout in 2026

As the broadcasting industry continues to evolve, cloud playout platforms are likely to play an increasingly important role. By 2026, we can expect to see widespread adoption of cloud playout platforms, with the majority of broadcasters using cloud-based solutions for at least some of their channels. However, the migration to cloud playout will not be without its challenges, and broadcasters need to carefully evaluate their options and plan their migration strategy.

Some of the key trends to watch in cloud playout in 2026 include the increasing use of artificial intelligence and machine learning to automate workflows and improve efficiency. We can also expect to see greater adoption of IP-based video transport protocols, such as SMPTE ST 2110 and NDI, as well as greater use of cloud-based storage and content delivery networks. As

the future of cloud playout is all about flexibility, scalability, and reliability, and broadcasters need to be prepared to adapt to changing viewer habits and technological advancements
, according to a recent report by a leading media research firm.

In terms of the build vs. buy decision, broadcasters need to carefully evaluate their options and consider factors such as TCO, latency, redundancy, and migration path. While building a custom cloud playout platform can provide a high degree of flexibility and control, it can also be expensive and time-consuming. Buying a cloud playout platform, on the other hand, can provide a faster and more cost-effective solution, but it may not offer the same level of customization and control. Ultimately, the decision to build or buy a cloud playout platform will depend on the specific needs and goals of the broadcaster.
