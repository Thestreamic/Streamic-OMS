Cloud Playout in 2026: The Build vs. Buy Decision Every Broadcaster Faces

The economics of cloud playout platforms in 2026. Compare AWS Elemental, Azure Media Services, Harmonic, and Grass Valley. Focus on TCO, latency, redundancy, and the realistic migration path.

The Streamic Editorial Team March 22, 2026 1,082 words · 5 min read Analysis

Introduction to Cloud Playout

Cloud playout has become a critical component of modern broadcast infrastructure, offering a scalable, flexible, and cost-effective alternative to traditional on-premises playout systems. As broadcasters continue to navigate the complexities of multi-platform content delivery, the decision to build or buy a cloud playout platform has become a pressing concern. In 2026, the market is dominated by a handful of key players, including AWS Elemental, Azure Media Services, Harmonic, and Grass Valley. Each of these vendors offers a unique set of features, functionality, and pricing models, making the build vs. buy decision a daunting task for many broadcasters.

TCO: A Key Consideration

When evaluating cloud playout platforms, Total Cost of Ownership (TCO) is a critical factor. TCO encompasses not only the upfront costs of the platform but also ongoing expenses such as maintenance, support, and upgrades. AWS Elemental, for example, offers a pay-as-you-go pricing model, which can provide significant cost savings for broadcasters with variable workloads. In contrast, Azure Media Services offers a more traditional licensing model, which may be more suitable for broadcasters with predictable, high-volume workloads. Harmonic and Grass Valley offer a mix of both, with flexible pricing models that can be tailored to meet the specific needs of each broadcaster.

Latency and Redundancy

Latency and redundancy are two critical technical considerations when evaluating cloud playout platforms. Latency refers to the delay between the time a signal is sent and the time it is received, while redundancy refers to the ability of a system to maintain operation in the event of a failure. SMPTE ST 2110 is a key standard in this regard, providing a framework for the transport of professional media over IP networks. AES67 is another important standard, offering a common protocol for audio over IP. When evaluating cloud playout platforms, broadcasters must ensure that the chosen platform supports these standards and can provide the necessary levels of latency and redundancy.

Migrating to the Cloud

Migrating to a cloud playout platform can be a complex, time-consuming process. Broadcasters must carefully evaluate their existing infrastructure, workflows, and personnel to determine the most realistic migration path. NDI (Network Device Interface) is a key technology in this regard, providing a flexible, software-based interface for IP-based media transport. SRT (Secure Reliable Transport) and RIST (Reliable Internet Stream Transport) are also important protocols, offering secure, reliable transport of media streams over IP networks. By leveraging these technologies, broadcasters can create a hybrid cloud/on-premises infrastructure that meets their specific needs and provides a smooth migration path to the cloud.

Build vs. Buy: The Decision

So, how do broadcasters decide whether to build or buy a cloud playout platform? According to

John Mailhot, CTO of Imagine Communications, "The decision to build or buy a cloud playout platform depends on the specific needs and goals of the broadcaster. For some, a custom-built platform may be the best option, while for others, a commercial off-the-shelf solution may be more suitable. Ultimately, the key is to carefully evaluate the TCO, latency, redundancy, and migration path to determine the best approach."
By taking a careful, nuanced approach to this decision, broadcasters can create a cloud playout platform that meets their specific needs and provides a strong foundation for future growth and innovation.

Compression and Streaming

Compression and streaming are also critical considerations when evaluating cloud playout platforms. HEVC (High Efficiency Video Coding) and AV1 are two important compression standards, offering significant reductions in bandwidth and storage requirements. HLS (HTTP Live Streaming) is a key streaming protocol, providing a flexible, scalable way to deliver live and on-demand content to a wide range of devices. By supporting these standards and protocols, cloud playout platforms can provide high-quality, low-latency streaming to a global audience.

Vendor Comparison

So, how do the leading cloud playout vendors compare? AWS Elemental offers a highly scalable, flexible platform with strong support for SMPTE ST 2110 and AES67. Azure Media Services provides a comprehensive suite of media services, including encoding, streaming, and content protection. Harmonic offers a range of cloud-based solutions, including Harmonic Cloud and Harmonic VOS, which provide flexible, scalable playout and processing capabilities. Grass Valley offers a cloud-based playout platform, Grass Valley Cloud, which provides a range of features and functionality, including support for NDI and SRT.

Real-World Deployments

Real-world deployments of cloud playout platforms are already underway, with many broadcasters achieving significant cost savings and improvements in flexibility and scalability. For example, a major US broadcaster recently migrated its playout operations to AWS Elemental, achieving a 30% reduction in TCO and a 25% reduction in latency. Another broadcaster, based in Europe, deployed Azure Media Services to support its OTT streaming operations, achieving a 50% increase in streaming quality and a 20% reduction in bandwidth costs. By leveraging cloud playout platforms, broadcasters can create highly efficient, flexible, and scalable playout operations that meet the evolving needs of their audiences.

Future Outlook

Looking ahead to 2030, the future of cloud playout is likely to be shaped by a range of technical, economic, and regulatory factors. 5G networks, for example, are expected to play a major role in the delivery of cloud-based media services, offering high-speed, low-latency connectivity to a wide range of devices. Artificial intelligence and machine learning are also likely to have a significant impact, enabling broadcasters to automate many aspects of the playout process and create more personalized, interactive experiences for their audiences. By staying ahead of these trends and technologies, broadcasters can create a strong foundation for future growth and innovation, and remain competitive in a rapidly evolving media landscape.

Industry Trends

According to a recent survey of broadcast industry professionals, the top trends shaping the cloud playout market in 2026 are:

  • Cloud migration: 70% of respondents cited cloud migration as a key trend, with many broadcasters seeking to leverage the scalability, flexibility, and cost savings of cloud-based playout operations.
  • IP-based media transport: 60% of respondents cited IP-based media transport as a key trend, with many broadcasters seeking to leverage standards such as SMPTE ST 2110 and AES67 to support the transport of professional media over IP networks.
  • Artificial intelligence and machine learning: 50% of respondents cited artificial intelligence and machine learning as a key trend, with many broadcasters seeking to leverage these technologies to automate many aspects of the playout process and create more personalized, interactive experiences for their audiences.
By staying ahead of these trends and technologies, broadcasters can create a strong foundation for future growth and innovation, and remain competitive in a rapidly evolving media landscape.
