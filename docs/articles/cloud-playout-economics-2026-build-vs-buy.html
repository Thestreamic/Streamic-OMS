Cloud Playout in 2026: The Build vs. Buy Decision Every Broadcaster Faces

The broadcast industry has undergone significant transformations in recent years, driven by the adoption of cloud-based technologies. One of the most critical decisions broadcasters face today is whether to build or buy a cloud playout platform. With the rise of cloud-based services, broadcasters can now choose from a variety of platforms, including AWS Elemental, Azure Media Services, Harmonic, and Grass Valley. In this analysis, we will delve into the economics of cloud playout platforms in 2026, comparing the total cost of ownership (TCO), latency, redundancy, and migration paths of these leading platforms.

TCO: A Key Consideration in the Build vs. Buy Decision

When evaluating the TCO of a cloud playout platform, broadcasters must consider the costs of infrastructure, maintenance, and support. Building a custom platform from scratch can be a costly and time-consuming endeavor, requiring significant investments in hardware, software, and personnel. In contrast, buying a cloud-based platform can provide a more cost-effective solution, with many providers offering pay-as-you-go pricing models. For example, AWS Elemental offers a range of pricing plans, including a pay-as-you-go model that allows broadcasters to scale their usage up or down as needed. Similarly, Azure Media Services provides a pricing model based on the number of hours of content processed, making it easier for broadcasters to predict and manage their costs.

The key to successful cloud playout is not just about the technology, but also about the operational and commercial models that surround it. Broadcasters need to think about how they can create a flexible and scalable infrastructure that can adapt to changing audience behaviors and market conditions.
As noted by a leading broadcast industry expert, the TCO of a cloud playout platform is not just about the initial investment, but also about the long-term costs of maintenance, support, and upgrades.

Latency: A Critical Factor in Cloud Playout

Latency is a critical factor in cloud playout, as it can have a significant impact on the quality of the viewing experience. Broadcasters require low-latency solutions that can deliver content in real-time, without buffering or delays. SMPTE ST 2110 is a key standard in this regard, providing a framework for the transport of professional media over managed **IP** networks. Many cloud playout platforms, including Harmonic and Grass Valley, support SMPTE ST 2110, ensuring that broadcasters can deliver high-quality, low-latency content to their audiences.

In addition to SMPTE ST 2110, other protocols such as NMOS, AES67, and SRT are also important in cloud playout. These protocols provide a range of benefits, including improved discovery and registration, enhanced audio quality, and increased security. By supporting these protocols, cloud playout platforms can provide broadcasters with the flexibility and reliability they need to deliver high-quality content.

Redundancy: Ensuring Uptime and Availability

Redundancy is critical in cloud playout, as it ensures that content is always available and can be delivered without interruption. Broadcasters require cloud playout platforms that can provide redundant systems, including duplicate servers, storage, and network connections. AWS Elemental and Azure Media Services both provide redundant systems, ensuring that broadcasters can deliver content without interruption. Additionally, RIST (Reliable Internet Stream Transport) is a protocol that provides a reliable and secure way to transport video content over the internet, which is essential for cloud playout.

In addition to redundant systems, cloud playout platforms must also provide robust monitoring and analytics capabilities. This includes real-time monitoring of system performance, content delivery, and audience engagement. By providing these capabilities, cloud playout platforms can help broadcasters to identify and resolve issues quickly, ensuring that content is always available and of high quality.

Migrating to the Cloud: A Realistic Path Forward

Migrating to the cloud can be a complex and challenging process, requiring significant planning and investment. However, many broadcasters are finding that the benefits of cloud playout, including increased flexibility, scalability, and cost savings, make the migration worthwhile. A realistic migration path involves a phased approach, with broadcasters transitioning their playout operations to the cloud in stages. This approach allows broadcasters to test and refine their cloud playout systems, ensuring a smooth transition and minimal disruption to their operations.

NDI (Network Device Interface) is a protocol that can help to simplify the migration process, providing a standardized way to transport video and audio over IP networks. By supporting NDI, cloud playout platforms can provide broadcasters with a flexible and scalable way to deliver content, without the need for expensive and proprietary hardware. Additionally, HLS (HTTP Live Streaming) and HEVC (High Efficiency Video Coding) are also important protocols in cloud playout, providing a way to deliver high-quality video content over the internet.

The cloud is not just a technology, it's a business model. Broadcasters need to think about how they can create a cloud-based playout operation that is flexible, scalable, and cost-effective, while also delivering high-quality content to their audiences.
As noted by a leading broadcast industry expert, the key to a successful migration is to focus on the business benefits of cloud playout, rather than just the technology.

Comparing Cloud Playout Platforms: A Closer Look

When comparing cloud playout platforms, broadcasters must consider a range of factors, including TCO, latency, redundancy, and migration paths. AWS Elemental and Azure Media Services are both leading cloud playout platforms, offering a range of benefits, including scalability, flexibility, and cost savings. Harmonic and Grass Valley are also major players in the cloud playout market, providing a range of solutions, including cloud-based playout, encoding, and streaming.

In addition to these platforms, AV1 (AOMedia Video 1) is an open-source, royalty-free video codec that can provide significant improvements in compression efficiency, which is essential for cloud playout. By supporting AV1, cloud playout platforms can provide broadcasters with a way to deliver high-quality video content at lower bitrates, reducing the costs of content delivery and storage.

Ultimately, the choice of cloud playout platform will depend on the specific needs and requirements of the broadcaster. By carefully evaluating the TCO, latency, redundancy, and migration paths of each platform, broadcasters can make an informed decision and choose the platform that best meets their needs.

Future-Proofing Cloud Playout: Emerging Trends and Technologies

As the broadcast industry continues to evolve, cloud playout platforms must also adapt to emerging trends and technologies. One of the most significant trends in cloud playout is the use of artificial intelligence (AI) and machine learning (ML) to improve the efficiency and effectiveness of playout operations. By using AI and ML, cloud playout platforms can provide broadcasters with a range of benefits, including automated content recognition, personalized advertising, and predictive analytics.

Another emerging trend in cloud playout is the use of edge computing, which involves processing and analyzing data at the edge of the network, rather than in a centralized cloud or data center. By using edge computing, cloud playout platforms can provide broadcasters with a range of benefits, including reduced latency, improved security, and increased scalability. SRT and RIST are protocols that can help to enable edge computing in cloud playout, providing a reliable and secure way to transport video content over the internet.

In conclusion, the build vs. buy decision for cloud playout platforms is a complex one, requiring careful consideration of TCO, latency, redundancy, and migration paths. By evaluating these factors and considering emerging trends and technologies, broadcasters can make an informed decision and choose the cloud playout platform that best meets their needs.
