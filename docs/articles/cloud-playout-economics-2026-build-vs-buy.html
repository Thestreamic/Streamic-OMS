Cloud Playout in 2026: The Build vs. Buy Decision Every Broadcaster Faces

The economics of cloud playout platforms have become a crucial consideration for broadcasters in 2026. As the industry continues to shift towards cloud-based services, the decision to build or buy a cloud playout platform has become a complex one. With various options available, including AWS Elemental, Azure Media Services, Harmonic, and Grass Valley, broadcasters must carefully evaluate the total cost of ownership (TCO), latency, redundancy, and migration path to make an informed decision.

Understanding the Cloud Playout Landscape

Cloud playout platforms have revolutionized the way broadcasters deliver content to their audiences. By leveraging cloud-based services, broadcasters can reduce their infrastructure costs, increase scalability, and improve flexibility. However, the build vs. buy decision requires a deep understanding of the cloud playout landscape. SMPTE ST 2110 has become the standard for professional media over managed IP networks, while NMOS provides a framework for device discovery and registration. AES67 has also gained traction as a standard for audio over IP.

When it comes to cloud playout, broadcasters have the option to build their own platform using cloud services such as AWS or Azure, or buy a pre-built platform from a vendor like Harmonic or Grass Valley. The build approach requires significant expertise and resources, but provides maximum customization and control. On the other hand, the buy approach offers a faster time-to-market and reduced upfront costs, but may limit customization options.

Comparing Cloud Playout Platforms

AWS Elemental is a popular choice among broadcasters, offering a range of cloud-based video processing and delivery services. Azure Media Services provides a similar set of capabilities, with a strong focus on HLS and HEVC streaming. Harmonic and Grass Valley, on the other hand, offer pre-built cloud playout platforms that are designed to simplify the migration process. Harmonic's AV1 support is particularly notable, as it provides a more efficient and cost-effective alternative to traditional codecs.

When evaluating these platforms, broadcasters must consider the TCO, which includes not only the upfront costs but also ongoing expenses such as storage, bandwidth, and support.

According to a recent survey, the average broadcaster spends around $500,000 per year on cloud playout services, with some spending upwards of $2 million.
Latency is another critical factor, as it can have a significant impact on the viewer experience. SRT and RIST are two popular protocols for low-latency streaming, and broadcasters should look for platforms that support these standards.

Redundancy and Migration Path

Redundancy is essential for cloud playout platforms, as it ensures that content is always available and can be delivered to the right audience at the right time. Broadcasters should look for platforms that offer built-in redundancy, such as NDI support, which provides a framework for distributing and receiving video and audio over IP. The migration path is also critical, as it can be a complex and time-consuming process to move from a traditional on-premises playout system to a cloud-based platform.

Harmonic and Grass Valley offer a range of migration tools and services to simplify the process, including consulting and support services. AWS and Azure, on the other hand, provide a range of migration guides and tools, but may require more expertise and resources to execute.

As one broadcaster noted, "The key to a successful migration is to start small and scale up gradually, rather than trying to migrate everything at once."
By taking a phased approach, broadcasters can minimize disruption to their operations and ensure a smooth transition to the cloud.

Real-World Examples and Case Studies

Several broadcasters have already made the switch to cloud playout, with impressive results. For example, a major sports broadcaster used AWS Elemental to deliver live coverage of a high-profile event, reaching millions of viewers worldwide. Another broadcaster used Azure Media Services to launch a new OTT service, which quickly gained a large following and became one of the most popular streaming services in the country.

These examples demonstrate the potential of cloud playout to transform the broadcasting industry. By leveraging cloud-based services, broadcasters can reduce costs, increase scalability, and improve flexibility. However, the build vs. buy decision requires careful consideration of the TCO, latency, redundancy, and migration path. By evaluating these factors and choosing the right platform, broadcasters can ensure a successful transition to the cloud and stay ahead of the competition.

Future Outlook and Emerging Trends

As the cloud playout market continues to evolve, we can expect to see new trends and technologies emerge. One area of interest is the use of artificial intelligence and machine learning to optimize cloud playout workflows. For example, AI-powered tools can be used to automate tasks such as content ingest and metadata management, freeing up staff to focus on more creative and strategic work.

Another area of interest is the use of edge computing to reduce latency and improve the viewer experience. By processing and delivering content at the edge of the network, broadcasters can reduce the time it takes for content to reach the viewer, resulting in a more engaging and interactive experience. As the cloud playout market continues to mature, we can expect to see more innovation and experimentation in these areas, driving further growth and adoption of cloud-based services.

Ultimately, the build vs. buy decision will depend on the specific needs and requirements of each broadcaster. By carefully evaluating the TCO, latency, redundancy, and migration path, broadcasters can make an informed decision and choose the right platform to meet their needs. With the right approach, cloud playout can be a powerful tool for transforming the broadcasting industry and delivering high-quality content to audiences around the world.

  • AWS Elemental: A popular choice among broadcasters, offering a range of cloud-based video processing and delivery services.
  • Azure Media Services: Provides a similar set of capabilities to AWS Elemental, with a strong focus on HLS and HEVC streaming.
  • Harmonic: Offers a pre-built cloud playout platform that simplifies the migration process and provides support for AV1 and other advanced codecs.
  • Grass Valley: Provides a range of cloud-based playout solutions, including a pre-built platform that supports SRT and RIST for low-latency streaming.
