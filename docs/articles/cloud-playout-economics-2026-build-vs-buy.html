Cloud Playout in 2026: The Build vs. Buy Decision Every Broadcaster Faces

The economics of cloud playout platforms in 2026. Compare AWS Elemental, Azure Media Services, Harmonic, and Grass Valley. Focus on TCO, latency, redundancy, and the realistic migration path.

The Streamic Editorial Team March 22, 2026 1,035 words · 5 min read Analysis

Introduction to Cloud Playout in 2026

As the broadcast industry continues to shift towards more flexible and cost-effective solutions, cloud playout has become a critical consideration for every broadcaster. The decision to build or buy a cloud playout platform is a complex one, with various factors to consider, including total cost of ownership (TCO), latency, redundancy, and migration path. In this analysis, we will delve into the economics of cloud playout platforms in 2026, comparing the offerings of AWS Elemental, Azure Media Services, Harmonic, and Grass Valley.

Understanding the Economics of Cloud Playout

The primary driver behind the adoption of cloud playout is the potential for significant cost savings. By moving playout operations to the cloud, broadcasters can eliminate the need for expensive hardware and reduce their capital expenditures. However, the TCO of a cloud playout platform extends far beyond the initial investment. Operating expenses, such as data storage and transfer costs, must also be carefully considered. According to a recent study, the TCO of a cloud playout platform can be up to 30% lower than that of a traditional on-premises solution.

Comparing Cloud Playout Platforms

AWS Elemental, Azure Media Services, Harmonic, and Grass Valley are among the leading providers of cloud playout platforms. Each offers a unique set of features and capabilities, and the choice of which to use will depend on the specific needs of the broadcaster. AWS Elemental, for example, offers a highly scalable and flexible platform that supports a wide range of video codecs, including HEVC and AV1. Azure Media Services, on the other hand, provides a more integrated solution, with tight integration with other Microsoft Azure services.

AWS Elemental: A Deep Dive

AWS Elemental is a popular choice among broadcasters due to its ability to support a wide range of video formats, including HLS and DASH. The platform also features advanced redundancy and failover capabilities, ensuring that playout operations are always available. However, some users have reported issues with latency, particularly when using the platform for live events. To address this, AWS Elemental has introduced a new low-latency mode, which can reduce latency to as little as 1 second.

Migration Path and Redundancy

One of the most critical considerations when migrating to a cloud playout platform is the migration path. Broadcasters must carefully plan the transition to minimize disruption to their operations. A phased approach is often the best strategy, with the migration of non-critical channels first, followed by more critical ones. Redundancy is also essential, with multiple playout channels and data centers required to ensure that operations are always available. As noted by one industry expert:

The key to a successful migration is to have a clear understanding of your existing infrastructure and workflows, and to carefully plan the transition to minimize disruption to your operations.

Harmonic and Grass Valley: Alternative Solutions

Harmonic and Grass Valley offer alternative cloud playout solutions that are worth considering. Harmonic's platform, for example, features advanced channel-in-a-box capabilities, with support for a wide range of video formats and audio codecs, including AES67. Grass Valley's platform, on the other hand, provides a more integrated solution, with tight integration with other Grass Valley products, such as their NDI solutions.

TCO and Latency Considerations

When evaluating the TCO of a cloud playout platform, broadcasters must consider a range of factors, including data storage and transfer costs. Latency is also a critical consideration, particularly for live events. The use of SRT or RIST protocols can help to reduce latency, but these protocols also introduce additional complexity and cost. As the broadcast industry continues to evolve, the need for low-latency solutions will only continue to grow.

Standards and Interoperability

One of the key challenges facing the broadcast industry is the lack of standards and interoperability between different cloud playout platforms. The use of SMPTE ST 2110 and NMOS protocols can help to address this issue, by providing a common framework for the exchange of video and audio signals. However, more work is needed to ensure that these standards are widely adopted and implemented. As noted by another industry expert:

The lack of standards and interoperability is a major obstacle to the widespread adoption of cloud playout, and it is essential that the industry comes together to address this issue.

Realistic Migration Path

A realistic migration path to cloud playout involves a careful evaluation of the broadcaster's existing infrastructure and workflows. This includes an assessment of their current playout systems, video formats, and audio codecs. The migration path must also take into account the need for redundancy and failover capabilities, as well as the potential for scalability and flexibility. By carefully planning the migration path, broadcasters can minimize disruption to their operations and ensure a smooth transition to cloud playout.

Future of Cloud Playout

The future of cloud playout is exciting and rapidly evolving. As the broadcast industry continues to shift towards more flexible and cost-effective solutions, cloud playout will play an increasingly important role. The use of artificial intelligence and machine learning will also become more prevalent, enabling broadcasters to automate many of their playout operations and improve the overall efficiency of their workflows. As one industry expert noted:

The future of cloud playout is all about flexibility, scalability, and automation, and broadcasters must be prepared to adapt to these changes in order to remain competitive.

Key Considerations for Broadcasters

When evaluating cloud playout platforms, broadcasters must consider a range of key factors, including TCO, latency, redundancy, and migration path. They must also carefully evaluate the features and capabilities of each platform, including support for video codecs, audio codecs, and video formats. By taking a careful and considered approach to the evaluation process, broadcasters can ensure that they select the best cloud playout platform for their needs and achieve a successful migration to cloud playout.

Best Practices for Cloud Playout

To ensure a successful migration to cloud playout, broadcasters must follow best practices, including careful planning, thorough testing, and ongoing monitoring. They must also ensure that their cloud playout platform is highly redundant and scalable, with multiple playout channels and data centers to ensure that operations are always available. By following these best practices, broadcasters can minimize disruption to their operations and ensure a smooth transition to cloud playout.
