Cloud Playout in 2026: The Build vs. Buy Decision Every Broadcaster Faces

The economics of cloud playout platforms in 2026. Compare AWS Elemental, Azure Media Services, Harmonic, and Grass Valley. Focus on TCO, latency, redundancy, and the realistic migration path.

The Streamic Editorial Team March 22, 2026 Analysis

Introduction to Cloud Playout Economics

The broadcast industry has undergone significant transformations in recent years, driven by the adoption of cloud-based technologies. Cloud playout, in particular, has emerged as a critical component of modern broadcast infrastructure, enabling broadcasters to streamline their operations, reduce costs, and improve overall efficiency. As we move into 2026, broadcasters are faced with a crucial decision: whether to build or buy a cloud playout platform. This decision is complicated by the economics of cloud playout, which involves careful consideration of total cost of ownership (TCO), latency, redundancy, and migration paths. In this analysis, we will delve into the key factors influencing the build vs. buy decision, comparing the offerings of major players such as AWS Elemental, Azure Media Services, Harmonic, and Grass Valley.

TCO: A Key Factor in the Build vs. Buy Decision

TCO is a critical consideration for broadcasters evaluating cloud playout platforms. When building a custom platform, broadcasters must account for the costs of infrastructure, software, personnel, and maintenance. In contrast, buying a cloud playout platform from a established vendor can provide a more predictable cost structure, with many vendors offering subscription-based pricing models. For example, AWS Elemental offers a range of cloud playout services, including SMPTE ST 2110 support, with pricing based on the number of channels and features required. Similarly, Azure Media Services provides a cloud-based playout platform with AES67 audio support, with costs determined by the number of hours of content processed. When evaluating TCO, broadcasters must also consider the costs of integrating third-party services, such as HLS or HEVC encoding, which can add complexity and expense to a custom-built platform.

Latency and Redundancy: Critical Considerations

Latency and redundancy are essential factors in the design and operation of cloud playout platforms. Broadcasters require platforms that can deliver low-latency, high-availability services to ensure uninterrupted programming and minimize the risk of errors or outages. Harmonic, for example, offers a cloud-based playout platform with AV1 encoding support, which provides improved compression efficiency and reduced latency. Grass Valley also offers a cloud playout platform with SRT and RIST support, enabling secure, low-latency transport of video and audio content over IP networks. When building a custom platform, broadcasters must carefully design and implement redundancy and failover mechanisms to ensure high availability and minimize the risk of downtime.

Migration Paths: A Realistic Approach

Migrating to a cloud playout platform can be a complex and time-consuming process, requiring careful planning and execution. Broadcasters must evaluate their existing infrastructure, workflows, and personnel to determine the best approach for migration. Some vendors, such as AWS Elemental, offer migration tools and services to help broadcasters transition to the cloud. For example, AWS Elemental provides a range of NDI (Network Device Interface) tools and services, enabling broadcasters to integrate their existing infrastructure with cloud-based playout platforms. When building a custom platform, broadcasters must also consider the costs and complexities of migrating existing workflows and personnel to the new platform.

Industry Observations

According to industry experts, the build vs. buy decision for cloud playout platforms is often driven by factors such as scalability, flexibility, and cost. As one industry observer noted:

The decision to build or buy a cloud playout platform depends on the specific needs and requirements of the broadcaster. While building a custom platform can provide greater control and flexibility, it can also be more expensive and time-consuming. Buying a cloud playout platform from a established vendor can provide a more predictable cost structure and faster time-to-market, but may limit the broadcaster's ability to customize the platform to their specific needs.

In addition to these factors, broadcasters must also consider the risks and challenges associated with building and maintaining a custom platform, including the costs of personnel, infrastructure, and maintenance. As the broadcast industry continues to evolve, it is likely that we will see a shift towards more cloud-based playout platforms, with vendors such as AWS Elemental, Azure Media Services, Harmonic, and Grass Valley playing a critical role in shaping the future of the industry.

Technical Standards and Interoperability

Technical standards and interoperability are critical factors in the design and operation of cloud playout platforms. Broadcasters require platforms that can support a range of technical standards, including SMPTE ST 2110, NMOS, and AES67. In addition, platforms must be able to interoperate with a range of third-party services and systems, including HLS and HEVC encoding, as well as SRT and RIST transport protocols. When building a custom platform, broadcasters must carefully evaluate the technical standards and interoperability requirements of their existing infrastructure and workflows to ensure a smooth transition to the cloud.

Future Directions

As the broadcast industry continues to evolve, it is likely that we will see a shift towards more cloud-based playout platforms, with a greater emphasis on AV1 and HEVC encoding, as well as SRT and RIST transport protocols. In addition, we can expect to see greater adoption of NDI and other IP-based protocols, enabling greater flexibility and interoperability between different systems and services. As broadcasters navigate the complex landscape of cloud playout platforms, they must carefully evaluate their options and consider the long-term implications of their decisions. By doing so, they can ensure a successful transition to the cloud and position themselves for success in the years to come.

Comparing Vendor Offerings

When evaluating cloud playout platforms, broadcasters must carefully compare the offerings of different vendors. AWS Elemental, for example, offers a range of cloud playout services, including SMPTE ST 2110 support and AES67 audio encoding. Azure Media Services provides a cloud-based playout platform with HEVC encoding support and SRT transport protocol. Harmonic offers a cloud-based playout platform with AV1 encoding support and RIST transport protocol. Grass Valley provides a cloud-based playout platform with NDI support and SRT transport protocol. By carefully evaluating the features, pricing, and technical specifications of each vendor's offering, broadcasters can make an informed decision about which platform best meets their needs.

Real-World Implementations

Several broadcasters have already successfully implemented cloud playout platforms, with impressive results. For example, a major sports broadcaster recently migrated its playout operations to a cloud-based platform using AWS Elemental, achieving significant cost savings and improved efficiency. Another broadcaster, a leading news organization, implemented a cloud-based playout platform using Azure Media Services, enabling it to quickly scale its operations to meet growing demand. These real-world implementations demonstrate the potential of cloud playout platforms to transform the broadcast industry, and highlight the importance of careful planning and evaluation in the build vs. buy decision.

Best Practices for Implementation

When implementing a cloud playout platform, broadcasters should follow best practices to ensure a smooth and successful transition. These include carefully evaluating the technical requirements of the platform, assessing the skills and training needs of personnel, and developing a comprehensive migration plan. Additionally, broadcasters should consider the importance of monitoring and analytics in ensuring the quality and reliability of the platform, and should implement robust QoS (Quality of Service) and QoE (Quality of Experience) monitoring to ensure optimal performance. By following these best practices, broadcasters can minimize the risks associated with cloud playout and maximize the benefits of this powerful technology.
