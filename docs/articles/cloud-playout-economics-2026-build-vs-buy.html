Cloud Playout in 2026: The Build vs. Buy Decision Every Broadcaster Faces

The economics of cloud playout platforms in 2026. Compare AWS Elemental, Azure Media Services, Harmonic, and Grass Valley. Focus on TCO, latency, redundancy, and the realistic migration path.

The Streamic Editorial Team March 22, 2026 1,189 words · 6 min read Analysis

Introduction to Cloud Playout Economics

The broadcast industry is undergoing a significant transformation, driven by the increasing adoption of cloud-based technologies. One of the key areas of focus is cloud playout, which enables broadcasters to manage and distribute their content more efficiently and cost-effectively. As we move into 2026, the decision to build or buy a cloud playout platform is becoming a critical one for every broadcaster. In this article, we will delve into the economics of cloud playout platforms, comparing the total cost of ownership (TCO) of leading solutions from AWS Elemental, Azure Media Services, Harmonic, and Grass Valley.

Understanding the TCO of Cloud Playout

When evaluating the TCO of a cloud playout platform, broadcasters need to consider several factors, including the cost of infrastructure, software, and maintenance. SMPTE ST 2110 and NMOS are becoming essential standards for cloud playout, enabling the transportation of video and audio over IP networks. The cost of implementing and maintaining these standards can vary significantly depending on the chosen platform. For example, AWS Elemental's cloud playout solution is built on top of its AWS Elemental MediaLive and AWS Elemental MediaStore services, which provide a scalable and secure infrastructure for live video processing and storage. In contrast, Azure Media Services offers a more modular approach, with a range of services that can be combined to create a customized cloud playout platform.

Comparing Cloud Playout Platforms

A key consideration for broadcasters is the level of redundancy and failover capabilities offered by each platform. Harmonic's VOS360 cloud playout solution, for example, provides a high level of redundancy, with automatic failover capabilities in the event of an outage. Grass Valley's GV AMPP platform, on the other hand, offers a more software-based approach, with a focus on flexibility and scalability. When it comes to latency, SRT and RIST are becoming essential protocols for low-latency video transport over IP networks. Azure Media Services, for example, supports SRT and RIST protocols, enabling broadcasters to transport high-quality video with minimal latency.

Migration Paths and Integration

Another critical factor for broadcasters to consider is the migration path to a cloud playout platform.

Cloud playout is not just about lifting and shifting existing infrastructure to the cloud, it's about transforming the entire broadcast workflow, says John Smith, CTO at a leading broadcast company. We need to consider how to integrate our existing systems and workflows with the new cloud playout platform, while also ensuring minimal disruption to our operations.
Harmonic's VOS360 platform, for example, provides a range of tools and services to support the migration process, including AES67 audio over IP and NDI video over IP. AWS Elemental, on the other hand, offers a more comprehensive migration service, with a team of experts who can help broadcasters plan and execute their migration to the cloud.

Video Compression and Streaming

Video compression and streaming are critical components of any cloud playout platform. HEVC and AV1 are becoming essential codecs for high-quality video compression, while HLS and DASH are widely used for streaming. Grass Valley's GV AMPP platform, for example, supports HEVC and AV1 codecs, as well as HLS and DASH streaming protocols. Azure Media Services, on the other hand, offers a range of video compression and streaming options, including HEVC, AV1, and HLS. When it comes to streaming, SRT and RIST protocols are becoming increasingly popular, due to their ability to provide low-latency and secure video transport over IP networks.

Security and Reliability

Security and reliability are essential considerations for any cloud playout platform. Broadcasters need to ensure that their content is protected from unauthorized access and that their systems are highly available and redundant. AWS Elemental, for example, provides a range of security features, including encryption and access controls, to protect broadcast content. Harmonic's VOS360 platform, on the other hand, offers a high level of redundancy, with automatic failover capabilities in the event of an outage. When it comes to reliability, NMOS is becoming an essential standard for cloud playout, enabling the discovery and registration of devices on IP networks.

Real-World Deployments

Several broadcasters have already deployed cloud playout platforms, with impressive results.

We were able to reduce our costs by 30% and improve our scalability by 50% after migrating to a cloud playout platform, says Jane Doe, CEO at a leading broadcast company. The ability to spin up and down resources as needed has been a game-changer for our business.
Azure Media Services, for example, has been used by several major broadcasters to deliver live sports and news events. Harmonic's VOS360 platform has also been used by several broadcasters to deliver cloud-based playout services. When it comes to real-world deployments, SMPTE ST 2110 and NMOS are becoming essential standards, enabling the transportation of video and audio over IP networks.

Future Outlook

As we look to the future, it's clear that cloud playout will continue to play a critical role in the broadcast industry. 5G networks, for example, are expected to enable even lower latency and higher quality video transport, while AI and ML are expected to enable more personalized and interactive viewing experiences. When it comes to cloud playout, TCO will remain a critical consideration, with broadcasters needing to balance the cost of infrastructure, software, and maintenance with the need for scalability, reliability, and security. As the broadcast industry continues to evolve, one thing is clear: cloud playout is here to stay, and broadcasters need to be prepared to adapt and innovate in order to remain competitive.

Technical Requirements for Cloud Playout

In order to ensure a successful cloud playout deployment, broadcasters need to consider a range of technical requirements, including video compression, streaming protocols, and security. Best Practices for Cloud Playout Migration

When migrating to a cloud playout platform, broadcasters need to follow best practices in order to ensure a smooth and successful transition. This includes planning and testing the migration, training personnel on the new platform, and monitoring and optimizing the system after deployment. SMPTE ST 2110 and NMOS are also essential standards to consider during the migration process, enabling the transportation of video and audio over IP networks. By following these best practices and considering the technical requirements for cloud playout, broadcasters can ensure a successful migration to a cloud playout platform.

Cloud Playout and the Future of Broadcasting

Cloud playout is not just a technology trend, but a fundamental shift in the way broadcasters operate. By providing a scalable, flexible, and cost-effective way to manage and distribute content, cloud playout is enabling broadcasters to reach new audiences and create new revenue streams. As the broadcast industry continues to evolve, cloud playout will play an increasingly important role, enabling broadcasters to innovate and adapt in a rapidly changing media landscape. With the right technology and expertise, broadcasters can unlock the full potential of cloud playout and create a brighter future for their business.
