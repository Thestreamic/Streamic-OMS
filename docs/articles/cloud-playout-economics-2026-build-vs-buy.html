Cloud Playout in 2026: The Build vs. Buy Decision Every Broadcaster Faces

The shift towards cloud-based playout has become a significant trend in the broadcast industry, driven by the need for increased flexibility, reduced costs, and improved scalability. As broadcasters consider transitioning their playout operations to the cloud, they are faced with a critical decision: whether to build their own cloud playout platform or buy a pre-existing solution from a third-party vendor. In this analysis, we will examine the economics of cloud playout platforms in 2026, comparing the offerings of AWS Elemental, Azure Media Services, Harmonic, and Grass Valley, with a focus on Total Cost of Ownership (TCO), latency, redundancy, and the realistic migration path.

Cloud Playout Economics: TCO and the Benefits of Scalability

One of the primary drivers of the shift towards cloud playout is the potential for reduced TCO. By leveraging the scalability of cloud infrastructure, broadcasters can avoid the upfront capital expenditures associated with traditional on-premise playout systems. Instead, they can opt for a pay-as-you-go model, where costs are directly tied to usage. AWS Elemental, for example, offers a range of cloud-based playout solutions, including SMPTE ST 2110 support for IP-based video transport. With AWS Elemental, broadcasters can take advantage of the scalability of the cloud, while also benefiting from the vendor's expertise in HEVC and AV1 compression.

In contrast, Azure Media Services offers a more comprehensive suite of cloud-based media services, including live streaming, video-on-demand, and content protection. Azure Media Services also supports HLS and DASH streaming protocols, making it an attractive option for broadcasters looking to reach a wide range of devices and platforms. However, the TCO of Azure Media Services can be higher than AWS Elemental, particularly for large-scale deployments.

Latency and Redundancy: Critical Considerations for Cloud Playout

Latency and redundancy are critical considerations for cloud playout, as they can have a direct impact on the quality of the viewing experience. Harmonic, a leading provider of video compression and streaming solutions, offers a range of cloud-based playout products that prioritize low latency and high redundancy. Harmonic's VOS cloud-based playout solution, for example, features a redundant architecture that ensures uninterrupted playout, even in the event of a failure. Additionally, Harmonic's AES67 support enables low-latency audio transport, making it an attractive option for live events and sports broadcasting.

Grass Valley, another major player in the broadcast industry, offers a range of cloud-based playout solutions that prioritize flexibility and scalability. Grass Valley's GV AMPP cloud-based playout platform, for example, features a modular architecture that allows broadcasters to easily add or remove components as needed. Additionally, GV AMPP supports NDI and SRT protocols, making it an attractive option for broadcasters looking to integrate their cloud playout operations with existing on-premise infrastructure.

Migration Path: A Realistic Approach to Cloud Playout

For many broadcasters, the migration to cloud playout will be a gradual process, with a mix of on-premise and cloud-based infrastructure. As

one industry expert noted, "the key to a successful cloud playout migration is to start small, with a limited number of channels or services, and then gradually scale up as needed."
This approach allows broadcasters to test and refine their cloud playout operations, while also minimizing disruption to existing on-premise infrastructure. RIST and NMOS protocols, for example, can be used to enable the exchange of metadata and control signals between on-premise and cloud-based infrastructure, facilitating a smooth migration path.

In terms of the build vs. buy decision, broadcasters must carefully weigh the costs and benefits of each approach. Building a custom cloud playout platform can provide a high degree of flexibility and control, but it also requires significant expertise and resources. Buying a pre-existing solution from a third-party vendor, on the other hand, can provide a faster time-to-market and reduced TCO, but it may also limit the broadcaster's ability to customize and differentiate their playout operations.

Vendor Comparison: Evaluating the Options

When evaluating cloud playout vendors, broadcasters must consider a range of factors, including TCO, latency, redundancy, and migration path. The following table provides a summary of the key features and benefits of each vendor:

  • AWS Elemental: Scalable cloud infrastructure, SMPTE ST 2110 support, HEVC and AV1 compression
  • Azure Media Services: Comprehensive suite of cloud-based media services, HLS and DASH streaming protocols, high TCO
  • Harmonic: Low-latency and high-redundancy playout solutions, AES67 support, VOS cloud-based playout platform
  • Grass Valley: Flexible and scalable cloud playout solutions, NDI and SRT protocols, GV AMPP cloud-based playout platform

Ultimately, the choice of cloud playout vendor will depend on the specific needs and requirements of the broadcaster. By carefully evaluating the options and considering the key factors of TCO, latency, redundancy, and migration path, broadcasters can make an informed decision that supports their long-term goals and objectives.

Future-Proofing Cloud Playout Operations

As the broadcast industry continues to evolve, cloud playout operations must be future-proofed to accommodate emerging trends and technologies. 5G networks, for example, are expected to play a major role in the distribution of live video content, with their low latency and high bandwidth enabling new use cases such as remote production and immersive streaming. Broadcasters must therefore ensure that their cloud playout operations are optimized for 5G networks, with support for low-latency streaming protocols and high-bandwidth content delivery.

In addition to 5G, other emerging trends such as artificial intelligence and machine learning are also expected to have a significant impact on cloud playout operations. By leveraging these technologies, broadcasters can automate many aspects of their playout operations, improving efficiency and reducing costs. As

one industry expert noted, "the use of AI and ML in cloud playout operations will be a key differentiator for broadcasters, enabling them to provide more personalized and engaging viewing experiences for their audiences."
