Cloud Playout in 2026: The Build vs. Buy Decision Every Broadcaster Faces

The economics of cloud playout platforms in 2026. Compare AWS Elemental, Azure Media Services, Harmonic, and Grass Valley. Focus on TCO, latency, redundancy, and the realistic migration path.

The Streamic Editorial Team March 22, 2026 1,055 words · 5 min read Analysis

Introduction to Cloud Playout Economics

The shift to cloud-based playout has become a critical consideration for broadcasters, as it offers the potential for significant cost savings and increased flexibility. By 2030, the majority of playout operations are expected to be cloud-based, with many broadcasters already making the transition. However, one of the most significant decisions facing these broadcasters is whether to build or buy their cloud playout platform. This decision will have a major impact on the total cost of ownership (TCO), latency, redundancy, and the overall migration path.

Several major players are vying for dominance in the cloud playout market, including AWS Elemental, Azure Media Services, Harmonic, and Grass Valley. Each of these platforms has its own strengths and weaknesses, and broadcasters must carefully evaluate these factors when making their build vs. buy decision. In this article, we will delve into the economics of cloud playout platforms in 2026, comparing the leading options and examining the key considerations that will shape the future of broadcast engineering.

Build vs. Buy: Weighing the Options

Building a cloud playout platform from scratch can be a tempting option for broadcasters, as it allows for complete customization and control over the system. However, this approach can also be extremely costly and time-consuming, requiring significant investment in infrastructure, personnel, and research and development. Furthermore, building a platform that meets the required SMPTE ST 2110 standards for IP-based media transport can be a complex and challenging task, requiring specialized expertise.

In contrast, buying a cloud playout platform from a reputable vendor can provide a more straightforward and cost-effective solution. Vendors such as AWS Elemental and Azure Media Services offer pre-built platforms that are designed to meet the specific needs of broadcasters, with features such as NMOS (Networked Media Open Specification) for device discovery and registration, and AES67 for audio over IP. These platforms can also provide scalability, redundancy, and high availability, which can be difficult to achieve with a custom-built solution.

Comparing Cloud Playout Platforms

So, how do the leading cloud playout platforms compare? AWS Elemental is a popular choice among broadcasters, offering a range of features such as live encoding, streaming, and cloud-based playout. The platform also supports HLS (HTTP Live Streaming) and HEVC (High Efficiency Video Coding) for efficient video delivery.

Azure Media Services is another major player in the cloud playout market, offering a range of features such as live streaming, video on demand, and content protection. The platform also supports AV1 (AOMedia Video 1) for video compression, and SRT (Secure Reliable Transport) for secure and reliable video transport.

Harmonic and Grass Valley are also major vendors in the cloud playout market, offering a range of features such as cloud-based playout, live encoding, and streaming. Harmonic also offers support for RIST (Reliable Internet Stream Transport) for secure and reliable video transport, while Grass Valley offers support for NDI (Network Device Interface) for IP-based video transport.

Total Cost of Ownership

When evaluating the TCO of a cloud playout platform, broadcasters must consider a range of factors, including infrastructure costs, personnel costs, and operational costs. Building a custom platform can be extremely costly, with estimates suggesting that the TCO can be up to 5 times higher than buying a pre-built platform.

In contrast, buying a cloud playout platform from a reputable vendor can provide a more cost-effective solution. Vendors such as AWS Elemental and Azure Media Services offer pricing models that are based on usage, which can help to reduce costs and improve scalability.

The cloud playout market is becoming increasingly commoditized, with vendors competing on price and features. As a result, broadcasters are able to negotiate better deals and reduce their TCO. However, this also means that vendors must be able to demonstrate a clear return on investment (ROI) in order to remain competitive.

Latency, Redundancy, and Migration Path

When evaluating a cloud playout platform, broadcasters must also consider factors such as latency, redundancy, and migration path. Latency is a critical factor in live broadcasting, where delays of even a few seconds can be unacceptable. Vendors such as AWS Elemental and Azure Media Services offer features such as low-latency streaming and real-time encoding, which can help to minimize delays.

Redundancy is also a critical factor in cloud playout, where system failures can have significant consequences. Vendors such as Harmonic and Grass Valley offer features such as high availability and disaster recovery, which can help to ensure that systems remain operational even in the event of a failure.

Finally, the migration path is a critical consideration for broadcasters, where the transition to a new platform must be carefully planned and executed. Vendors such as AWS Elemental and Azure Media Services offer features such as hybrid cloud deployments and phased migration, which can help to minimize disruption and ensure a smooth transition.

By carefully evaluating these factors and considering the build vs. buy decision, broadcasters can make an informed choice about their cloud playout platform and ensure a successful transition to a cloud-based playout operation. As the broadcast industry continues to evolve, the importance of cloud playout will only continue to grow, and broadcasters must be prepared to adapt and innovate in order to remain competitive.

The future of cloud playout is likely to be shaped by emerging technologies such as 5G and edge computing, which will enable new use cases such as remote production and personalized advertising. As these technologies continue to mature, broadcasters will need to stay ahead of the curve and invest in the latest cloud playout platforms and technologies.

  • Key considerations for broadcasters when evaluating cloud playout platforms include TCO, latency, redundancy, and migration path.
  • Vendors such as AWS Elemental, Azure Media Services, Harmonic, and Grass Valley offer a range of features and pricing models to meet the needs of broadcasters.
  • Emerging technologies such as 5G and edge computing will shape the future of cloud playout and enable new use cases such as remote production and personalized advertising.

Ultimately, the build vs. buy decision will depend on the specific needs and requirements of each broadcaster. By carefully evaluating the options and considering the key factors, broadcasters can make an informed decision and ensure a successful transition to a cloud-based playout operation. The future of cloud playout is exciting and rapidly evolving, and broadcasters must be prepared to adapt and innovate in order to remain competitive.
