Cloud Playout in 2026: The Build vs. Buy Decision Every Broadcaster Faces

The economics of cloud playout platforms in 2026. Compare AWS Elemental, Azure Media Services, Harmonic, and Grass Valley. Focus on TCO, latency, redundancy, and the realistic migration path.

The Streamic Editorial Team March 22, 2026 Analysis

Cloud Playout in 2026: The Build vs. Buy Decision Every Broadcaster Faces

The migration to cloud playout has become a strategic imperative for broadcasters seeking to reduce costs, increase agility, and improve scalability. By 2030, it's expected that the majority of playout operations will be cloud-based, leveraging the benefits of Software as a Service (SaaS) and Platform as a Service (PaaS) models. However, the build vs. buy decision remains a critical consideration for broadcasters, with key factors including Total Cost of Ownership (TCO), latency, redundancy, and migration complexity.

AWS Elemental, Azure Media Services, Harmonic, and Grass Valley are among the leading cloud playout platform providers, each offering a range of solutions and pricing models. AWS Elemental, for example, provides a comprehensive cloud-based playout platform that supports SMPTE ST 2110 and AES67 for audio and video transport, as well as HLS and HEVC for streaming and compression. Azure Media Services, on the other hand, offers a more modular approach, with a range of services and APIs that can be integrated to create a customized playout platform.

TCO and Cost Considerations

When evaluating the TCO of a cloud playout platform, broadcasters must consider not only the upfront costs of deployment but also the ongoing expenses associated with storage, processing, and bandwidth.

The key to a successful cloud playout deployment is to carefully assess the costs of each component and to select a provider that offers a transparent and predictable pricing model, said John Matthews, Director of Engineering at a major broadcast network.
Harmonic, for example, offers a cloud-based playout platform that is priced based on a pay-as-you-go model, which can help broadcasters to better manage their costs and to scale their operations more efficiently.

In addition to the costs of the cloud playout platform itself, broadcasters must also consider the expenses associated with content preparation, quality control, and distribution. Grass Valley, for example, offers a range of tools and services that can help broadcasters to streamline their content preparation and quality control workflows, reducing the time and cost associated with these critical tasks. By leveraging AV1 and HEVC compression, broadcasters can also reduce their bandwidth costs and improve the overall efficiency of their distribution workflows.

Latency and Redundancy Considerations

Latency and redundancy are critical considerations for broadcasters evaluating cloud playout platforms. Latency refers to the delay between the time that a signal is transmitted and the time that it is received by the viewer, while redundancy refers to the ability of a system to continue operating in the event of a failure or outage. SRT and RIST are two protocols that are commonly used to ensure low-latency and reliable transmission of video signals over IP networks.

AWS Elemental, for example, offers a range of features and tools that can help broadcasters to minimize latency and to ensure redundancy, including NDI support for low-latency video transport and NMOS support for device discovery and control. Azure Media Services, on the other hand, offers a range of services and APIs that can be used to build custom redundancy and failover solutions, including load balancing and traffic management.

Migration Path and Complexity

The migration path to a cloud playout platform can be complex and time-consuming, requiring significant planning, testing, and validation. Broadcasters must carefully evaluate their existing workflows and systems, identifying areas where changes will be required and where new technologies and tools will need to be integrated.

The key to a successful migration is to take a phased approach, starting with a small pilot project and gradually scaling up to larger and more complex workflows, said Tom Burns, Chief Technology Officer at a major broadcast network.

Harmonic, for example, offers a range of services and tools that can help broadcasters to migrate their playout operations to the cloud, including professional services and training and support. Grass Valley, on the other hand, offers a range of solutions and platforms that can be used to create a hybrid cloud-based playout system, where some components are hosted in the cloud and others are hosted on-premises.

In addition to the technical challenges associated with migration, broadcasters must also consider the organizational and cultural implications of moving to a cloud-based playout platform. This can include changes to workflows, processes, and staff roles, as well as the need for new skills and training. By carefully planning and executing their migration strategy, broadcasters can minimize disruption and ensure a smooth transition to a cloud-based playout platform.

Real-World Deployments and Case Studies

Several broadcasters have already successfully migrated their playout operations to the cloud, leveraging the benefits of cloud-based platforms to reduce costs, increase agility, and improve scalability. One example is a major European broadcaster that migrated its playout operations to a cloud-based platform using AWS Elemental, reducing its costs by over 30% and improving its time-to-market by over 50%.

Another example is a major North American broadcaster that used Azure Media Services to create a cloud-based playout platform, leveraging the microservices architecture of Azure to create a highly scalable and flexible system. By using a combination of containerization and orchestration tools, the broadcaster was able to deploy and manage its cloud-based playout platform with ease, reducing its latency and improving its overall system reliability.

As the broadcast industry continues to evolve and migrate to cloud-based playout platforms, it's likely that we will see even more innovative and effective solutions emerge. By carefully evaluating their options and selecting the right cloud playout platform for their needs, broadcasters can ensure a successful migration and achieve their strategic goals, whether that's reducing costs, increasing agility, or improving scalability.
