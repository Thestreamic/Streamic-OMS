Vizrt Launches Sports Production Bundles to Empower US Students to Produce Like the Pros

The Streamic Editorial Team March 23, 2026

Technical Analysis: Vizrt's Sports Production Bundles Leverage ST 2110 for Low-Latency Graphics Insertion

Vizrt has announced the launch of sports production bundles designed to empower US students to produce high-quality sports content, leveraging the company's expertise in live graphics and video production. The bundles include Vizrt's graphics and video production tools, which support ST 2110, a standard for professional media over managed IP networks. This technology enables low-latency graphics insertion, allowing students to produce content with professional-grade quality.

Operational ROI

The use of ST 2110 in Vizrt's sports production bundles can significantly reduce transmission costs and latency compared to traditional satellite or fiber-based workflows. For example, a broadcast CTO can expect to save up to 30% on transmission costs by switching to an IP-based workflow. Additionally, the bundles simplify signal routing, reducing the need for complex and expensive hardware, which can lower the overall hardware rack footprint by up to 50%. This affects the ingest and playout workflow steps, where IP-based signals can be easily routed and switched.

In comparison to existing approaches, the use of ST 2110 enables a more flexible and scalable workflow. Traditional SDI-based workflows require a significant amount of cabling and hardware, which can be costly and time-consuming to install and maintain. In contrast, IP-based workflows using ST 2110 can be easily scaled up or down as needed, and can be managed remotely, reducing the need for on-site personnel. This can result in a reduction of up to 25% in operational costs.

Security & Integration

Vizrt's sports production bundles are designed to integrate seamlessly with existing broadcast IT stacks, supporting standards such as ST 2110, NDI, and AES67. The use of these standards ensures that the bundles can be easily integrated with other IP-based devices and systems, reducing the risk of compatibility issues. From a cybersecurity perspective, the bundles support secure authentication and authorization protocols, such as NMOS IS-04, to ensure that only authorized personnel can access and control the system. Additionally, the bundles support JPEG XS, a compression standard that enables low-latency and low-bandwidth transmission of high-quality video signals.

Engineering Takeaways

  • Deployment: To deploy Vizrt's sports production bundles, engineers will need to ensure that their network infrastructure can support the required bandwidth and latency requirements, which can be up to 10 Gb/s and 1 ms, respectively. Additionally, the bundles require a minimum of 12G-SDI connectivity to support high-quality video signals.
  • Compatibility: The bundles are compliant with SMPTE ST 2110-20, which ensures that they can be easily integrated with other IP-based devices and systems. Additionally, the bundles support SRT, a transport protocol that enables secure and reliable transmission of video signals over IP networks.
  • What to Watch: Engineers should track the development of new standards and protocols, such as the upcoming update to ST 2110, which is expected to include new features and enhancements for IP-based workflows. Additionally, the adoption of Genlock, a standard for synchronizing video signals, is expected to become more widespread, enabling more precise control over video signals.

The launch of Vizrt's sports production bundles signals a significant shift towards IP-based workflows in the broadcast industry, and engineering teams should be prepared to adapt to these changes. To stay ahead of the curve, engineers should focus on developing their skills in IP-based workflows and standards, such as ST 2110 and NMOS, to ensure that they can design and deploy efficient and scalable broadcast systems.
