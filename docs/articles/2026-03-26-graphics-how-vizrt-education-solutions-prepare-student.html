Why Real-Time Graphics Rendering Is Forcing Broadcasters to Rethink Live Production Architectures

Live broadcast production is under pressure to deliver higher-quality graphics and real-time rendering, all while maintaining ultra-low latency and frame-accurate signal transport. As a result, broadcasters are being forced to reassess their live production architectures and consider new technologies that can meet these demands.

What This Development Actually Means

The development of advanced graphics rendering solutions, such as those offered by Vizrt, has significant implications for live broadcast production. These solutions enable real-time rendering of complex graphics, such as 3D animations and virtual sets, which can be integrated into live broadcasts with minimal latency. The use of standards such as ST 2110 and NMOS IS-04/IS-05 enables the transport of these graphics over IP networks, allowing for greater flexibility and scalability in live production architectures.

The industry context for this development is the increasing demand for high-quality, engaging live content, particularly in the sports and news sectors. Broadcasters are under pressure to deliver live productions that are visually stunning and immersive, while also meeting the demands of a rapidly changing media landscape. The ability to render complex graphics in real-time is a key component of this, and is driving the adoption of new technologies and architectures in live broadcast production.

Why It Matters for Broadcast Operations

The ability to render complex graphics in real-time has significant implications for broadcast operations, particularly in terms of latency and signal transport. The use of IP networks and standards such as ST 2110 and NMOS IS-04/IS-05 enables the transport of graphics and video signals over long distances with minimal latency, making it possible to produce live broadcasts from remote locations. This is particularly relevant for sports and news productions, where the ability to deliver live, high-quality content from anywhere in the world is critical. Vendors such as Matrox, Riedel, and Evertz are already supporting these standards in their products, such as the Matrox ConvertIP gateway and the Riedel MediorNet network. Meanwhile, companies like Pebble Beach Systems and Harmonic are integrating real-time graphics rendering into their playout and encoding solutions, such as the Pebble Beach Systems Marina and the Harmonic VOS360.

Impact by Stakeholder

  • Regional and national broadcasters: Will need to budget for the deployment of IP-based live production architectures, including the purchase of new equipment and the training of staff. They can eliminate the need for traditional baseband video routing and reduce their transmission costs by using IP networks. For example, they can use the RIST protocol for reliable and secure transport of video signals over the internet.
  • OTT and streaming platforms: Will need to consider the impact of real-time graphics rendering on their content delivery networks (CDNs) and streaming protocols, such as HLS and DASH. They may need to optimize their ABR ladders and CDN cost per GB to ensure that they can deliver high-quality, graphics-rich content to their users. Companies like Telestream and AWS Elemental are already providing solutions for this, such as the Telestream Vantage and the AWS Elemental MediaLive.
  • Post-production and MAM teams: Will need to consider the implications of real-time graphics rendering for their ingest, QC, and archive workflows. They may need to invest in new equipment and software, such as the Blackmagic Design ATEM and the Adobe Creative Cloud, to support the rendering of complex graphics and the transport of IP signals.

Deep Insight: The Broader Industry Shift

The development of real-time graphics rendering solutions is part of a broader industry shift towards IP-based live production architectures. This shift is being driven by the need for greater flexibility, scalability, and cost-effectiveness in live production, as well as the increasing demand for high-quality, engaging content. The use of standards such as ST 2110 and NMOS IS-04/IS-05 is critical to this shift, as it enables the transport of IP signals over long distances with minimal latency. By 2025, we expect to see the widespread adoption of IP-based live production architectures, with the use of real-time graphics rendering and other advanced technologies becoming increasingly common. The vendors who will struggle in this environment are those who fail to adapt to the shift towards IP and the increasing demand for high-quality, engaging content. Companies like Sony and Grass Valley are already investing in IP-based solutions, such as the Sony XVS series and the Grass Valley Kayenne.
