Wave Central, EVS Announce SMPTE ST 2110 Interop Collaboration

Wave Central, EVS Announce SMPTE ST 2110 Interop Collaboration

The Streamic Editorial Team April 14, 2026 552 words · 3 min read Analysis
Source Attribution This analysis is based on publicly available reporting from: TV Technology (April 2026).
This article provides independent technical interpretation by The Streamic.

The Quick Hit

Wave Central and EVS announced a collaboration to enable SMPTE ST 2110 interoperability between their systems. This directly impacts the IP fabric that connects cameras, switchers, and distribution paths in live production environments [Source 1].

What Happened

  • Wave Central and EVS formalized a partnership to ensure SMPTE ST 2110 compatibility between their respective products [Source 1].
  • The collaboration targets the infrastructure layer—specifically signal routing and transport across IP networks [Source 1].
  • The initiative addresses timing, redundancy, and IP routing behavior, which are critical for live production workflows [Source 1].
[Source 1]

The Tech Spec

The collaboration focuses on SMPTE ST 2110, the suite of standards for professional media over managed IP networks. Key technical considerations include:
  • Timing: ST 2110 relies on Precision Time Protocol (PTP) for synchronization, ensuring all devices on the network share a common clock reference. The source does not specify whether the collaboration includes PTP profile alignment (e.g., SMPTE ST 2059-2) [Source 1].
  • Redundancy: The partnership likely addresses hitless failover mechanisms, though the source does not detail whether this involves ST 2022-7 seamless protection switching or another redundancy model [Source 1].
  • Routing: ST 2110 separates video, audio, and ancillary data into discrete essence streams, enabling independent routing. The collaboration may standardize how Wave Central and EVS systems discover, connect, and manage these streams, though the source does not confirm support for NMOS IS-04/IS-05 [Source 1].
  • Throughput: The source does not specify bandwidth requirements or whether the collaboration supports uncompressed 4K/UHD streams, JPEG XS compression, or other codec optimizations [Source 1].
[Source 1]

So What?

For broadcasters, this collaboration reduces integration friction between Wave Central and EVS systems in IP-based production environments. Key operational impacts include:
  • Simplified Workflows: Engineers can route video, audio, and metadata streams between Wave Central and EVS systems without SDI gateways or format conversions, potentially accelerating live production timelines [Source 1].
  • Infrastructure Flexibility: The partnership enables hybrid SDI/IP environments to migrate toward full IP without vendor lock-in, though the source does not clarify whether this extends to cloud or edge deployments [Source 1].
  • Risk Mitigation: Standardized ST 2110 interoperability may reduce latency and packet loss in live production, but the source does not quantify performance improvements or provide benchmarks [Source 1].
The collaboration signals broader industry alignment around ST 2110, but engineering teams must still validate timing accuracy, redundancy models, and routing behavior in their specific environments before deployment [Source 1]. [Source 1]

Blind Spots

  • The source does not specify which Wave Central or EVS products are included in the collaboration (e.g., EVS XT-VIA servers, Wave Central’s playout or automation systems).
  • No details are provided about PTP profile support (e.g., SMPTE ST 2059-2) or whether the collaboration includes timing synchronization for mixed-vendor environments.
  • The source does not confirm whether NMOS IS-04/IS-05 is supported for device discovery and connection management.
  • There is no mention of bandwidth requirements, codec support (e.g., JPEG XS), or whether the collaboration enables uncompressed 4K/UHD streams.
  • The source does not quantify performance metrics, such as latency, jitter, or packet loss thresholds, or provide benchmarks for real-world deployments.
  • No information is given about licensing, certification processes, or whether third-party testing (e.g., JT-NM) is involved.
