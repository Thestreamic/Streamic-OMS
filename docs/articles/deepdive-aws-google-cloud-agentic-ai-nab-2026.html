Infrastructure · Deep Dive 02From Bots to Agents: AWS & Google Cloud Solve the Newsroom

By The Streamic Editorial Team · 8 min read · April 18, 2026

2025 was the year of AI that created stuff. NAB 2026 is the year of agentic AI — AI that does stuff autonomously. AWS, Google Cloud, and Microsoft Azure all showed up with real deployment products, not demos. Here is what actually shipped.

01 · AWS Elemental InferenceThe Social Media Fixer

Walking through the North Hall at NAB 2026, it was impossible to miss the “AI” signs. But where 2025 was about AI that created stuff, 2026 is about agentic AI — AI that does stuff autonomously.

The standout official announcement from AWS is Elemental Inference, a fully managed service that works in parallel with live encoding. We’ve all seen auto-clipping before, but this is architecturally different. It’s an agent that monitors the live broadcast feed, recognises high-value moments — a goal, a breaking news quote, a key reaction — and then autonomously re-formats the clip for vertical social feeds, writes the caption in the station’s house voice, and queues it for human approval.

The output window is under ten seconds from live broadcast to queued social asset. For sports and news organisations, this collapses a workflow that previously required a dedicated social producer into a background service.

02 · The Google Cloud / Avid MarriageAgentic Search Inside Media Composer

The biggest show-floor surprise was the formal Google Cloud and Avid strategic partnership announcement. Google’s Gemini models are now integrated directly into Avid Media Composer, and Avid Content Core — the cloud-native SaaS platform built on Google Cloud’s BigQuery and Vertex AI — is generally available for global asset management.

The practical impact lives inside the editor’s daily workflow. Instead of an assistant editor spending six hours logging and tagging metadata on a shoot day’s rushes, an editor can now ask the system: “Find me every shot of the sunset where the presenter is wearing a blue tie.” The AI performs multimodal visual understanding against the cloud archive and returns the clips instantly.

This is what agentic search looks like in production: natural language against visual content, not keyword matching against manual metadata tags. The editor doesn’t learn a new tool; they keep Media Composer, but the cloud archive becomes conversationally navigable.

“2025 was the year of AI that created stuff. 2026 is the year of AI that does stuff. The difference is measured in hours saved per project, not demos watched per keynote.”

03 · Microsoft Azure’s PivotFrom Silent Backbone to Agentic Partner

Microsoft Azure’s NAB 2026 story is the quiet strategic pivot. Azure is moving away from being “the silent backbone” — the undifferentiated cloud that ran everyone else’s workloads — and repositioning as a partner-led agentic AI leader.

The demonstration piece is Copilot Studio for Media, showing how Copilot can replace commodity hardware with agent-to-agent workflows that handle newsroom tasks, metadata extraction, and archive search. The subtext is sharper than the demo: Azure is willing to compete for the workflow layer, not just the compute layer underneath.

04 · The Wider Agentic MapAmagi, Imagine, Evertz, EVS

The hyperscalers aren’t alone. Across the show floor, established broadcast vendors are shipping their own agentic solutions on top of these cloud substrates:

Amagi Newspulse is an agentic AI platform that autonomously scans live news and VOD to package social-ready clips and bulletins in real-time. Imagine Communications has added AI-assisted scheduling to the Landmark platform, automating non-prime scheduling (overnights, off-peak) using machine learning. Evertz is showcasing cloud-native live event management for dynamic market switching across distributed hybrid workflows. EVS Tactiq is a modular software-defined control interface that decouples the operator from the physical hardware — any operator can control any room from any location.

05 · What Changes for NewsroomsControl and Visibility Become the Product

The common operational thread at NAB 2026 is control and visibility. Broadcasters are no longer trying to move everything to the cloud. Hybrid is not a transition; it is the destination. High-value continuous workflows — main channel playout, live event production — stay on-prem or on private compute. Cloud becomes the burst capacity layer: pop-up channels, sports betting feeds, disaster recovery, social clipping, archive search.

The new competitive surface is the single pane of glass that manages both estates as one virtual broadcast facility. The vendor who wins that orchestration layer — and the agentic AI that runs on top of it — wins the 2026 newsroom. AWS, Google, and Microsoft are all making credible claims. The broadcasters betting correctly are the ones treating this as an architecture decision, not a vendor decision.
