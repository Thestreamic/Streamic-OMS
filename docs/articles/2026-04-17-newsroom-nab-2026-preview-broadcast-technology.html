NAB 2026 Preview: What Broadcast Engineers Should Be Watching This Week

NAB Show 2026 opens this week. From Avid's agentic AI partnership with Google Cloud to Haivision's multi-codec Makito ONE, here are the technology trends for broadcast engineers.

The Streamic Editorial Team April 17, 2026 439 words · 2 min read Analysis

The Quick Hit

NAB Show 2026 opens at the Las Vegas Convention Center on April 18-22, and the pre-show announcements are already signalling where vendor investment and engineering attention are converging. Three themes dominate: agentic AI entering post-production workflows, multi-codec encoding platforms that bridge SDI and ST 2110 in a single blade, and 5G mobile contribution moving from demonstration to production deployment. For broadcast engineering teams, the question at this NAB is no longer whether these technologies work. It is whether they integrate cleanly into existing operational architectures without requiring a full infrastructure refresh.

Technical Deep Dive

The Avid-Google Cloud partnership, announced April 16, is the most architecturally significant announcement heading into the show. Embedding Gemini models and Vertex AI into Media Composer and the new cloud-native Content Core platform targets a real operational pain point: media discovery in large-scale productions. Natural-language search across media bins, automated metadata generation, and agentic workflows represent a meaningful step beyond the auto-tagging features that MAM vendors have been shipping since 2023.

On the contribution side, Haivision's Makito ONE consolidates encoding and decoding with H.264, HEVC, and JPEG XS plus SDI and ST 2110 connectivity on a single hot-swappable blade. Haivision's Falkon X4 mobile transmitter pushes 5G contribution further with a four-modem architecture providing eight cellular antennas for high-throughput stability in congested RF environments. Blackmagic Design's DaVinci Resolve 21 continues the company's strategy of bundling professional colour grading, editing, VFX, and audio tools into a single application at zero per-seat licensing cost. Grass Valley is expanding its LDX camera portfolio with the LDX 110 for triple-speed HD capture and demonstrating its Media Universe ecosystem.

Deployment Roadmap

For engineering teams heading to NAB this week, the practical evaluation agenda should prioritise three areas. First, cloud dependency: the Avid-Google Cloud announcement raises questions about data residency, egress costs, and hybrid architecture support. Second, codec consolidation: the Makito ONE's multi-codec approach is only valuable if it supports the specific profiles and interface standards your facility requires. Third, 5G contribution: if your operation relies on mobile video for news gathering or sports, the Falkon X4's four-modem architecture deserves hands-on evaluation.

Operational Wins

The broader trend emerging from pre-NAB announcements is convergence: convergence of encode and decode functions, convergence of SDI and IP connectivity, convergence of manual editing and AI-assisted workflows, and convergence of on-premises and cloud infrastructure. For engineering teams, convergence means fewer boxes in the rack, fewer management interfaces to maintain, and fewer vendor-specific integration headaches. The risk is vendor lock-in, and the vendors making the strongest convergence arguments are the ones engineering teams should interrogate most carefully about open standards, API access, and exit paths.
