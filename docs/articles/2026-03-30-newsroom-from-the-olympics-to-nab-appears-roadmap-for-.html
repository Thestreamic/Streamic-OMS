From the Olympics to NAB: Appear’s Roadmap for the All-IP Era

From the Olympics to NAB: Appear’s Roadmap for the All-IP Era

The Streamic Editorial Team March 30, 2026

Why Broadcasters Must Rethink Their IP Signal Transport Strategies

Live signal transport is at a critical inflection point, driven by the increasing adoption of IP-based workflows and the need for lower latency. The Olympics and NAB have showcased the capabilities of all-IP signal transport, but the industry is still grappling with the operational implications. Broadcasters must reassess their IP signal transport strategies to ensure they can deliver high-quality, low-latency content to their audiences.

What This Development Actually Means

Appear has outlined its roadmap for the all-IP era, emphasizing the importance of ST 2110 and NMOS in enabling seamless signal transport and discovery. The company's focus on JPEG XS and AES67 highlights the need for high-quality, low-latency transport over IP networks. By leveraging these standards, Appear aims to simplify signal routing and reduce hardware rack footprints for broadcasters.

This development responds to the growing pressure on broadcasters to reduce latency and improve signal integrity. The shift to IP-based workflows has created new opportunities for innovation, but it also introduces complex technical challenges that must be addressed.

Why It Matters for Broadcast Operations

The adoption of all-IP signal transport will have significant operational implications for broadcasters. One of the key challenges will be integrating IP gateways from vendors like Matrox ConvertIP and Riedel MediorNet with existing ST 2110 fabrics. This will require careful planning and testing to ensure seamless signal transport and discovery. Additionally, broadcasters will need to consider the impact on their CapEx and OpEx budgets, as well as the potential for reduced transmission costs and simplified signal routing.

For OTT and streaming platforms, the shift to all-IP signal transport will require significant changes to their ABR ladder and CDN cost per GB. This will involve re-evaluating their SSAI complexity and latency budgets to ensure they can deliver high-quality content to their audiences. Post-production and MAM teams will also need to adapt to the new workflows, with a focus on ingest, QC, and archive operations.

Impact by Stakeholder

  • Regional and national broadcasters: Will need to budget for IP gateways and ST 2110 fabrics, but can eliminate the need for satellite and fibre transmission. Reduced transmission costs and simplified signal routing will be key benefits.
  • OTT and streaming platforms: Will need to re-evaluate their ABR ladder and CDN cost per GB, with a focus on reducing SSAI complexity and latency budgets. This will involve investing in new infrastructure and workflows.
  • Post-production and MAM teams: Will need to adapt to new ingest, QC, and archive workflows, with a focus on ensuring high-quality content is delivered to audiences.
Analysis: As I see it, the push for ST 2110 adoption will inevitably lead to a surge in NMOS device management complexity. Broadcast CTOs will need to contend with the added overhead of dynamic device discovery and registration, potentially overwhelming their existing network infrastructure unless they implement robust NMOS management and monitoring tools.
