IABM to Host Breakfast Event at 2026 NAB Show

IABM to Host Breakfast Event at 2026 NAB Show

The Streamic Editorial Team March 25, 2026 909 words · 5 min read

Why Real-Time Transport is Forcing Broadcasters to Rethink Signal Integrity

Live signal transport is under pressure to deliver frame-accurate video and audio with minimal latency. As broadcasters move towards IP-based infrastructures, the need for reliable and efficient real-time transport protocols has become a top priority. With the increasing demand for high-quality live content, broadcasters must ensure that their signal transport systems can handle the complexity of modern production workflows.

What This Development Actually Means

The IABM's decision to host a breakfast event at the 2026 NAB Show signals a growing interest in the broadcast industry's shift towards IP-based infrastructures and real-time transport protocols. This development is centered around the adoption of standards such as ST 2110, which provides a framework for the transport of professional media over managed IP networks. The event will likely focus on the latest advancements in IP signal transport, including the use of protocols like SRT, NDI, and RIST, as well as the integration of these technologies with existing broadcast systems.

The industry context for this development is the increasing demand for live content and the need for broadcasters to reduce latency and improve signal integrity. With the rise of online streaming and social media, broadcasters must be able to deliver high-quality live content to a wide range of platforms and devices, while also ensuring that their signals are robust and reliable. This has led to a growing interest in real-time transport protocols and IP-based infrastructures, as well as a need for industry events and discussions that can help broadcasters navigate this complex and rapidly changing landscape.

Why It Matters for Broadcast Operations

The adoption of real-time transport protocols and IP-based infrastructures will have significant implications for broadcast operations over the next 12-18 months. Broadcasters will need to assess their current signal transport systems and determine whether they are compatible with the latest protocols and standards, such as ST 2110 and NMOS. This may involve upgrading or replacing existing equipment, such as IP gateways from vendors like Matrox or Riedel, or investing in new technologies like JPEG XS or AES67. Additionally, broadcasters will need to consider the integration of these systems with their existing workflows and infrastructure, including their MAM systems and playout platforms from vendors like Avid or Pebble Beach Systems.

Impact by Stakeholder

  • Regional and national broadcasters: Will need to budget for the upgrade or replacement of existing signal transport systems to ensure compatibility with the latest protocols and standards. This may involve significant CapEx investments, but will also provide opportunities for reduced transmission costs and improved signal integrity. For example, broadcasters can eliminate the need for expensive satellite or fiber links by using IP-based transport protocols like SRT or RIST.
  • OTT and streaming platforms: Will need to assess the impact of real-time transport protocols on their ABR ladder and CDN cost per GB. The use of protocols like SRT or NDI can help reduce latency and improve signal integrity, but may also require changes to their existing workflows and infrastructure. For example, OTT platforms may need to upgrade their encoding and packaging systems to support the latest codecs and container formats.
  • Post-production and MAM teams: Will need to consider the implications of real-time transport protocols for their ingest, QC, and archive workflows. The use of IP-based infrastructures and protocols like ST 2110 can provide significant benefits in terms of flexibility and scalability, but may also require changes to their existing systems and workflows. For example, post-production teams may need to invest in new equipment or software to support the latest codecs and formats.

Deep Insight: The Broader Industry Shift

The adoption of real-time transport protocols and IP-based infrastructures is part of a broader industry shift towards more flexible and scalable broadcast systems. This shift is driven by the need for broadcasters to deliver high-quality live content to a wide range of platforms and devices, while also reducing latency and improving signal integrity. The industry is moving away from traditional SDI-based systems and towards IP-based infrastructures, which provide greater flexibility and scalability. By 2028, we expect to see widespread adoption of IP-based infrastructures and real-time transport protocols, with vendors like Riedel and Matrox leading the charge. The real risk is not the adoption of new technologies, but rather the failure to invest in the necessary infrastructure and training to support these systems.
