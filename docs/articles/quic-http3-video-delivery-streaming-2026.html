Beyond TCP: Why QUIC Is Redefining Video Delivery

Faster video start, fewer buffering issues, and smoother playback — why HTTP/3 and QUIC are quietly changing how modern streaming works across broadcast and OTT delivery.

By Prerak K Mehta March 2026 · 5 min read

The way video is delivered over the internet is undergoing a fundamental shift. For decades, streaming relied on TCP — a protocol built for reliability, but not specifically for real-time media.

Why TCP Is No Longer Enough

While dependable, TCP comes with structural limitations for modern high-bitrate streaming:

  • Handshake Latency: Connection setup takes multiple steps before data flows.
  • Head-of-Line Blocking: One lost packet stalls the entire stream, causing buffering.
  • Network Fragility: Switching from Wi-Fi to 5G often breaks the active session.

The Multi-Lane Logic

Think of TCP as a single-lane road where one stalled car stops everyone. QUIC behaves like a multi-lane highway; if one data stream hits a "pothole," the other lanes keep moving at full speed.

Real-World Benefits

OTT & Playback

Faster startup means users start watching instantly without the "spinning wheel" of death.

Mobile Mobility

Seamless handoffs between Wi-Fi and Cellular without stream interruptions.

Technical Comparison

Feature TCP (HTTP/2) QUIC (HTTP/3)
Connection Setup Slow multi-step Near-Instant
Security Separate TLS Always Encrypted
Resilience Vulnerable to loss Highly Robust
