Harmonic Delivers Winning Live Sports Streaming Innovations

Harmonic Delivers Winning Live Sports Streaming Innovations

The Streamic Editorial Team March 26, 2026 496 words · 2 min read

Why Cloud-Native Live Sports Streaming Is Forcing Broadcasters to Rethink Their Infrastructure

Live sports streaming has become a critical component of the broadcasting landscape, with millions of viewers tuning in to watch their favorite teams and players in action. As the demand for high-quality, low-latency streaming continues to grow, broadcasters are under pressure to deliver. One short sentence sums it up: scalability is key. The ability to handle massive volumes of data, scale up or down as needed, and ensure that content is delivered quickly and efficiently is crucial for broadcasters looking to stay ahead of the competition, and this is where cloud-native live sports streaming comes into play, offering a range of benefits including reduced operational overhead, faster content delivery, and lower infrastructure costs.

What This Development Actually Means

Harmonic has announced a range of innovations in live sports streaming, including the use of cloud-native technologies such as microservices and Kubernetes to enable more efficient and scalable delivery of live content. The company's VOS360 platform is a cloud-based solution that allows broadcasters to ingest, process, and deliver live video content in a variety of formats, including HLS and DASH, and it supports a range of codecs, including HEVC and AV1. This development is significant because it responds to the growing trend towards cloud-based workflows and the need for more efficient and cost-effective delivery of live sports content.

The industry context for this development is the increasing demand for live sports streaming and the need for broadcasters to deliver high-quality, low-latency content to a wide range of devices. With the rise of OTT platforms and social media, broadcasters are under pressure to provide a seamless viewing experience across multiple platforms, and this is driving the adoption of cloud-native technologies and more efficient delivery protocols such as SRT and RIST.

Why It Matters for Broadcast Operations

The move towards cloud-native live sports streaming has significant implications for broadcast operations, particularly in terms of infrastructure decisions and CapEx/OpEx trade-offs. Broadcasters will need to consider the costs and benefits of migrating to cloud-based workflows, including the potential for reduced operational overhead, faster content delivery, and lower infrastructure costs. For example, a broadcaster like BBC or Sky might consider using a cloud-based platform like Amazon S3 or Google Cloud Storage to store and process their live sports content, and then use a vendor like Harmonic or Telestream to deliver that content to a range of devices. Alternatively, a platform like DAZN or ESPN might use a cloud-native solution like Kubernetes or Docker to manage their live sports workflows and ensure low-latency delivery to a global audience.
