NAB Show: Ross Video To Demonstrate MXL and Dynamic Media Facility Workflows

NAB Show: Ross Video To Demonstrate MXL and Dynamic Media Facility Workflows

The Streamic Editorial Team April 13, 2026 571 words · 3 min read Analysis
Source Attribution This analysis is based on publicly available reporting from: TV Technology (April 2026).
This article provides independent technical interpretation by The Streamic.

The Quick Hit

Ross Video is showing off a new way to route and manage live video signals at NAB, using both old-school SDI and modern IP setups—think of it like upgrading from a patchbay to a smart traffic controller for your broadcast gear.

What Happened

  • Ross Video will demonstrate its MXL and Dynamic Media Facility Workflows at NAB Show 2026, focusing on infrastructure-level signal routing and transport [Source 1].
  • The demos will cover both ST 2110 (IP-based video transport) and SDI (traditional serial digital interface) fabrics, connecting cameras, production switchers, graphics engines, and distribution paths [Source 1].
  • The company is framing this as a practical upgrade for engineering teams, with implications for cost, throughput, reliability, and editorial speed [Source 1].

The Tech Spec

Ross Video’s NAB demos center on two key components: MXL and its Dynamic Media Facility Workflows. The source doesn’t specify what "MXL" stands for, but it appears to be a system for managing signal routing at the infrastructure level—essentially the backbone that ties together cameras, switchers, and graphics engines [Source 1].

The workflows support both ST 2110 (think of it like Ethernet for video, where each signal—video, audio, metadata—travels as separate IP packets) and SDI (like a dedicated coaxial cable for each video feed), giving broadcasters flexibility to mix legacy and modern gear [Source 1]. The focus is on timing, redundancy, and IP routing behavior—critical factors for live production where dropped frames or latency can derail a broadcast [Source 1].

The source doesn’t detail specific hardware or software products, but the emphasis on "infrastructure level" suggests this isn’t a standalone tool but rather a framework for integrating existing Ross Video gear (or third-party systems) into a unified signal fabric [Source 1].

So What? (The Human Impact)

For broadcast engineers and operations leads, this announcement signals a potential shift in how signals move through a facility. If Ross Video’s demos deliver on their promise, teams could see measurable improvements in three areas:

  • Reliability: Redundant IP routing might reduce single points of failure compared to traditional SDI setups, where a single cable cut can take a feed offline [Source 1].
  • Flexibility: Mixing ST 2110 and SDI could let broadcasters phase in IP upgrades without scrapping existing gear, lowering the cost of transitioning to all-IP workflows [Source 1].
  • Editorial Speed: Dynamic routing might simplify ad-hoc production changes—like rerouting a camera feed to a graphics engine mid-show—without manual patching [Source 1].

That said, the source doesn’t quantify these benefits (e.g., latency reductions or cost savings), so engineering leads will need to test the workflows against their own setups before committing [Source 1].

Blind Spots

  • The source doesn’t specify what MXL stands for or whether it’s a new product, an update to an existing system, or a conceptual framework [Source 1].
  • No details on compatibility: Does this work only with Ross Video gear, or can it integrate with third-party switchers, cameras, or graphics engines? [Source 1]
  • The source mentions "measurable" impacts on cost, throughput, and reliability but doesn’t provide benchmarks, case studies, or customer testimonials [Source 1].
  • No pricing or licensing model is disclosed—is this a one-time purchase, subscription, or tied to existing Ross Video contracts? [Source 1]
  • The source doesn’t clarify whether these workflows are production-ready or still in a demo/beta phase [Source 1].
