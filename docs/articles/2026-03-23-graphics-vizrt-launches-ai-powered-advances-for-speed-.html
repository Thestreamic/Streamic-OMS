Technical Analysis: Vizrt Integrates JPEG XS for Low-Latency Graphics Transport in Live Broadcasts

Vizrt has announced the integration of JPEG XS compression into their graphics processing workflow, enabling low-latency and high-quality video transport for live broadcasts. This development involves the use of JPEG XS, a compression standard designed for low-latency and high-quality video transport, in conjunction with Vizrt's graphics processing technology. The integration is set to improve the efficiency and quality of live broadcast graphics transport.

Operational ROI

The integration of JPEG XS into Vizrt's graphics processing workflow is expected to save broadcast CTOs significant costs and time by reducing transmission costs and latency. With JPEG XS, broadcast engineers can transport high-quality video signals over existing network infrastructure, reducing the need for costly and bulky SDI cabling. This affects the signal routing workflow step, where JPEG XS enables simplified signal routing and reduced hardware rack footprint. For example, a single 10GbE link can transport multiple JPEG XS compressed video signals, reducing the overall cost and complexity of signal transport.

In comparison to the existing approach of using uncompressed SDI or other compression standards, JPEG XS offers lower latency and higher quality video transport. This change operationally means that broadcast engineers can now transport high-quality video signals over long distances without compromising on latency or quality, making it ideal for live broadcast applications. Additionally, the use of JPEG XS compression reduces the bitrate required for video transport, resulting in lower transmission costs and improved network efficiency.

Security & Integration

The integration of JPEG XS into Vizrt's graphics processing workflow is designed to fit into existing broadcast IT stacks, with a focus on security and compliance with relevant standards. The use of JPEG XS compression is compliant with the SMPTE ST 2110 standard for professional media over managed IP networks, ensuring seamless integration with other ST 2110-compliant devices. Additionally, the integration is designed to work with NMOS (Networked Media Open Specifications) protocols, such as NMOS IS-04, for device discovery and registration. The use of AES67 audio over IP protocol also ensures that audio signals are transported securely and in sync with the video signals.

Engineering Takeaways

  • Deployment: The deployment of JPEG XS compression in Vizrt's graphics processing workflow requires a minimum of 10GbE network infrastructure and compatible hardware, such as JPEG XS-enabled encoders and decoders.
  • Compatibility: The integration is compliant with SMPTE ST 2110-20 and NMOS IS-04, ensuring seamless integration with other ST 2110-compliant devices and NMOS-enabled systems.
  • What to Watch: Engineers should track the upcoming updates to the SMPTE ST 2110 standard, which is expected to include new specifications for JPEG XS compression and other emerging technologies.

The integration of JPEG XS into Vizrt's graphics processing workflow signals a significant shift towards low-latency and high-quality video transport in live broadcasts. Engineering teams should assess their current infrastructure and workflows to determine the feasibility of deploying JPEG XS compression, and plan for future upgrades and integrations with other ST 2110-compliant devices.

Analysis: Vizrt's AI-powered media management supports ST 2110, enabling efficient IP-based video transport with reduced latency and optimized bitrate for live broadcast workflows.
