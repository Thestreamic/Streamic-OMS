NEP Group Launches NEP Platform for Software-Based Live Media Production

Software-Based Live Production Shifts Broadcast Infrastructure Priorities

The launch of software-based live media production platforms is a significant development for broadcast infrastructure, as it promises to reduce latency, increase flexibility, and lower costs. For engineers, this means reevaluating the role of traditional hardware-based systems and considering the implications of software-based solutions on signal transport, processing, and distribution. As the industry moves towards more agile and adaptable production workflows, the importance of standards like ST 2110, NDI, and SRT will only continue to grow.

Domain Signal Extraction

  • Broadcast technologies: ST 2110, NDI, SRT, JPEG XS, and AES67 are all relevant to this development, as they enable the transport and processing of live signals in a software-based environment. Adjacent technologies like 12G-SDI and Genlock will also be impacted, as software-based systems begin to replace traditional hardware.
  • Architecture layer: This development primarily affects the signal transport and processing layers of the media chain, where software-based systems can provide greater flexibility and scalability. The impact on the encoding pipeline, SSAI insertion, and playout automation will also be significant, as software-based solutions enable more efficient and adaptable workflows.
  • Cybersecurity surface: The shift to software-based live production introduces new cybersecurity risks, particularly around the potential for malware and ransomware attacks on software-based systems. However, the use of standards like ST 2110 and AES67 can help mitigate these risks by providing a secure and reliable framework for signal transport and processing.
  • Business signal: The underlying commercial pressure driving this development is the need for greater efficiency, flexibility, and cost savings in live production workflows. As the demand for high-quality, low-latency live content continues to grow, broadcasters and streaming platforms are looking for ways to reduce costs and improve productivity, without compromising on quality or reliability.

Technology Intelligence

The launch of software-based live media production platforms is a significant development for the broadcast industry, as it enables the creation of more agile and adaptable production workflows. The use of standards like ST 2110 and NDI provides a secure and reliable framework for signal transport and processing, while the adoption of SRT and JPEG XS enables the efficient and high-quality transport of live signals over IP networks. However, the shift to software-based systems also introduces new challenges, particularly around the potential for latency and packet loss, which must be carefully managed to ensure high-quality and reliable signal transport. In practice, this means that engineers will need to carefully evaluate the trade-offs between different technologies and architectures, in order to create workflows that meet the demands of high-quality, low-latency live production. For example, the use of ST 2110 and AES67 can provide a high level of quality and reliability, but may require more complex and expensive infrastructure, while the use of SRT and JPEG XS can provide greater efficiency and cost savings, but may introduce new risks around latency and packet loss.

Why This Matters

  • Broadcasters: The shift to software-based live production will have a significant impact on broadcasters, particularly in terms of reduced transmission costs, lower latency, and simplified signal routing. Regional and national broadcasters will be able to take advantage of these benefits to improve the quality and reliability of their live productions, while sports rights holders will be able to use software-based systems to create more agile and adaptable workflows for live event production.
  • Streaming platforms: The adoption of software-based live media production platforms will also have a significant impact on streaming platforms, particularly in terms of the ability to create high-quality, low-latency live content. OTT operators will be able to use software-based systems to improve the efficiency and scalability of their live production workflows, while also reducing costs and improving the quality of their live streams.
  • Cybersecurity risk: The shift to software-based live production introduces new cybersecurity risks, particularly around the potential for malware and ransomware attacks on software-based systems. However, the use of standards like ST 2110 and AES67 can help mitigate these risks by providing a secure and reliable framework for signal transport and processing.
  • Market impact: The launch of software-based live media production platforms will have a significant impact on the broadcast industry, particularly in terms of the vendors and technologies that will be used to support live production workflows. Companies like Sony, Grass Valley, and Imagine Communications will be well-positioned to take advantage of this trend, while others may struggle to adapt to the changing landscape.
