Miri V410 Live 4K Encoder/Decoder to Make North American Debut at 2026 NAB Show

Miri V410 Live 4K Encoder/Decoder to Make North American Debut at 2026 NAB Show

The Streamic Editorial Team March 23, 2026

Technical Analysis: Miri V410 Live 4K Encoder/Decoder Reduces Latency to 1ms with JPEG XS Compression

The Miri V410 Live 4K Encoder/Decoder is set to make its North American debut at the 2026 NAB Show, announced by the company as a solution for low-latency live signal transport. This technology utilizes JPEG XS compression to achieve a latency of 1ms, making it suitable for real-time applications. The device supports 12G-SDI and ST 2110 interfaces, ensuring compatibility with existing broadcast infrastructure.

Operational ROI

The Miri V410 Live 4K Encoder/Decoder offers significant operational savings for broadcast CTOs, primarily through reduced transmission costs. By utilizing JPEG XS compression, the bitrate required for 4K transmission is significantly lower than traditional uncompressed or lightly compressed methods, resulting in cost savings of up to 50% compared to satellite or fiber transmission. This affects the signal contribution and distribution workflow step, where the reduced bitrate enables more efficient use of available bandwidth. Additionally, the device's compact form factor reduces the hardware rack footprint, leading to lower power consumption and cooling costs.

In comparison to existing approaches, the Miri V410 Live 4K Encoder/Decoder simplifies signal routing by supporting both 12G-SDI and ST 2110 interfaces, allowing for easy integration into existing SDI-based infrastructures while also providing a path for migration to IP-based systems. This flexibility reduces the complexity and cost associated with signal routing and switching, making it an attractive solution for broadcast engineers. Furthermore, the device's low latency of 1ms enables real-time interaction and feedback, which is critical for live applications such as sports and news broadcasts.

Security & Integration

The Miri V410 Live 4K Encoder/Decoder is designed to fit into existing broadcast IT stacks, with support for ST 2110, AES67, and NMOS IS-04, ensuring seamless integration with IP-based systems. The device's use of JPEG XS compression also ensures compliance with relevant standards for low-latency video transport. From a cybersecurity perspective, the device's support for RIST (Reliable Internet Stream Transport) protocol ensures secure and reliable transport of live video over IP networks. Additionally, the device's compliance with SMPTE ST 2110-20 ensures that it can be easily integrated into existing IP-based broadcast systems, reducing the risk of compatibility issues and ensuring a smooth transition to IP-based workflows.

Engineering Takeaways

  • Deployment: The Miri V410 Live 4K Encoder/Decoder requires a minimum network bandwidth of 10GbE to support 4K video transport, and it is recommended to use a dedicated network switch to ensure reliable and low-latency transmission. Additionally, the device supports Genlock synchronization, ensuring frame-accurate video transport.
  • Compatibility: The device is compliant with SMPTE ST 2110-20, NMOS IS-04, and SRT (Secure Reliable Transport) protocol, ensuring compatibility with a wide range of IP-based broadcast systems and devices.
  • What to Watch: Engineers should track the upcoming updates to the JPEG XS standard, which is expected to include new features such as improved compression efficiency and support for higher resolutions, which will further enhance the capabilities of the Miri V410 Live 4K Encoder/Decoder.

The introduction of the Miri V410 Live 4K Encoder/Decoder signals a significant shift in the market towards low-latency, IP-based live signal transport solutions. Engineering teams should evaluate this technology as a potential solution for their live production workflows, considering its compatibility with existing infrastructure and its potential to reduce transmission costs and improve signal quality.

Analysis: Miri V410 supports ST 2110, enabling low-latency 4K video transport over IP networks with bitrates up to 50 Gbps, streamlining live broadcast workflows.
