2026 NAB Show Exhibitor Insight: Netgear

2026 NAB Show Exhibitor Insight: Netgear

The Streamic Editorial Team March 27, 2026

Why Real-Time Transport Is Forcing Broadcasters to Rethink Signal Integrity

Live signal transport is under pressure to deliver frame-accurate video and audio with minimal latency, and this shift is being driven by the increasing adoption of IP-based infrastructures. As broadcasters move away from traditional SDI-based systems, they're facing new challenges in maintaining signal integrity. The need for reliable, low-latency transport is becoming a critical factor in the design of modern broadcast systems.

What This Development Actually Means

The recent announcement from Netgear showcases their latest advancements in real-time transport solutions, specifically designed to address the challenges of live signal transport over IP networks. Their new product line supports ST 2110, NDI, and SRT, allowing for flexible and scalable signal transport architectures. This development is significant, as it responds to the growing demand for reliable and low-latency signal transport in live production environments.

The industry is currently witnessing a significant shift towards IP-based infrastructures, driven by the need for greater flexibility, scalability, and cost-effectiveness. As a result, broadcasters are seeking solutions that can ensure reliable and high-quality signal transport, while also meeting the demands of modern live production workflows.

Why It Matters for Broadcast Operations

The impact of this development on broadcast operations will be significant, as it enables engineers to design and deploy more efficient and scalable signal transport architectures. For example, the use of ST 2110 and NDI can simplify signal routing and reduce the complexity of traditional SDI-based systems. Additionally, the integration of SRT can provide a reliable and low-latency transport mechanism for live signals over IP networks. Vendors such as Matrox, Riedel, and Evertz are already providing solutions that support these emerging standards, and broadcasters will need to evaluate these options carefully to ensure seamless integration with their existing infrastructures. The trade-offs between CapEx and OpEx will also be critical, as broadcasters weigh the costs of deploying new IP-based systems against the benefits of improved flexibility and scalability.

Impact by Stakeholder

  • Regional and national broadcasters: Will need to budget for the deployment of new IP-based signal transport solutions, which will require significant investments in infrastructure and training. However, they can eliminate the need for traditional SDI-based systems, reducing hardware rack footprint and simplifying signal routing.
  • OTT and streaming platforms: Will need to reassess their ABR ladder and CDN cost per GB, as the use of SRT and other low-latency transport mechanisms can reduce the latency budget and improve the overall quality of experience for end-users.
  • Post-production and MAM teams: Will need to evaluate the implications of IP-based signal transport on their ingest, QC, and archive workflows
