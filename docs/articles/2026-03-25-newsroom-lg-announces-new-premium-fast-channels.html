LG Announces New Premium FAST Channels

LG Announces New Premium FAST Channels

The Streamic Editorial Team March 25, 2026 888 words · 4 min read

Why Premium FAST Channels Are Forcing Broadcasters to Rethink Live Signal Transport

As the demand for high-quality, low-latency live streaming continues to grow, broadcasters are under increasing pressure to deliver flawless video experiences to their audiences.

The shift towards premium FAST (Free Ad-Supported Streaming TV) channels has brought new challenges to the forefront, particularly when it comes to live signal transport and latency. With the rise of online streaming, viewers expect broadcast-quality video with minimal delay, making it essential for broadcasters to reassess their signal transport infrastructure. The complexity of managing multiple channels, each with its own set of requirements, has become a significant operational challenge.

What This Development Actually Means

LG's announcement of new premium FAST channels marks a significant development in the live streaming landscape, as it underscores the growing importance of high-quality, low-latency video delivery. The company's focus on premium content is likely to drive demand for more efficient and reliable signal transport solutions, such as those utilizing ST 2110, NDI, or SRT protocols. As a result, broadcasters will need to evaluate their existing infrastructure and consider upgrades to support the increased demand for high-quality live streaming.

This development is part of a broader industry trend towards IP-based signal transport, driven by the need for greater flexibility, scalability, and cost-effectiveness. The use of standards like ST 2110, AES67, and RIST is becoming increasingly prevalent, as they enable broadcasters to build more efficient and reliable signal transport networks. The current landscape is characterized by a mix of traditional broadcast infrastructure and emerging IP-based technologies, with vendors like Matrox, Riedel, and Evertz playing a crucial role in shaping the future of live signal transport.

Why It Matters for Broadcast Operations

The emergence of premium FAST channels has significant implications for broadcast operations, particularly in terms of live signal transport and latency. Broadcasters will need to invest in infrastructure that can support the delivery of high-quality, low-latency video, which may involve upgrading their existing signal transport networks to support IP-based protocols like ST 2110 or SRT. This, in turn, will require careful evaluation of CapEx and OpEx trade-offs, as well as consideration of the potential impact on workflow complexity and competitive dynamics. Vendors like Harmonic, with their VOS360 platform, and Telestream, with their Vantage system, are well-positioned to support broadcasters in this transition, offering solutions that can help optimize signal transport and reduce latency.

Impact by Stakeholder

  • Regional and national broadcasters: Will need to budget for upgrades to their signal transport infrastructure, including the potential replacement of traditional SDI-based systems with IP-based solutions. This may involve investing in new equipment, such as IP gateways from Matrox or Riedel, and training staff to manage the new infrastructure. By doing so, they can eliminate the need for costly satellite or fiber transmissions and reduce their hardware rack footprint.
  • OTT and streaming platforms: Will need to reassess their content delivery networks (CDNs) and consider optimizations to their ABR ladder, SSAI complexity, and latency budget to support the growing demand for high-quality live streaming. This may involve working with vendors like Akamai or Limelight to optimize their CDN configurations and reduce the cost per GB of delivered content.
  • Post-production and MAM teams: Will need to evaluate their workflow implications for ingest, QC, and archive operations, particularly in terms of managing the increased volume of high-quality, low-latency content. This may involve investing in new tools and technologies, such as those offered by Avid or Vizrt, to support more efficient and automated workflows.

Deep Insight: The Broader Industry Shift

The emergence of premium FAST channels is part of a broader industry shift towards IP-based signal transport and cloud-connected live production. This trend is driven by the need for greater flexibility, scalability, and cost-effectiveness, as well as the growing demand for high-quality, low-latency video delivery. As the industry continues to evolve, we expect to see a greater adoption of standards like ST 2110, AES67, and RIST, as well as the development of new technologies and protocols that can support the delivery of high-quality, low-latency video. The vendors who will struggle in this new landscape are those that fail to adapt to the changing needs of broadcasters and OTT platforms, while those who invest in IP-based signal transport and cloud-connected live production will be well-positioned for success. By 2025, we expect to see a significant increase in the adoption of cloud-connected live production workflows, with a corresponding decrease in the use of traditional broadcast infrastructure.
