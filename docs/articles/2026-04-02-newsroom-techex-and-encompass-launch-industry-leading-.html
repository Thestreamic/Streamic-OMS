Techex and Encompass Launch Industry-Leading Cloud-Based Master Control Service for Sports and Live Events

What Was Announced Techex and Encompass have launched a cloud-based master control service specifically designed for sports and live events, marking a signif...

The Streamic Editorial Team April 02, 2026 687 words · 3 min read Analysis

What Was Announced

Techex and Encompass have launched a cloud-based master control service specifically designed for sports and live events, marking a significant development in the broadcast industry's shift towards software-defined workflows and cloud-based operations. This new service aims to provide a more flexible and scalable solution for managing live event broadcasts, potentially reducing the complexity and costs associated with traditional master control operations. By leveraging cloud infrastructure, the service can handle the demands of live event broadcasting, including high-volume ingest, real-time monitoring, and distribution to various platforms.

The Engineering and Operational Significance

The introduction of this cloud-based master control service addresses a critical need in the broadcast industry for more agile and responsive live event broadcasting workflows. Traditional master control operations often rely on dedicated hardware and infrastructure, which can be inflexible and costly to scale or modify. By moving to a cloud-based model, broadcasters can take advantage of on-demand resource allocation, automated scaling, and reduced capital expenditures, ultimately leading to improved operational efficiency and reduced costs. Furthermore, this service can facilitate the integration of IP-based workflows, such as those based on SMPTE ST 2110, and enable the use of advanced metadata management and automated content publishing, streamlining the process of delivering live events to various platforms, including OTT and social media.

The service's ability to handle high-volume ingest, monitoring, and distribution also underscores the importance of reliable and low-latency transport protocols, such as SRT or Zixi, in ensuring the integrity and quality of live event broadcasts. Additionally, the use of cloud-based master control can facilitate the adoption of advanced technologies, such as artificial intelligence and machine learning, to enhance live event broadcasting, including automated highlight reels, personalized content recommendations, and real-time analytics.

Industry Context

The launch of this cloud-based master control service reflects the broader trend in the broadcast industry towards software-defined workflows, cloud-based operations, and IP-based infrastructure. As broadcasters continue to navigate the complexities of live event broadcasting, including the need for greater flexibility, scalability, and cost efficiency, cloud-based services are increasingly being adopted to support a wide range of applications, from ingest and playout to distribution and monetization. Comparable approaches, such as Amazon Web Services' (AWS) Elemental and Microsoft Azure's Media Services, have already gained significant traction in the market, highlighting the growing demand for cloud-based broadcast solutions. The use of cloud-based master control also aligns with the industry's shift towards more agile and responsive workflows, as seen in the adoption of microservices-based architectures and containerization technologies, such as Docker.

The industry's transition to IP-based workflows, driven in part by the adoption of standards such as SMPTE ST 2110, has also created new opportunities for cloud-based services to integrate with existing broadcast infrastructure, including SDI and AES67-based systems. As the broadcast industry continues to evolve, the importance of cloud-based services, such as master control, will only continue to grow, enabling broadcasters to respond more effectively to changing market conditions, audience behaviors, and technological advancements.

Implications for Broadcast and Media Teams

For engineering leaders and operations managers, the introduction of this cloud-based master control service presents a significant opportunity to reassess their live event broadcasting workflows and infrastructure, with a focus on simplifying operations, reducing costs, and improving responsiveness to changing market conditions. By evaluating the service's capabilities and limitations, broadcasters can determine whether it can help address specific pain points, such as scalability, flexibility, or reliability, and develop a more effective strategy for managing live event broadcasts. Additionally, the service's potential to facilitate the adoption of advanced technologies, such as AI and machine learning, can help broadcasters stay competitive in a rapidly evolving market.

As broadcasters consider the adoption of cloud-based master control, they should carefully evaluate the service's interoperability with existing infrastructure, including MAM and PAM systems, as well as its ability to integrate with other cloud-based services, such as CDN and ABR. By taking a holistic approach to evaluating the service, broadcasters can ensure that it aligns with their overall technology strategy and business objectives, ultimately leading to improved operational efficiency, reduced costs, and enhanced live event broadcasting capabilities.
