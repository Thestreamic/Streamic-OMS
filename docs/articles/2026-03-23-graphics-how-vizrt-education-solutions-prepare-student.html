How Vizrt Education Solutions Prepare Students for Real-World Media Careers

The Streamic Editorial Team March 23, 2026 560 words · 3 min read

Technical Analysis: Vizrt's JPEG XS Implementation Reduces Latency in Live Graphics Transport

Vizrt, a leading provider of real-time graphics and video solutions, has announced the integration of JPEG XS into their live graphics transport workflow. This development is significant for broadcast CTOs and infrastructure engineers, as it enables the transport of high-quality video signals over IP networks with reduced latency. The technology is based on the JPEG XS standard, which is designed for low-latency, low-complexity video compression.

Operational ROI

The implementation of JPEG XS in Vizrt's live graphics transport workflow can save broadcast operators significant amounts of money by reducing transmission costs. With JPEG XS, operators can transport high-quality video signals over existing IP networks, eliminating the need for dedicated fiber or satellite links. This can result in cost savings of up to 50% compared to traditional transmission methods. The reduced latency of JPEG XS also simplifies signal routing, as it enables the use of standard IP switches and routers, reducing the need for specialized video routing equipment.

In comparison to existing approaches, such as using uncompressed video over IP, JPEG XS offers a more efficient and cost-effective solution. Uncompressed video requires significant bandwidth, which can be costly and difficult to manage, especially in large-scale live productions. JPEG XS, on the other hand, provides a compressed video signal that can be transported over standard IP networks, reducing the bandwidth requirements and making it easier to manage and route signals.

Security & Integration

Vizrt's implementation of JPEG XS is designed to integrate seamlessly with existing broadcast IT stacks, including those using ST 2110, NMOS, and AES67. The use of JPEG XS compression ensures that the video signal is secure and compliant with relevant standards and regulations. The integration with NMOS, in particular, enables the use of standardized APIs for device discovery and registration, making it easier to manage and monitor the live graphics transport workflow. Additionally, the use of AES67 audio over IP protocol ensures that the audio signal is synchronized with the video signal, providing a complete and integrated solution for live graphics transport.

Engineering Takeaways

  • Deployment: To deploy Vizrt's JPEG XS solution, engineers will need to ensure that their IP network infrastructure can support the required bandwidth and latency requirements. This may involve upgrading network switches and routers to support 10GbE or 25GbE connectivity.
  • Compatibility: The solution is compliant with SMPTE ST 2110-22, which defines the transport of compressed video over IP networks. It is also compatible with NMOS IS-04, which provides a standardized API for device discovery and registration.
  • What to Watch: Engineers should track the development of new standards and protocols, such as the upcoming SMPTE ST 2110-23, which will define the transport of compressed audio over IP networks. This will enable the use of JPEG XS for both video and audio compression, providing a complete and integrated solution for live graphics transport.

The implementation of JPEG XS in Vizrt's live graphics transport workflow signals a significant shift towards more efficient and cost-effective live production workflows. Engineering teams should begin evaluating the use of JPEG XS in their own workflows, focusing on the potential cost savings and operational efficiencies that can be achieved through the use of this technology.

Analysis: Vizrt's use of ST 2110 and NMOS protocols enables students to learn IP-based broadcast workflows, preparing them for professional environments with standardized, low-latency video transport.
