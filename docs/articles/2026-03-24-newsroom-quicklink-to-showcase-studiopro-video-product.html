QuickLink to Showcase StudioPro Video Production Ecosystem at NAB 2026

QuickLink to Showcase StudioPro Video Production Ecosystem at NAB 2026

The Streamic Editorial Team March 24, 2026 440 words · 2 min read

Why Real-Time Transport Innovations Are Forcing Broadcasters to Rethink Signal Integrity

Broadcast engineers are under pressure to maintain frame-accurate, low-latency signal transport as live production workflows become increasingly complex.

The shift towards IP-based infrastructures, driven by standards like ST 2110 and AES67, has introduced new challenges in ensuring signal integrity and synchronisation, particularly in hybrid environments where traditional SDI signals meet IP streams. As a result, broadcasters must carefully evaluate their signal transport architectures to mitigate potential issues with Genlock, PTP, and signal routing. For instance, the use of 12G-SDI for high-bandwidth signal transport can introduce complexities in signal distribution and switching, which can be alleviated by adopting IP-based signal transport protocols like SRT or RIST.

What This Development Actually Means

QuickLink's StudioPro ecosystem is designed to streamline live video production by integrating real-time signal transport, processing, and distribution, leveraging IP protocols like SRT and NDI to facilitate low-latency, frame-accurate signal transport. This development represents a significant step forward in live production, as it enables broadcasters to maintain high signal quality while reducing the complexity and cost associated with traditional signal transport methods. By adopting JPEG XS compression, for example, broadcasters can reduce the bandwidth requirements for high-resolution video signals, making it easier to transport them over IP networks.

The industry context for this development is the growing demand for high-quality, low-latency live video production, driven by the increasing popularity of live events, sports, and news programming. As broadcasters strive to deliver immersive, engaging experiences to their audiences, they must adopt innovative signal transport solutions that can meet the stringent requirements of live production, including ultra-low latency, high-bandwidth signal transport, and precise synchronisation. Vendors like Matrox, Riedel, and Evertz are already responding to this demand with their own IP-based signal transport solutions, such as Matrox's ConvertIP gateway and Riedel's MediorNet network.

Why It Matters for Broadcast Operations

The implications of this development are far-reaching, as broadcasters will need to reassess their signal transport architectures to ensure they can maintain the required level of signal integrity and low latency. This will involve evaluating the trade-offs between different signal transport protocols, such as SRT, RIST, and ST 2110, and considering the integration complexity and workflow disruption associated with adopting new technologies. For example, broadcasters may need to invest in new equipment, such as IP gateways or signal processors, to facilitate the transition to IP-based signal transport. Vendors like Harmonic, with their VOS360 platform, and Telestream, with their Vantage system, are well-positioned to support this transition, offering a range of solutions for IP-based signal processing and distribution.
