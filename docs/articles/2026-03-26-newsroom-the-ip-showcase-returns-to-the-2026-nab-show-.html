The IP Showcase Returns to the 2026 NAB Show as IPMX Transitions From Specification to Deployment

The IP Showcase Returns to the 2026 NAB Show as IPMX Transitions From Specification to Deployment

The Streamic Editorial Team March 26, 2026

Why IPMX Is Forcing Broadcasters to Rethink Live Signal Transport

As the broadcast industry continues to shift towards IP-based infrastructures, the need for standardized, low-latency signal transport has become a pressing concern. With the IP Showcase returning to the 2026 NAB Show, it's clear that IPMX is transitioning from specification to deployment, and broadcasters must take notice. The pressure to adopt IPMX is mounting, driven by the demand for more efficient, flexible, and scalable live production workflows.

What This Development Actually Means

The IPMX specification, which builds upon the ST 2110 standard, aims to provide a comprehensive framework for IP-based live production, including signal transport, timing, and device control. With IPMX, broadcasters can expect to achieve lower latency, improved signal integrity, and enhanced frame-accuracy, all of which are critical for high-quality live productions. The transition from specification to deployment marks a significant milestone, as vendors such as Matrox, Riedel, and Sony begin to integrate IPMX into their products, including IP gateways like Matrox ConvertIP and Riedel MediorNet.

This development sits within the broader context of the broadcast industry's shift towards IP-based infrastructures, driven by the need for greater flexibility, scalability, and cost-effectiveness. As broadcasters increasingly adopt IP-based workflows, the demand for standardized, low-latency signal transport has become a major priority, with many turning to solutions like SRT, RIST, and AES67 to meet their needs.

Why It Matters for Broadcast Operations

The adoption of IPMX will have significant implications for broadcast engineers, who will need to reassess their infrastructure decisions over the next 12-18 months. With IPMX, broadcasters can expect to achieve significant CapEx and OpEx savings, as well as reduced integration complexity, by leveraging IP-based signal transport and device control. However, this will also require careful consideration of workflow disruption, competitive dynamics, and the potential need for new training and support. Vendors like Pebble Beach Systems, Evertz Mediator-X, and Harmonic VOS360 will play a crucial role in supporting this transition, as they develop IPMX-compliant products and solutions that meet the evolving needs of broadcasters.
