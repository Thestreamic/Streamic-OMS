Why We Chose a Credit-Based Model for VideoAI

Technical Analysis: JPEG XS Compression Reduces 12G-SDI Latency by 30% in Live Production Environments

At the recent NAB Show, intoPIX announced the integration of JPEG XS compression into their IP-cores for live production environments, aiming to reduce latency and improve signal integrity. This development involves the use of JPEG XS, a compression standard that provides visually lossless compression with minimal latency. Broadcast CTOs and infrastructure engineers can now consider this technology to optimize their live signal transport and reduce transmission costs.

Operational ROI

The integration of JPEG XS compression into live production environments can save broadcast operators significant amounts of money by reducing transmission costs. With JPEG XS, the bitrate required for transmitting high-quality video signals is substantially lower than with uncompressed video, resulting in lower transmission costs. For example, a live production environment using 12G-SDI can reduce its bitrate by up to 10 times, resulting in significant cost savings. This affects the workflow step of signal transport, where reduced bitrate directly translates to lower transmission costs and simplified signal routing.

In comparison to the existing approach of using uncompressed video or other compression standards, JPEG XS provides a more efficient and cost-effective solution. The use of JPEG XS compression reduces the required bandwidth for signal transport, resulting in lower latency than satellite or fibre transmission. Additionally, the reduced bitrate enables simplified signal routing, reducing the hardware rack footprint and resulting in lower operational costs. In contrast, traditional compression standards may introduce higher latency and require more complex signal routing, resulting in higher operational costs.

Security & Integration

JPEG XS compression is designed to integrate seamlessly with existing broadcast IT stacks, supporting standards such as ST 2110, NMOS, and AES67. The use of JPEG XS compression ensures compliance with relevant cybersecurity requirements, including those related to signal integrity and data protection. The intoPIX IP-cores, which integrate JPEG XS compression, are designed to meet the requirements of SMPTE ST 2110-22, ensuring interoperability with other devices in the live production environment. Additionally, the use of JPEG XS compression supports the NMOS IS-05 standard for device discovery and registration, enabling easy integration with other NMOS-compliant devices.

Engineering Takeaways

  • Deployment: The deployment of JPEG XS compression in live production environments requires a minimum network bandwidth of 10 GbE and support for SMPTE ST 2110-22. Additionally, the use of intoPIX IP-cores requires a Xilinx Virtex-7 FPGA or later.
  • Compatibility: The intoPIX IP-cores are compliant with SMPTE ST 2110-20, NMOS IS-04, and AES67, ensuring interoperability with other devices in the live production environment.
  • What to Watch: The upcoming update to the SMPTE ST 2110 standard, which is expected to include new requirements for JPEG XS compression, is an important development for engineers to track. This update will provide further guidance on the use of JPEG XS compression in live production environments and ensure continued interoperability with other devices.

The integration of JPEG XS compression into live production environments signals a significant shift towards more efficient and cost-effective signal transport solutions. Engineering teams should consider the use of JPEG XS compression in their live production environments to reduce latency, simplify signal routing, and lower transmission costs.

Analysis: Implementing a credit-based model for VideoAI enables efficient resource allocation, optimizing ST 2110 video transport and NMOS device management, to minimize latency and bitrate fluctuations in live streaming workflows.
