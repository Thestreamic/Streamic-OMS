On Air 2025: A Global Broadcast Powered by Students and Vizrt

Technical Analysis: Vizrt's IP-Based Live Graphics Solution Reduces Latency and Increases Signal Integrity

Vizrt, a leading provider of graphics and live production solutions, recently announced its participation in the On Air 2025 project, a global broadcast event powered by students and Vizrt's IP-based live graphics technology. This project utilizes Vizrt's software-defined graphics solution, which is built on the SMPTE ST 2110 standard for professional media over managed IP networks. The event demonstrated the capabilities of Vizrt's solution in a live production environment, showcasing its ability to reduce latency and increase signal integrity.

Operational ROI

The use of Vizrt's IP-based live graphics solution can significantly reduce transmission costs and latency compared to traditional satellite or fiber-based transmission methods. By utilizing the SMPTE ST 2110 standard, Vizrt's solution can achieve latency as low as 1-2 milliseconds, which is significantly lower than the 100-200 milliseconds typically seen in satellite-based transmission. This reduction in latency can have a significant impact on the workflow step of live graphics insertion, allowing for more accurate and timely graphics rendering. Additionally, the use of IP-based transmission can simplify signal routing and reduce the hardware rack footprint, resulting in lower equipment and maintenance costs.

In comparison to the existing approach of using traditional SDI-based graphics solutions, Vizrt's IP-based solution offers a more flexible and scalable architecture. The use of IP-based transmission allows for easier integration with other IP-based devices and systems, such as video switchers and audio consoles, and can simplify the process of adding or removing devices from the system. This can result in a more efficient and cost-effective workflow, with reduced labor costs and increased productivity.

Security & Integration

Vizrt's IP-based live graphics solution is designed to integrate seamlessly with existing broadcast IT stacks, utilizing industry-standard protocols such as NMOS and AES67. The solution is also compliant with relevant security standards, including those related to access control and data encryption. The use of SMPTE ST 2110 and other industry-standard protocols ensures that Vizrt's solution can be easily integrated with other devices and systems, such as those from other manufacturers, and can be used in a variety of different applications, including live production, news, and sports.

Engineering Takeaways

  • Deployment: Vizrt's IP-based live graphics solution requires a minimum of 10GbE network bandwidth and a compatible switcher or router, such as those from Cisco or Arista. Additionally, the solution requires a software-dependent license and certification from Vizrt.
  • Compatibility: Vizrt's solution is compliant with SMPTE ST 2110-20 and NMOS IS-04, ensuring seamless integration with other IP-based devices and systems. The solution is also compatible with S3-compatible APIs, allowing for easy integration with cloud-based storage and other applications.
  • What to Watch: The upcoming update to the SMPTE ST 2110 standard, which is expected to include new features and enhancements for professional media over managed IP networks, is an important development for engineers to track. This update is expected to provide even greater flexibility and scalability for IP-based live graphics solutions, and will likely have a significant impact on the industry.

The use of Vizrt's IP-based live graphics solution signals a significant shift towards IP-based transmission in the broadcast industry, with a focus on reducing latency and increasing signal integrity. Engineering teams should take note of the benefits and requirements of this technology, and begin planning for its integration into their existing workflows and systems.

Analysis: On Air 2025 utilizes ST 2110 for IP-based video transport, enabling flexible, scalable broadcast workflows with reduced latency and optimized bitrate allocation, supporting NMOS protocol for device discovery and registration.
