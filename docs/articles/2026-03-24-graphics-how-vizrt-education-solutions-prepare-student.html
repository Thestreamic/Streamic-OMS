Technical Analysis: Vizrt's JPEG XS Implementation Reduces Latency in Live Graphics Production

Vizrt, a leading provider of real-time graphics and video production tools, has announced the integration of JPEG XS into their live graphics production workflow. This development is significant, as it enables broadcast engineers to transport high-quality video signals over IP networks with reduced latency. The implementation of JPEG XS is expected to have a major impact on live graphics production, particularly in applications where low-latency and high-quality video are critical.

Operational ROI

The integration of JPEG XS into Vizrt's live graphics production workflow is expected to save broadcast engineers significant amounts of time and money. By reducing the bitrate required for high-quality video transport, JPEG XS enables the use of lower-bandwidth networks, resulting in reduced transmission costs. Additionally, the lower latency provided by JPEG XS simplifies signal routing and reduces the need for complex signal processing equipment, resulting in a reduced hardware rack footprint. This affects the workflow step of signal transport, where engineers can now use existing network infrastructure to transport high-quality video signals.

In comparison to existing approaches, such as using uncompressed SDI or MPEG-4, JPEG XS provides a more efficient and cost-effective solution for live graphics production. The use of JPEG XS eliminates the need for expensive and complex signal processing equipment, such as encoders and decoders, and reduces the amount of bandwidth required for signal transport. This results in a more streamlined and efficient workflow, with reduced operational costs and improved signal quality.

Security & Integration

The integration of JPEG XS into Vizrt's live graphics production workflow is designed to meet the security and compliance requirements of the broadcast industry. JPEG XS is a standardized technology, compliant with the SMPTE ST 2110-22 standard, which ensures interoperability with other devices and systems that support this standard. Additionally, Vizrt's implementation of JPEG XS is designed to work seamlessly with other broadcast standards, such as AES67 and NMOS, to provide a secure and integrated solution for live graphics production. The use of JPEG XS also provides a secure and reliable method for transporting sensitive video signals, such as those used in live sports and news production.

Engineering Takeaways

  • Deployment: The deployment of JPEG XS in live graphics production requires a network infrastructure that supports the SMPTE ST 2110 standard, with a minimum bandwidth of 1 Gb/s and a maximum latency of 1 ms. Additionally, engineers will need to ensure that their equipment is compatible with the JPEG XS standard and that they have the necessary software and firmware updates to support this technology.
  • Compatibility: Vizrt's implementation of JPEG XS is compliant with the SMPTE ST 2110-22 standard and is designed to work seamlessly with other devices and systems that support this standard. Engineers should ensure that their equipment is compatible with this standard to ensure interoperability and seamless integration.
  • What to Watch: Engineers should track the development of the SMPTE ST 2110 standard, particularly the upcoming updates to the standard that will provide additional features and functionality for JPEG XS. Additionally, engineers should monitor the adoption of JPEG XS by other manufacturers and the development of new products and solutions that support this technology.

The integration of JPEG XS into Vizrt's live graphics production workflow signals a significant shift in the broadcast industry towards the use of more efficient and cost-effective technologies for live signal transport. Engineering teams should begin to evaluate the use of JPEG XS in their own workflows, particularly in applications where low-latency and high-quality video are critical, to take advantage of the operational and cost benefits provided by this technology.
