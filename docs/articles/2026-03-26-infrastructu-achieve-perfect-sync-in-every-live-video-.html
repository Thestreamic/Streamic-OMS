Achieve Perfect Sync in Every Live Video Production

The Streamic Editorial Team March 26, 2026 475 words · 2 min read

Why Perfect Sync in Live Video Production Demands a Rethink of Traditional Signal Transport Architectures

Live video production has always been a high-pressure environment, where every second counts and signal integrity is paramount. As the industry continues to evolve, the need for perfect sync in every live video production has become a critical requirement. With the increasing adoption of IP-based workflows and the rise of remote production, the traditional signal transport architectures are being put to the test.

What This Development Actually Means

The development of new technologies and standards, such as ST 2110, NDI, and SRT, has enabled the creation of more efficient and flexible signal transport architectures. For instance, the use of ST 2110 allows for the separation of video, audio, and metadata, enabling more precise control over signal routing and processing. This, in turn, has led to the creation of more complex and distributed production environments, where signals need to be synchronized across multiple locations and devices. Companies like Haivision, Matrox, and Riedel are at the forefront of this development, providing solutions that cater to the growing demand for perfect sync in live video production.

The industry context for this development is the increasing demand for high-quality, low-latency live video production, driven by the growth of online streaming and social media platforms. The need for perfect sync is no longer just a technical requirement, but a critical factor in delivering a high-quality viewer experience. As a result, broadcast engineers are under pressure to design and deploy signal transport architectures that can meet these demands, while also reducing costs and complexity.

Why It Matters for Broadcast Operations

The ability to achieve perfect sync in live video production has significant implications for broadcast operations. For example, the use of Genlock and 12G-SDI can ensure that signals are synchronized across multiple devices, while the adoption of JPEG XS can reduce the bandwidth required for signal transport. However, the integration of these technologies can be complex, and broadcast engineers need to carefully evaluate the trade-offs between different solutions. Vendors like Pebble Beach Systems, Evertz Mediator-X, and Harmonic VOS360 are providing solutions that cater to the growing demand for perfect sync, but broadcast engineers need to assess the specific requirements of their operations and choose the solutions that best fit their needs. The use of AES67 and RIST can also provide a more efficient and reliable way of transporting audio signals, while NMOS can enable the discovery and registration of devices on the network.

Impact by Stakeholder

  • Regional and national broadcasters: Will need to budget for the deployment of new signal transport architectures, including the adoption of ST 2110, NDI, and SRT. This will enable them to reduce transmission costs, lower latency, and simplify signal routing

    Analysis: I'm skeptical about "perfect sync" claims, especially with HLS and MPEG-DASH latency discrepancies - CTOs should scrutinize SSAI implementations.
