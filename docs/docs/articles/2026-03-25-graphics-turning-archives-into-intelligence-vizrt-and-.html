Turning archives into intelligence: Vizrt and DeepVA introduce AI-driven metadata enrichment in Viz One media asset management

The Streamic Editorial Team March 25, 2026 238 words · 1 min read

The integration of AI-driven metadata enrichment in MAM systems represents a significant technological advancement, with far-reaching implications for broadcast infrastructure decisions over the next 12–18 months. By leveraging machine learning algorithms to automate the metadata enrichment process, broadcasters and streaming platforms can reduce the time and cost associated with manual metadata entry, while also improving the accuracy and consistency of their metadata. This, in turn, can enable more efficient content discovery, improved personalized recommendations, and enhanced content monetization strategies. However, the successful implementation of AI-driven metadata enrichment will require careful consideration of the underlying cloud infrastructure, including the use of object storage, microservices, and Kubernetes. Furthermore, the potential for egress costs to escalate as a result of increased metadata processing and storage must be carefully managed through optimized API orchestration and data transfer protocols.

In practice, this means that broadcasters and streaming platforms must carefully evaluate their existing MAM architectures and workflows, with a view to integrating AI-driven metadata enrichment in a way that minimizes disruption and maximizes benefits. For engineering teams, the real challenge is to balance the potential benefits of AI-driven metadata enrichment against the potential risks and complexities associated with implementing and maintaining these systems. The trade-off here is between the potential for improved content discovery and monetization, and the potential for increased egress costs

Analysis: I'm wary of AI-driven metadata enrichment in MAM systems like Viz One, as it may introduce hidden DRM complexities.
