Studio-Grade Video Workflow in 2026

From camera ingest and automated proxy creation to collaborative editorial, audio conform, color finishing, mastering, compliance checks, and final OTT delivery, this is what a modern high-end post-production workflow actually looks like when built for scale, speed, and reliability.

The Streamic Editorial Updated for 2026 Broadcast & OTT Post-Production

The most effective OTT post-production pipelines in 2026 are not built around a single application. They are built around preserved metadata, shared storage discipline, collaborative locking, reliable relinking, and deliverable integrity. That is what separates a clean internal workflow from one that can actually support premium longform, episodic, promo, or platform delivery at scale.

1. Ingest and Automated Proxy Creation

The process begins at the source: digital camera cards carrying formats such as ARRI RAW, REDCODE, or Sony X-OCN. At this stage, the priority is not speed alone. It is speed with verification.

In a proper facility workflow, media is securely offloaded using tools such as OffShoot, with MD5 checksum verification confirming that the copied media matches the original data written on the card. That verification step matters because every later decision, from relink to conform to archive integrity, depends on the assumption that ingest was clean.

Once media lands, an automated transcode layer can monitor the ingest destination. Systems such as Telestream Vantage or MOG mediaMOVE can generate editorial proxies in formats like Avid DNxHR LB or ProRes Proxy, while keeping essential metadata such as timecode and reel identifiers aligned with source media.

Those proxies, along with the associated project structure, are then moved into Avid NEXIS F-Series shared storage, giving editorial teams a central, controlled storage layer rather than fragmented local drives.

2. Collaborative Editorial and the Locking Layer

Shared storage alone does not create a collaborative workflow. What matters is how editorial applications manage access, project ownership, and conflict prevention.

Avid Media Composer

In Avid Media Composer, collaborative editing on NEXIS is mature and dependable because bin locking is native. Editors can work in parallel without overwriting each other’s material, and the locking model remains one of the reasons Avid still holds value in larger multi-editor environments.

Adobe Premiere Pro

For Adobe Premiere Pro, the modern equivalent is the Productions workflow. Instead of one giant project file, teams work inside a Production structure, where separate projects behave like controlled containers. When one editor opens a project, a lock file is created, and another editor can still open it in read-only mode without causing collisions.

In 2025 and 2026 style workflows, facilities also increasingly use Avid NEXIS | Remote Panel for Premiere Pro to browse, pin, and work more naturally with media from the NEXIS environment.

Why this matters Collaboration is not just about two editors touching the same show. It is about making sure assistants, promo teams, finishing editors, and post supervisors can all move quickly without project corruption, duplicate media confusion, or accidental overwrite risk.

3. Picture Lock

Once the edit is approved, the workflow reaches picture lock. This is the handoff point where creative decisions stop changing and technical finishing can proceed with confidence.

At this stage, the project is usually exported as AAF or XML, depending on the downstream finishing path. The real strength of the earlier ingest and proxy discipline now becomes visible: the metadata captured and preserved during editorial is what allows the next stages to reconnect to the original, high-resolution media correctly.

4. Audio Conform and Mix

This is the stage that is often simplified in diagrams but should never be ignored in a real studio-grade workflow. After picture lock, the soundtrack moves into an audio post environment such as Pro Tools or an equivalent finishing platform for audio conform, cleanup, design, and final mix.

Dialogue, FX, ambience, and music are reassembled against the locked edit. From there, the team can perform dialogue restoration, sound design, stem creation, and final deliverables in stereo, 5.1, 7.1, or Dolby Atmos, depending on the platform requirement.

In practical high-end workflows, audio conform and color finishing often progress in parallel after picture lock, before they come back together for final mastering.

5. Conform and Color Finishing

With picture lock approved, the sequence is relinked inside a finishing system such as DaVinci Resolve or Baselight. The goal here is not to keep working on proxies. It is to pull the original camera files or high-resolution source from the proper storage tier based on the metadata carried forward from ingest and editorial.

This stage includes online conform, shot validation, version checking, and final image treatment. For premium OTT projects, the grading pass may include Dolby Vision HDR, along with the creation of supporting trim or mapping metadata required for downstream SDR versions.

6. Mastering

Once image and sound are approved, the workflow moves into mastering. The actual output depends on the target destination. For premium OTT platforms, that often means an IMF package. For other broadcaster or regional delivery requirements, the output may be a high-bitrate MXF OP1a or another approved mezzanine format.

This stage is where editorial, audio, subtitles, timed text, metadata, and version control all stop being separate disciplines and become one controlled delivery object.

7. Playout and QC

Before delivery, a high-end pipeline should still validate playback and compliance rather than assuming an export is automatically safe. Files may be checked through playout-oriented environments such as Harmonic Spectrum or similar server-based verification paths to confirm that the master behaves correctly under real playback conditions.

Automated QC systems such as Interra Baton or Telestream Vidchecker are then used for compliance checks including loudness, black levels, cadence issues, and other delivery risks. In premium environments, this is where expensive failures are prevented before the file leaves the facility.

8. OTT Delivery

The last step is delivery, but by this point the real work has already been done. A trustworthy OTT delivery workflow is less about the final upload button and more about the chain of decisions that came before it: verified ingest, robust proxy generation, shared editorial discipline, proper locking, preserved metadata, correct relink, synchronized audio finishing, mastering integrity, and compliance validation.

When those layers are built well, delivery becomes a controlled conclusion rather than a stressful gamble.
